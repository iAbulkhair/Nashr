## 0. Actors and the functions that identify them

| Actor | How the code identifies it | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Anonymous | No check | none |
| Signed-in login | Cookie `nashr_session` to `session:{token}` (`{email, at, imp?}`) to the LOGIN it names: an owner record `user:{email}` or a member record `member:{email}` (the owner record wins should both exist). It always returns the OWNER's `User`, which is the account, with a runtime-only `actor` saying who is acting, plus `brandId` and `scope`. The refusals are in 0.1. | `currentUser()` and `resolveSession` in `src/lib/auth.ts` |
| Account owner | The login whose `user:{email}` record is the account: actor `{kind:"owner", role:"owner"}`. Created by signup. The only role that can pay, delete the account, write governance, mint the MCP token, approve OAuth apps or manage admins (0.1). | `currentUser()` |
| Member of an account (role `admin` or `member`) | A login at `member:{email}` whose `memberOf` names the owner, created only by accepting an invite. Resolves to the OWNER's account: every read and write uses the owner's workspace. Actor `{kind:"member", email: the member's own address, role}`. Never in `users:all`, never given credits, a referral code or signup tracking. | `currentUser()`; records in `src/lib/team.ts` |
| Account of the resource | Every workspace reader and writer takes the caller's scope `sc(u)` (`src/lib/auth.ts`), built from the OWNER email and the acting login's active brand, so ids from the request only resolve inside the account's own workspace. The owner and the account's members share that workspace; nobody outside the account can be given access to it. | `sc(u)` plus the scoped getters in `src/lib/brand.ts`, `files.ts`, `conversations.ts`, `connectors/store.ts` |
| Founder impersonating an account | A session written by the console's `impersonate` action carries `imp` (the founder's email, or `"key"` for the console key): actor `{kind:"founder", role:"owner", impersonating}`. Refused every action in `IMPERSONATION_BLOCKED` (0.1) and the founder console itself. | `currentUser()`, `can()` |
| Founder admin | `WAITLIST_ADMIN_KEY` in header `x-admin-key` (30 tries per IP per 15 min, bucket `adminhdr:{ip}`) or a key session opened by the key form (cookie `nashr_admin` = random id, record `adminsession:{sha256(id)}` holding a fingerprint of the key, so rotating the key ends it and logout deletes it; it ends after 15 minutes of inactivity and 12 h after the key entry at most), never in the URL; or a session whose actor is the owner login itself (`kind:"owner"`, actor email equal to the account email) with that email in `ADMIN_EMAILS`. A member of a founder's account and a founder impersonating never pass. | `adminActor(req)` and `isAdmin(req)` (APIs), `isAdminCookie()` (pages), `isFounderActor(u)`, all in `src/lib/auth.ts` |
| Cron | Header `Authorization: Bearer ${CRON_SECRET}`; denied when the variable is unset | inline in each cron route |
| MCP bearer | Header `Authorization: Bearer nashr_...` to `mcptoken:{token}` to `user:{email}`; refuses a `suspended` account and a token whose email is a member login (even should an owner record exist at the same address); sets `scope` from the owner's active brand like a session; actor `{kind:"token", role:"owner"}` | `userFromBearer(req)` `src/lib/mcp.ts` |

There is no global guard. `src/proxy.ts` does not authenticate (`src/proxy.ts:15-30`); every handler enforces its own rule, and a handler without a check is public.

### 0.1 Roles, members and seats (2026-09-29)

Files: `src/lib/roles.ts` (the table), `src/lib/team.ts` (member logins, invites, seats), `src/lib/auth.ts` (resolution; it re-exports `PLAN_SEATS` and `seatLimit`), `src/lib/pricing.ts` (the plan table: `PLAN_SEATS`, `seatLimit`, since 2026-09-30). Design: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-29-teams-design.md`.

1. Three roles. `owner` belongs to the `user:` record only and can never be assigned, removed or transferred. `admin` and `member` belong to member logins (`Member.role`): chosen at invite, changed only by the owner.
2. One table, `MIN_ROLE` in `src/lib/roles.ts`, names the minimum role of each action. `can(u, action)` answers; `forbid(u, action)` returns a 403 `{error:"forbidden"}` or `null`. Routes call it right after `currentUser()`; the chat tools (`TOOL_ACTION` in `src/lib/chat.ts`), MCP dispatch (`MCP_TOOL_ACTION` in `src/lib/mcp.ts`) and the pages use the same function. A control hidden on a page is display only; the route refuses regardless.
3. `can` fails closed. It answers false without a user or an actor, for an unknown action (an own-property test, so `__proto__` is unknown), for a role that is not one of the three, and when kind and role disagree (a member holding `owner`; an owner, token or founder not holding `owner`). `team.leave` is true only for kind `member`. Otherwise the actor's rank (member 1, admin 2, owner 3) must reach `MIN_ROLE[action]`. Users built by the cron (`allUsers()`) carry no actor, so a `can()` check on a cron path refuses.

|---|---|---|
| owner | `billing`, `account.delete`, `governance.write`, `mcp.token`, `oauth.approve`, `oauth.grants`, `referral`, `partner`, `partner.bank` (2026-10-01: the partner page and the partner's bank details; `partner.bank` is also impersonation-blocked), `profile.account`, `team.invite.admin`, `team.role`, `team.remove.admin` | Pay links and activation; the chat's "continue on credits" switch (row 114, since 2026-10-01); the billing page's top-up request buttons (since 2026-10-01: the message carries the account email); account deletion; governance writes; the personal MCP token (status, mint, revoke); OAuth consent and connected apps; referral data; the account's phone, language and email preferences; inviting or promoting an admin; any role change; removing an admin |

4. Founder impersonation. `IMPERSONATION_BLOCKED` is `account.delete`, `password.change`, `team.invite.member`, `team.invite.admin`, `team.invite.cancel`, `team.role`, `team.remove.member`, `team.remove.admin`, `mcp.token`, `oauth.approve` and, since 2026-10-01, `partner.bank` (where a partner's commission is paid). Actor kind `founder` is refused all of them whatever its role, so nothing that outlives the two-hour session can be created. Everything else the owner may do, a founder impersonating may do.
5. Member refusals (`resolveSession`, on every request). `currentUser()` returns `null` for a member session when the member record is missing (removed, or left), is `disabled` (seat-paused), holds a role other than `admin` or `member`, or has an empty `memberOf` or one equal to its own address; when the session carries `imp`; when the session predates `member.sessionsAfter` (the member's own password change or reset; set to `acceptedAt` on creation); when the owner record is missing or suspended; when the member is not on the owner's roster (`members:{owner}`, `onRoster`); when the session predates `owner.teamSessionsAfter` (owner reset, founder reset); or when the member was accepted before the owner record was created, so a re-registered owner address inherits nothing. Membership is never cached in the session: a removal, a role change or a seat pause applies at the member's next request. Owner sessions keep their checks (`suspended`, `sessionsAfter`), and `createUser` now stamps `sessionsAfter` at creation.
6. Scope. Always `scopeFor(owner email, brandId)`. `brandId` comes from `activebrand:{login}`, the acting login's own key; a member's value must be one of the owner's active brands, else `default`. A member's address never becomes part of a workspace key, and `tests/scope-usage.test.ts` fails when a page or customer route passes an actor, member, owner or login email to a workspace reader.
7. Shared by everyone in the account: the credits and the plan (`charge(u.email, ...)`), the per-agent caps and counters, the chat ceiling (`chat:h:{owner}`, `chat:d:{owner}`), the monthly chat allowance (since 2026-10-01: one counter per account, `usage:{owner}:{YYYY-MM}:chat_msgs`, or `chatm:{owner}:trial` on a trial, with `:chat_over` and `:chat_cred` for the messages continued on credits and what they were billed, counting the owner's, every member's, every brand's and every connected app's messages alike; the owner alone decides what happens when it runs out, `billing`, row 114), the notification bell and its read state (`notifs:{owner}`), model usage, and product analytics (a member's login is tracked on the owner email). Recorded since step 3 of the agent-team upgrade: decision records, governance revisions and activity rows carry `by`, the acting login (section 4 item 10), so which login approved, edited, rejected, published or changed governance is kept there. Not recorded: which login chatted; tasks themselves and chat history carry no actor. Support tickets and integration requests record both the acting login (`user` / `email`) and the account (`account`).
8. Seats. `PLAN_SEATS` (`src/lib/pricing.ts`, since 2026-09-30): trial 3, starter 1, pro 3, business 7, the owner included. Agency has no fixed number: `seatLimit` gives it Business's 7 unless the founder sets `User.seats` for its contract (a deal's seats live there, not in the deal). The retired `enterprise` (25) reads as agency. `User.seats`, set in the founder console, overrides every plan when it is a positive number (`seatLimit` floors it; the console accepts whole numbers 1 to 500). Seats used = 1 + members not disabled + unexpired invites, counted under `lock:team:{owner}` at invite and again at accept. `enforceSeats` runs after every plan or seat change in `POST /api/admin/plan`, today the only code that changes either.
9. The founder console follows the actor, not the account: `isFounderActor(u)` is true only for the owner login itself with its own email in `ADMIN_EMAILS` (section 4).

## 3. Cross-tenant isolation

### 3.1 How a scope is derived
1. `currentUser()` resolves the session's login (section 0.1): an owner login loads its own record; a member login loads the owner's. In both cases `activeBrandId(login)` reads `activebrand:{login}` (default `"default"`), a member's value must be one of the owner's active brands, and `scopeFor(ownerEmail, brandId)` returns the owner email for the default brand or `` `${ownerEmail}#${brandId}` `` otherwise (`src/lib/auth.ts`, `src/lib/brands.ts`). A member's own address is never a scope.
2. `sc(u) = u.scope ?? u.email` (`src/lib/auth.ts:101`). `scope` and `brandId` are runtime only and stripped by `saveUser` (`auth.ts:149`).
3. The active brand can only be set to an id found in the account's own `brands:{owner}` list (`brands/route.ts`), or to a brand just created by `addBrand`. Both move only the acting login's `activebrand:{login}`. Archiving the brand the caller has open resets the caller's key to `default`, and an admin archiving the brand the owner has open resets the owner's key too. `activeBrandId` does not re-validate the owner's own stored id; a member's is re-validated on every request.
4. Brand ids are 8 base36 characters from `Math.random()` (`brands.ts:69`).
5. Every brand-scoped key embeds the scope string (see `architecture.md` section 3). Isolation between tenants therefore depends on (a) the session to email mapping and (b) scope strings of different accounts never being equal.
6. Account-level (not brand-level) records: user, credits, sessions, notifications, MCP tokens, referral data, usage, analytics, and since 2026-10-01 the chat allowance's counters (`usage:{email}:{YYYY-MM}:chat_msgs`, `:chat_over` and `:chat_cred`, `chatm:{email}:trial`) and the owner's "continue on credits" choice (`User.chatOverflow`): one allowance for every brand and seat of the account. The per-agent monthly counters live on the account's user record but are keyed by brand scope (`agentSpend.by[scope]`), and the caps they are checked against are per brand (`gov:{scope}.agentCaps`), so one brand's spend and caps never apply to another. Chat thumbs are per brand scope since 2026-09-21 (`fblog:{scope}`), and so is the feedback built from them, the decision records and the judge's notes for an agent's prompts (`feedbackFor`, 2026-09-29). Media: a job record is per scope (`mjob:{sc(u)}:{id}`) but charges and refunds the account (`job.email`); the trial video marker (`mtrialvid:{email}`) and the render limits (`rate:media:*:{email}`) are per account; the render spend counters (`render:_all:*`) are global and carry no email. Since 2026-09-29 the account also holds its team: `members:{owner}`, `invites:{owner}`, the `member:{email}` records and each member's `activebrand:{member}`; all of them are keyed by the owner email or the member's own login, never used as a scope.
7. Cron and admin code construct scopes with the same `scopeFor` (`dailyRoster()` and `allUsers()` in `schedule.ts:237-258`, `admin/users/[email]/route.ts:37`, `account.ts:37`). The routines cron keys every read and write by the `scope` `allUsers()` set (`routinelast:{scope}:{id}`, `routinelock:{scope}:{id}`, `routinecap:{scope}`, `routinehold:{scope}:{id}`, `routinetry:{scope}:{id}:{period}`, `schedule:{scope}`, `gov:{scope}`, the tasks).
8. MCP callers resolve the active brand the same way a browser session does (`userFromBearer`, `src/lib/mcp.ts`), so a bearer token works in the brand the owner has active. Fixed 2026-09-20; it used to always mean the default brand. Tokens resolve to owner accounts only: a token naming a member login is refused.
9. Members (2026-09-29). A member session resolves to the owner's `User`, so `sc(u)`, `charge(u.email)`, plan checks and every scoped getter work unchanged and never see the member's address; only `u.actor` differs. The routes that act on a member or an invite take the email or invite id from the request but act only on a record whose `memberOf` or `owner` is the caller's account (3.2).

## 4. Admin model

1. `adminEmails()` (`src/lib/auth.ts:327-329`): `ADMIN_EMAILS` split on commas, normalized. Fails closed since 2026-10-01: unset or empty means no founder email at all (rule 425); there is no address in the source.
2. `isFounder(email)`: membership test. Used by `isFounderActor(u)` (sidebar link, `adminActor`, `isAdminCookie`) and as the recipient list for golden-set alerts (`golden.ts`, `adminEmails()`). `isFounderActor(u)` requires `u.actor.kind === "owner"` and `u.actor.email === u.email` besides the list: a member of the founder's own account (whose `u.email` is the founder's) and a founder impersonating never pass (2026-09-29).
3. `adminActor(req)` (`src/lib/auth.ts`) and `isAdmin(req)` (= `adminActor(req) !== null`), used by every `/api/admin/*` data and action route plus the admin branches of `/api/support`, `/api/affiliates`, `/api/integrations`: `"key"` when `WAITLIST_ADMIN_KEY` is set and the `x-admin-key` header equals it (`sameSecret`, throttled 30 per IP per 15 min), or when `adminKeySession()` finds a live key session for the `nashr_admin` id (the cookie holds a random id, never the key; item 5); that check also renews the key session, at most once a minute (rules 422, 435); otherwise the founder's email when `isFounderActor(currentUser())`, otherwise `null`. The key is never read from the URL.
4. `isAdminCookie()`, used by `admin/page.tsx`: a live key session (`adminKeySession()`) or `isFounderActor(currentUser())`.
5. `nashr_admin` cookie: set by `POST /api/admin/login` after a rate-limited `sameSecret` comparison; the value is a random 32-byte id, never the key; the cookie carries the 12-hour absolute cap; httpOnly; lax; Secure by transport (`sessionCookie`). The record `adminsession:{sha256(id)}` holds a fingerprint of the key it was opened with: logout deletes it, rotating `WAITLIST_ADMIN_KEY` invalidates every one, unsetting the key disables the path (rules 420-424, 2026-10-01). The session ends after 15 minutes without an admin request (`ADMIN_SESSION_IDLE`) and never outlives 12 hours from the key entry (`ADMIN_SESSION_MAX`): every admin request (API call or console page load) renews it, at most once a minute, with one `SET XX EX` whose TTL never runs past the cap; both limits are also checked in code against the record's `at` and `seen`. Requests made through a customer impersonation that the key session opened renew it the same way (`keyImpLive`: the founder working in the customer view is not idle), and a key impersonation ends with it (rules 422, 429, 435; founder decision 2026-10-01: 15 minutes of inactivity). A cookie that still holds the raw key opens nothing.
6. Founder accounts are ordinary users: they sign up through the public signup route. Whoever registers an address listed in `ADMIN_EMAILS` becomes a founder (there is no default address since 2026-10-01), because signup has no email verification (`auth/signup/route.ts`).
7. A suspended founder loses the session path (`currentUser` returns `null`) but the key path still works.
8. Impersonation (`admin/users/[email]/route.ts`): requires `adminActor`. Saves the caller's current `nashr_session` value in `nashr_return` (2 h), sets `nashr_impersonate` to the target email (2 h), then `createSession(target, 2 h, {imp: who})`: the session record carries the founder (or `"key"`) and resolves to actor kind `founder`, role `owner`. With the key, the record also carries `kp` (the key's fingerprint) and, from a key session, `as` (that key session's record hash), from `keyImpBinding`; `currentUser` refuses an `imp: "key"` session once the key is rotated or removed, once that key session is logged out or has ended (15 minutes without a request in the console or the customer view, 12 hours at most), or when `kp` is missing (rules 429, 435). The log entry carries `expiresInMin: 120` and, for one opened from a key session, `idleMin: 15, endsWithKeySession: true`. While impersonating, the founder console is closed through the session path (`isFounderActor` is false) and the `IMPERSONATION_BLOCKED` actions are refused; everything else acts as the owner.
9. Return (`admin/impersonate/route.ts`): no admin check; deletes the impersonation session record, restores `nashr_return` into `nashr_session` when that token is still a live session, clears the helper cookies, and logs `impersonate:stop` with `actor` = the `imp` the burned session carried and the `nashr_impersonate` value as the subject.
10. Audit: `admin:log` entries written by `POST /api/admin/users/{email}` and `POST /api/admin/plan` carry `actor` = the founder's email or `"key"` (`adminActor`), the target email, action, optional before/after (plan, credits, seats, the Agency deal as `credits@priceSar` or `none` since 2026-09-30, the account's chat messages, `chatAllowanceOf`, since 2026-10-01, the team members paused or restored) and reason. `POST /api/admin/golden` still writes the literal `"founder"`. Since 2026-09-29 decisions on items (`decisions:{scope}`), governance revisions (`govhist:{scope}`) and the activity log (`activity:{scope}`) also carry `by` from `byOf(u)` (`src/lib/actor.ts`), worked out from the session's actor (section 0.1), never from a request body: the owner as `{kind: "owner", email}`, a member as `{kind: "member", email: <the member's own login>, role}`, an MCP bearer as `{kind: "token", email: <account>}`, scheduled work as `{kind: "system", job}`, and a founder impersonating as `{kind: "founder", email: <account>, imp: <the founder's email or "key">}`, from the session record's `imp`, not as the owner. `imp` names the founder console's own login (or the key), so it is kept in storage and shown only to the founder console (row 80): what the account's own logins receive (GET /api/activity, the Activity page, the Governance page, and the `entry` of PUT /api/governance and POST /api/governance/rollback) goes through `publicBy` (`src/lib/actor.ts`), which drops it, so such a row reads "Thakeen support"; the CSV export writes only `by.kind` and `by.email`. No cookie marks an action as support's: `byOf` reads only the session's actor, and the impersonation cookies (`nashr_impersonate`, `nashr_return`, `nashr_admin`) are never looked at. A user with no actor (a cron, routine or script user, never a request) is recorded as `{kind: "system", job: "cron"}`, not as the owner. The task records themselves (their `published` entries included) and the brand file carry no actor. `setLang` is not logged.
11. `WAITLIST_ADMIN_KEY` has two further roles: it is one of the ways through `isAdmin`, which gates `GET /api/waitlist` (row 74), and outside production it is the fallback encryption secret for connector credentials when `CONNECT_SECRET` is unset (`src/lib/connectors/store.ts:23`); in production an unset `CONNECT_SECRET` throws (`:22`).

## 5. Unclear or missing rules

Each item states what the code does and why the intended rule cannot be read from it. None of these is guessed as intended or unintended.

1. **Email characters versus scope strings.** Signup accepts any address matching `^[^\s@]+@[^\s@]+\.[^\s@]+$` (`auth/signup/route.ts:13`), which allows `#` and `:`. Extra-brand scopes are `email#brandId` (`brands.ts:61`) and keys are `prefix:{scope}[:{id}]`. An account registered as `victim@x.com#<brandId>` has a default scope equal to the victim's brand scope, and an account registered as `victim@x.com:<convId>` has a default thread key equal to the victim's `chat:{scope}:{convId}`. Both require knowing a random id. No rule forbids these characters and nothing states that scope strings must be unique across accounts. Fixed 2026-09-18: `validEmail` (`auth.ts:119`) refuses `:` and `#`, at signup and for invites (`team.ts:203`), so no address can equal another account's brand scope or thread key.
2. **No email verification at signup.** Accounts become active immediately. Combined with `ADMIN_EMAILS` (no hard-coded default since 2026-10-01, rule 425), founder status is granted to whoever first registers a listed address. No rule says founder addresses must be pre-registered.
3. **Rate limits on the public routes.** Fixed for OAuth on 2026-09-29: `POST /api/oauth/register` allows 30 registrations per IP per hour and `POST /api/oauth/token` 60 requests per IP per 15 minutes (`OAUTH_*` in `src/lib/rate.ts`), counted on every attempt and answered 429 `{error:"slow_down"}` in OAuth's shape with the CORS headers. Hosted MCP clients (claude.ai, ChatGPT) call both from shared egress addresses for many accounts, so the ceilings are sized for that; if one of them is ever throttled, raise the constant rather than drop the limit. The token endpoint's secret check now uses `sameSecret` (was `!==`). Registered clients still never expire. Login and signup are throttled per IP and per email; support, affiliates and waitlist were given a per-IP limit on 2026-09-20; chat is bounded per account (`chatCaps(u)` in `src/lib/rate.ts`: 30 an hour and 150 a day on a paid plan, 10 an hour and 30 a day on a trial, since 2026-10-01) because a turn can run up to five model calls; since 2026-10-01 that is a burst limit in front of the plan's monthly chat allowance (`src/lib/chat-allowance.ts`, the section "Chat allowance" at the end), and each turn's chat model calls are capped at $0.35 in code (`src/lib/chat-budget.ts`). `clientIp` prefers the platform headers and falls back to the last `x-forwarded-for` hop (`rate.ts`).
4. **Password policy differs by route.** Reset requires 8 chars plus a letter and a digit (`auth/reset/route.ts:13`); signup and change-password require only 8 chars server-side (`auth/signup/route.ts:10`, `account/route.ts:12`); the signup form enforces the stricter rule client-side only. Update: signup, change and reset, and invite acceptance, all use `strongPassword` (`auth.ts:117`) server-side now. The line references above are to `4959f7e`.
5. **Sessions end when the password changes.** Fixed 2026-09-18. A session record is `{email, at}`; the user record carries `sessionsAfter`, set on password change, password reset and founder `resetPassword`; `currentUser` refuses any session issued before it, including the older bare-email records (treated as issued at 0). The device that changed the password gets a new session. Password writes now happen inside the user lock on the fresh record. Suspension is still enforced on read. Since 2026-09-29 a session record may also carry `imp` (impersonation), `createUser` stamps `sessionsAfter` at creation so a re-registered address never revives an old session, each member has its own `sessionsAfter` (its own password change or reset), and the owner record's `teamSessionsAfter` (owner reset, founder reset) signs every member out without touching the owner's sessions.
6. **Suspension.** Fixed: `userFromBearer` (`mcp.ts:47`) refuses a suspended account, so its MCP personal and OAuth tokens stop working, and `allUsers()` (`dailyRoster`, `schedule.ts:246`) skips suspended accounts, so no cron (weekly plan, routines, daily emails) charges or emails them; the daily cron only purges their brand scopes' expired to-dos (`purgeIdle`). The `User.suspended` comment says "login and all API access blocked" (`auth.ts:51`).

Every OAuth token pair belongs to a grant (`mcpgrant:{email}:{id}`, index `mcpgrants:{email}`). `GET /api/mcp/grants` lists connected apps without tokens, `DELETE ?id=` or `?all=1` revokes; the MCP page shows the list. Tokens issued before this change have no grant and cannot be listed; they expire on their own (30 d access, 90 d refresh) or die at the next password reset only if they were the personal token.

On 2026-09-20 a token started resolving the active brand like a session, and `ask_noor` was put under the same per-account chat ceiling as the web route. Still open: there is one OAuth scope, so every token grants every MCP tool including `ask_noor` (which reaches `write_file`, `update_brand` and credit-spending tools; `publish_item` still needs an approved item).

Update (step 3 of the agent-team upgrade, merged after): decision records, governance revisions and the activity log carry `by`, the acting login (a member's own address and role, a founder's `imp`, a token or a system job; section 4 item 10), so which login approved, rejected, edited, published, generated or changed governance is kept there. Routines and the weekly plan go through `generateBatch` (`via` "routine" / "weekly"), which has no role check: a cron or routine run records `systemBy(...)` (`systemBy("routine")`, `systemBy("cron")`; `byOf` gives a user with no actor `{kind: "system", job: "cron"}`), while the weekly plan's "Run now" (POST /api/schedule `{run: "weekly"}`) records the login that pressed it. `generateBatch` is also the path of `/api/generate`, chat and MCP, so a `can()` check added inside it would refuse every scheduled run as well as gate those.

With mail set up the link reaches only the invited address (a failed send withdraws the invite). Signup itself does not verify an address either, so an unregistered address can be taken either way; the founder console can now look a member login up by address and remove it (`removeMember`), as it can delete an owner account.

Removing the paused member and inviting the address again works.
36. **Media webhooks are hints, and only kie's can be signed.** Higgsfield does not sign its webhooks, and kie's callbacks are checked (`verifyKieSignature`, `X-Webhook-Timestamp` within ±300 s, `X-Webhook-Signature` compared with `sameSecret`) only when `KIE_WEBHOOK_HMAC_KEY` is set; without it a kie callback is accepted unsigned. A forged hint costs at most that one throttled status call per job per 4 s and cannot write outside the job's own scope; it cannot deliver a result, because result URLs in the body are never read. Anyone may still spend the per-IP allowance (240 per minute per provider and IP). Whether the founder will set `KIE_WEBHOOK_HMAC_KEY` is an owner step (`docs/Owner_Tasks_AR.md`, section ج2).

Whole-record USER saves outside the credit lock (item 18) are unchanged.
38. **Who changed governance is kept until the account is wiped.** Each revision in `govhist:{scope}` stores `by` with an email (the owner's, or a member's own login email; section 4 item 10) and whole before and after settings. Removing a member, or a member leaving, does not remove their email from past revisions or decisions; `wipeAccount` deletes `govhist:{s}` and `govlock:{s}` for every brand. Whether a member's email should be dropped from history when they leave is not decided.
39. **Support access can change and restore governance.** `governance.write` is not in `IMPERSONATION_BLOCKED` (section 0.1 item 4), so a founder impersonating an account can save or restore its governance (rows 18 and 104), including a restore that raises autonomy (turns auto-approve back on, removes caps). It is recorded as the founder, with the founder's email or `"key"` in `imp` (section 4 item 10), not refused; the account's logins see it as "Thakeen support", without `imp`. Whether restores or autonomy-raising changes belong in `IMPERSONATION_BLOCKED` is not decided.
40. **The activity log keeps member emails and free text until the account is wiped.** Each row of `activity:{scope}` stores `by` with an email (the owner's, or a member's own login email) and a note of up to 200 characters: an item's title, a file or brand field name, the changed governance settings, or the title an outside app gave a draft. Removing a member will not remove their email from past rows; `wipeAccount` deletes `activity:{s}` and the routines' `actskip:` markers for every brand, and the log is trimmed to its newest 2000 rows per brand. The notes are rendered as text and escaped in the CSV, never fed back into a prompt. The log is not an MCP tool: a bearer token cannot read or export it. Reading and exporting it is `read` (every login, rows 105 and 106), so a plain member sees which login did what, the other logins' addresses included (never the founder's: `imp` is dropped on the way out, section 4 item 10); whether a plain member should see them is not decided.

## Brand rules (2026-09-28)

The owner's banned words are enforced in code, not only in prompts (`src/lib/brand-rules.ts`). A customer-facing item whose text contains one cannot become `approved` through `PATCH /api/tasks/{id}` (409 `brand_violation`), cannot be published through `POST /api/publish/{id}` (409) or the chat tool `publish_item` (refused), and is created `pending` even when its type is auto-approved. ## Drafts from the owner's own assistant (2026-09-29)

MCP `save_draft` lets a bearer (the owner's Claude, ChatGPT or any MCP client, through a personal token or an OAuth grant; tokens resolve to owner accounts only, section 0) put text into the approval inbox of its own active brand. Its role in `MCP_TOOL_ACTION`: see `tests/role-gates.test.ts`. It can create, never decide: the item is always `pending` whatever `gov.autoApprove` says, the brand gate applies to it like to any agent's draft, and deciding stays with signed-in logins in the app: approving and publishing with the owner or an admin (`publish`), editing and rejecting with any login of the account (`drafts`). It spends no credits, so its only bound is `draftAllowed` (30 an hour, 150 a day per account). The text is untrusted input: typed and bounded by `parseDraft`, rendered in the inbox like model output (`render`), never executed or followed.

## Reem, the secretary (2026-09-29)

Phase 1 of the secretary agent keeps the owner's own to-dos, follow-ups and "who owes what" per brand scope (`src/lib/secretary.ts`, spec `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-29-secretary-reem-phase1.md`). What code enforces, not prompts:

1. Scope. Every key is built from `sc(u)` (the session's or the bearer's active brand); an item id from a request is format-checked and resolved under that scope only (rows 101-103, section 3.2).
2. Only the owner closes. `complete` (a written result is required), `confirm_done`, `reject_done`, `delete` and `restore` are `OWNER_ONLY`: `changeItem` and `mutateItem` refuse them unless the actor is the owner through the web route. Chat and MCP tools can create, update, snooze, add notes and propose "done" with evidence; a proposal ends any snooze and waits under "needs your confirmation". The one other close is deterministic: an item linked to an inbox task of the same scope closes with the result "linked item approved" when that task is approved (`closeLinked`, re-checked under the item lock). Because a link can close an item, only the owner changes a link on an existing item; chat and MCP set one only at creation and only to a `pending` task, so the close always follows an approval the owner made after the link (tests rule 192).
3. Concurrency. Writes to one item go through `sec:lock:{scope}:{id}` and bump `rev`; a request that sends an older `rev` gets 409.
4. Limits. 200 new items a day and 600 changes an hour per account (across brands), 600 reads an hour, 500 open items per brand, counted on create and on a restore that reopens an item.
5. Untrusted text. Titles, notes and evidence are cleaned and bounded; the chat tool answers quote them as JSON under a line that says they are data; private notes never reach a model (`itemLine` leaves them out of `agenda_get`, in chat and over MCP), and only the owner on the web can write one (a note from chat or MCP is stored shared). An agent's "done" (`proposedDone.evidence`) is the agent's claim: the page shows it attributed and marked as not checked, next to the fact code read from the stored linked task (`linkFactText` over `facts`: status, `decidedAt`, publish receipt) or "Nothing in the app confirms this"; the evidence text never becomes the checked fact.
6. Deletion. A delete is soft for 30 days; the daily cron purges after that in every brand scope, archived brands and suspended accounts included (`purgeIdle` over `dailyRoster().idle`); `wipeAccount` deletes every `sec:*` key of every brand scope (`secretaryKeys`).
7. No new vendor, no new environment variable, no WhatsApp or Telegram delivery, no time-based reminder job: reminders reach the owner through the page, the 8 AM brief (and its email, per the owner's preference) and the in-app overdue alerts (item 8). Nothing fires at an item's due time, and the copy (Reem's prompt and playbook, the chat chips, help and the site) says so instead of promising it.
8. Overdue alerts. One per item that fell behind on the local day it did and 1, 3 and 7 days later, then only the brief; each step's marker (`notifseen:{email}:todo-due:{scope}:{id}:{anchor ms}:{step}`, the due time for the owner's own item, the chase moment's anchor for what someone else owes since 2026-10-03) is claimed with SET NX before anything is pushed, at most 5 a brand a local day (`sec:alerts:{scope}:{day}`), claims past the allowance handed back. Since 2026-10-03 a contact's steps count working days and none is sent on a quiet day (item 21). Every other follow-up's marker is claimed the same way (`pushNotif`), so two overlapping cron runs notify once.
9. Time zone. A brand's zone is one of six allow-listed Gulf zones (`zones.ts`), stored only through `PUT /api/brand` (row 9) and re-checked on every read (`zoneOf`); `update_brand` cannot set it. It decides local days, the reading and writing of dates, the snooze presets, the country whose holidays apply (item 20) and the default weekend the chase clock skips.
10. Chase clock. A they-owe item is due for a follow-up at its due time, else at `expectBy` (two working days after creation by default), else two working days after the last recorded follow-up; recording one (`chased`, the owner's action or `todo_update {chased: true}`) never closes the item. Since 2026-10-03 the working days are the brand's, and the chase waits out quiet days, Ramadan's off-hours and a contact's absence (item 21).
11. Team roles (after main's teams change was merged, 2026-09-29). The list is the account's, per brand, shared by its logins. Any member adds, updates, snoozes, reschedules, notes and records follow-ups (`todo.write`, member); completing, confirming or sending back an agent's claim, deleting and restoring need the owner or an admin (`todo.close`, admin+; the route refuses with 403, the page hides the buttons). "Owner" in Reem's audit and in `OWNER_ONLY` means a person in the app rather than an agent; `login` in each audit line says which person. MCP tools map to `read` and `todo.write` (`MCP_TOOL_ACTION`); the chat tools are member-level, and none of them closes an item whatever the role.

Phase 2 (2026-09-30, spec `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-30-secretary-reem-phase2.md`; item 7 above no longer holds for reminders: there is now a time-based job, and still no WhatsApp or Telegram delivery):

12. Reminders. `remindAt` is set by the owner in the app or by an agent (`remind_at`), in the future, in the brand's zone (a date alone is 08:00 local). Only `indexWrites` writes `sec:due:{scope}` and `sec:duescopes`; the sweep never removes a due id: it leases it (`SET NX sec:firing:{scope}:{id}`, 330 s), so one run at a time works on it, and fires only an open item whose time came and is still the instant it read, re-read under its lock. Delivery comes before the item's write: the notification (fingerprint `todo-remind:{scope}:{id}:{remindAt ms}`), then the email only when that push was new and the preference is not `none`, then the item; a failure or a killed run leaves the id due for the next sweep, and neither the notification nor the email repeats (late, never lost). One scope's or account's failure never stops another's delivery, and no account starts after the sweep's deadline. A suspended account's reminders, and an archived brand's, wait. Every read-then-write of the bell holds the account's bell lock, so concurrent writers never drop each other's entries.
13. The tick. `POST /api/cron/secretary` refuses oversized input first, then runs for a signed QStash call or the CRON_SECRET bearer; only failed calls count against the per-IP limit, so a verified call is never refused by it. The scopes it serves come from the store, never from the request. The two QStash keys are secrets (`docs/system/variables.md`), compared in constant time, never logged. Tool answers and the page promise a reminder "at" its time only while a QStash call is under an hour old (`timedReminders`); otherwise with the next morning brief, at its local time in the brand's zone (08:00 or 09:00, `briefClock`), when the daily cron's fallback sweep fires it.
14. Reopen is `OWNER_ONLY` (admin+, `todo.close`) and counts against the open cap; chat and MCP cannot reopen. A reopen drops the item's inbox link, so the linked close cannot undo it.
15. A contact's phone number is written by the owner in the app only (`owner_only` from chat, MCP or an agent; the tools have no argument for it), only on a they-owe item, in the international form (8 to 15 digits, no leading zero, after Arabic digits, punctuation and a leading 00 are cleaned); a change of who owes the item drops it, whoever makes the change, unless the owner gives the new party's number in the same change; it never reaches a tool answer, a prompt, the walk card or the audit line. The page's wa.me link opens a fixed message the owner sends; the app sends nothing.
16. Walk. A card lists only the actions the viewer may take, and never Approve on a draft the brand guard holds; its buttons call the existing routes, which keep their gates (a member's approve would still be 403 at the task PATCH, a held draft 409 `brand_violation`). A confirm card shows the agent's claim as a claim next to what the app checked, and an approval card the draft's opening, so nothing is confirmed or approved unseen.
17. Evening wrap. Built from stored facts after the agenda closed approved linked items, sent at most once per scope and local day (SET NX marker, released only when the in-app notification could not be written), only when something happened or is due; email only with preference `all`; off when follow-ups are off (the switch's text says so).
18. Agents' handoff notes («ملاحظات التسليم», the chat tool `handoff_note`; not Majed's handoffs, section "Handoffs"). Chat only (refused over MCP); the sender is the turn's agent; bodies are bounded (500), cleaned, stored per scope (30) and shown to the receiver as `asData` inside `<handoff_notes>`, labelled as data. A blocked note opens at most one item per text that says the same thing and agent (a second one becomes a note on it; different requests in the same frame, and one-word bodies that differ, stay apart), at most 20 a day per account; to the owner it is due today; its item is a Reem item like any other, which only the owner closes.
19. Account deletion also removes `sec:due`, `sec:walk`, `sec:wrap` markers and `notes:{scope}` for every brand scope, and the scopes' members of `sec:duescopes`.

Phase 3 A1 (2026-10-03, spec `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-02-secretary-reem-phase3a-design.md` sections 1-3, `flows.md` 39; rows 132-137 and section 3.2): the brand's work calendar, one chase rule and contacts who are away. What code enforces, not prompts:

20. Working days. Stored per brand in `sec:cal:{sc(u)}`, written only by `PUT /api/secretary/calendar` (admin+, `brand.write`; 30 saves an hour; under `withLock(scope, "cal")`; one `calendar` audit line naming the login) after `parseCalendar` checks every field by name (an unknown field such as `scope` or `email` is refused, not ignored). Members read them (`read`), on the page and through GET; a member's PUT is 403 with nothing written. No chat or MCP tool changes them (`update_brand` does not touch them). The country's holidays are code (`COUNTRY_CAL`), never a request's or a model's; a stored value the calendar cannot be built from gives the defaults, so a bad record never stops the agenda or a chat turn.
21. Quiet days pause only the chasing of contacts. The owner's own items, the reminders the owner set and what an agent owes keep their dates and their alerts (an agent's count calendar days, any day). For a contact's debt, the chase moment and the alert steps are computed in code from the brand's calendar and away map (`chaseMoment`, `overdueAlerts`): working days, nothing sent on a weekend, Eid, official or added holiday, nor while the contact is away; a step that only holds keeps its marker alive (7 days), so it is never sent twice.
22. Away contacts. `sec:away:{sc(u)}`, one field per contact (`partyKey`), written by any member with `todo.write` through `PUT` / `DELETE /api/secretary/away` (`sec:write`; under `withLock(scope, "away")`; one `away` or `away_end` audit line naming the login, never the note). Only a contact can be away: the owner, an owner alias or an agent id is `not_contact` however it is written. At most 200 fields with ended ones, a last day from today to today + 366 in the brand's zone, a note of 200 characters. Ending is idempotent and keeps the entry for the back alert; only `remove: true` deletes it; the daily clean-up removes entries ended over 30 days ago.
23. Reem only proposes an absence. `contact_away` writes nothing to the away list: it saves a 7-day proposal shown as a card, and the absence is saved only when a person with `todo.write` presses Save (`POST /api/secretary/away/proposals/{id}`), once, through every check of `PUT` / `DELETE` again, with an audit line naming that login. The tool is offered only in Reem's chat turns and refused in `runTool` for any other agent, a turn with no agent, MCP and a token; MCP never lists it. Text from a customer, a supplier, a connector or a file can at most lead Reem to propose a card, which a person must save.
24. Account deletion also removes `sec:cal`, `sec:away`, and every away proposal's record and outcome (found through `secawayprops:{scope}`, since their ids are random) for every brand scope.

Phase 3 A2 (2026-10-03, spec `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-02-secretary-reem-phase3a-design.md` sections 4-6, rulings R32-R54 of the plan):

The owner keeps a series (row 138) so none of its copies is ever warned about or deleted; releasing it is admin+ only. A kept series is limited to 500 per brand.
28. Account deletion also removes `sec:series`, `sec:serieslist`, `sec:sdone`, `sec:spurge`, `sec:serieskeep`, `sec:swarn` and every `sec:spawn` claim (`secretaryKeys`).

Found by comparing Taskuary's send path with ours (study: private artifact of 2026-09-29), each checked in code with no model in the loop and pinned in `tests/security-gates.test.ts` (rules 213-220 of `tests.md`):

1. Unfilled placeholders. `gateTask` (`brand-rules.ts`) holds a customer-facing item whose body (and, for an article, whose published title; no screen edits a title, and cart recovery drops an unoffered percentage from a title instead of writing `[رقم]` there) has an unfilled placeholder (`[رقم]`, `[date]`, `[دليل مطلوب / proof needed]`, also with a note in parentheses; brackets are read as placeholders, a markdown link to an address, a checkbox, a footnote and, at approval, a script's direction label such as `[VO]`, `[VISUAL: …]`, `[Scene 1: …]` or `[لقطة قريبة]` are not, unless the label names a value; at publish direction labels hold too) at approval, auto-approval (every creation site passes the item's channel) and publish. A personalisation slot (`{{1}}`, `[الاسم]`, `[name]`) passes at approval only on a WhatsApp, email, SMS or call-script draft; at publish (`gateTask(..., { publish: true })`) every placeholder holds the item, and the inbox's publish buttons follow that stricter check. The inbox, MCP `check_brand` (which also reports `publishable`) and `save_draft` run the same function with the channel.
2. Chat publishes only what a person approved (`decidedAt`, set by an approval, by an owner's rewrite of an approved item, or an earlier owner publish), and nothing over MCP.
3. Brand gate inputs. An agent may add banned words (merged, up to the 400 characters the Brand page keeps); taking one out (`banned_remove`) and changing the offers are proposals (`brandprop:{scope}:{id}`, 7 days) that only admin+ (`brand.write`) applies through `POST /api/brand/proposals/{id}`, once. An offers change applies only while the offers still hold the value it was made against; a removal takes its words out of the list as it is then. Agent writes and applied proposals run under `brandlock:{scope}`; a reloaded chat shows each card's outcome (`brandprop-done:{scope}:{id}`); a plain member sees the card without Save; a card from MCP says so. Deleting the account deletes every proposal of every brand, its outcome and claim (found through `brandprops:{scope}`, since the ids are random), so the old and proposed offers or banned words do not outlive it (rule 240).
4. One publish per item and platform (`publishOnce`): a lock held a little longer than the caller may run (70 s from the publish route, 330 s from chat and MCP), the caller's checks again on the copy that is sent, an attempt marker kept unless the platform clearly refused (the connector's own leading 4xx status, a refused token, a check before sending; never a number inside the platform's message), a refusal of a repeat, and the owner's `again` to publish anyway. 5. The judge's automatic fail withdraws an auto-approval, never an owner's approval, never a published item.
6. Memory files are fenced data in every generation and chat prompt; `preferences.md` shows its newest notes; core memory files are not written over MCP, and the catalog import (which rewrites `products.md`) is not run over MCP.

## Handoffs (2026-09-30)

Majed, the lead, proposes work for the agents who own it, and a login of the account runs it (`src/lib/handoffs.ts`, `flows.md` 34). These are Majed's handoffs (`propose_handoff`, rows 107-109), not the agents' handoff notes (`handoff_note`, item 18 of the Reem section), which run nothing. What code enforces, not prompts:

1. Who proposes. Only a chat turn with Majed: the tool is offered in his turns only (`toolsFor`) and `runTool` refuses it to any other agent, a turn with no agent, a token or a turn over MCP (`ctx.via`, `ask_noor`) and a second proposal in one reply. No MCP tool proposes, runs, declines or lists handoffs (`MCP_TOOL_ACTION` and `tools/list` name none, and `ask_noor` talks to Noor). Proposing is free.
2. What the model sets. Only each item's type (from `HANDOFF_TYPES`) and brief, and `why`. The agent (`agentForTask`), the cost (`TASK_COST`), the status, the scope (`sc(u)` of the turn), `requestedBy` (the turn's `by`) and the origin (`web_chat`) are set in code.
3. No bypass. In Majed's turns `generate_content` and `draft_items` are refused for a type he does not own (`leadRefusal`), before any charge.
4. The brief is not a source. It reaches the child batch, its reviewer, the cross-reviewers and the judge as a labelled `<lead_brief>` data block, escaped with `asData`, never as owner notes; a figure, price or claim found only there is unsourced. A task made by a run keeps no `brief`.
5. Running. One item per request, by its position, under `handofflock:{scope}:{id}` for the whole run, with the item's status checked in code; the charge, the agent's cap, the refund, the brand gate, auto-approval and the cross-review hold are `generateBatch`'s, for the login that pressed Run (`generate`, member). Declining needs `drafts` (member). Both are refused to a login no role check accepts.
6. Depth 1. A run is a generation, which offers no tools: nothing a handoff makes can propose another handoff. `createHandoff` has one caller, the chat tool.
7. Retention. A record lives 180 days from its last write; the index keeps the newest 200 ids per brand, and the proposal that pushes an id past 200 removes it by value with its record, lock and auto-run attempt counters (two proposals at once each remove what they pushed off), while a run or decline rewrites a record only while it exists (SET XX), so no record outlives the index; `wipeAccount` deletes every record, lock, auto-run attempt counter and notification marker and index of every brand (`handoffKeys`), and the auto-run's hold (`handoffhold:{scope}`). A record keeps the owner's own message (500 characters) and the logins that proposed, ran or declined it until then; the card's view (`GET /api/handoffs/{id}`) shows neither.
8. Majed's auto-run (opt-in, `flows.md` 35). Off by default; only the owner switches it on (`governance.write`, row 18), only with a cap on an agent Majed hands work to and an explicit confirmation in the request, both checked in code (`govRefusal`). The routines cron (row 91) then starts at most one item a day per brand, and only an item `autoBlock` allows under the handoff's lock with the settings read again: Majed's own proposal, from the app's chat, requested by the account's OWNER (never a member, support or a connected app: `requestedBy.kind` is the chat turn's `by`, set in code), whose card the owner has (the reply carrying it was saved, `shownAt`, and its conversation was not deleted), still proposed, at most 7 days old, for an agent with an owner-set cap, of a type that keeps the 60% margin floor on a cold cache (`autoUnderFloor`: never `article` or `followup_seq` today), in a proposal of at most 3 items and 12 credits. Paused, it retries without reading anything; without a key it skips. It is inside the weekly cap (`isAutonomous` work and the routines' holds), bounded by an attempt counter per item, charged to the item's agent under that cap, logged and notified as the system's `auto_handoff` job, and every task it makes waits for the owner (`forcePending`), even for an auto-approved type. What it may start next is listed on the Governance page with Decline (`autoQueue`, the same rule), so an item whose card scrolled out of its thread can still be declined. The model never decides that anything runs.

## Chat allowance (2026-10-01)

Pricing phase 2 (`src/lib/chat-allowance.ts`, `src/lib/chat-budget.ts`, `flows.md` 37). What code enforces, not prompts:

1. One allowance per account. Every message is counted on the account's counter (`u.email`, the owner's for every seat): the owner's, an admin's, a member's, every brand's and a connected app's (MCP `ask_noor`) alike, against `chatAllowanceOf(u)` from `src/lib/pricing.ts`. No request field names the counter, the plan or the allowance.
2. Counted before the turn. `reserveChatTurn` runs after the session, the text and the burst limit and before `chat()` saves the message or calls a model (rows 30 and 53); INCRBY, so parallel messages cannot pass it. A refused message is in no thread, reached no model and cost nothing. A failed or uncosted turn is given back, a message the workspace is too large for included (no model call is made).
3. Past the allowance only the owner decides (`billing`, row 114): paused (the default), or continued on credits on a paid plan. A member, an admin or a connected app cannot change it, and no chat tool or MCP call does; a founder impersonating the account can, since `billing` is not in `IMPERSONATION_BLOCKED` (section 0.1 item 4). A trial never continues on credits.
4. Bounded cost per message. Nothing is held or taken before an over-allowance message: the gate only checks the balance is at least `chatOverflowHold()` (5 credits), so the turn's own tools can spend the whole balance. After the reply it is billed at most that: its metered model cost at the floor rate (`maxCostPerCreditUsd`), never more than the balance still holds, the remainder carried under one credit (`User.chatCarryMicro`), in one save under the user lock (`settleChatOverflow`), and the credits billed added to `usage:{email}:{YYYY-MM}:chat_cred`; a failed message is not billed. Every message, inside the allowance or not, has its chat model calls capped at $0.35 in code (`nextMaxTokens`, with `fitFirstCall` for the first call) and its history trimmed (`trimHistory`); generation asked for in chat stays charged in credits under the agents' caps, which never count chat messages.
5. The founder may override an account's allowance (`POST /api/admin/plan` `chatAllowance`, row 79): a whole number 0..100,000, checked in code; 0 stops chat for the account, "continue on credits" included (`reserveChatTurn` refuses every message with `chat_allowance`).
6. The status every login sees (`ChatStatus`: used, clamped to the allowance; allowance; left; `counted`, the raw count; period; the switch; the messages on credits this month and the credits they were billed; `hold`, the least a balance needs for one and the most one is billed) is the account's, read on the server; the client components import its type only. Deleting the account deletes the counters (section 5 item 17).

## Top-ups (2026-10-01)

Credit top-ups (`src/lib/topups.ts`, `flows.md` 38, rules 440-464). What code enforces, not prompts:

1. Only the founder adds, voids or refunds a pack (row 131, `adminActor`). No owner, member, connected app, chat tool or model output can add credits to a pack: the billing page's buttons only open a WhatsApp or email message to support, and nothing in the app takes a payment.
2. Packs are sold to paid plans only: `addTopup` refuses a trial under the ledger lock (409), whatever the console shows.
3. The pack, the amount paid, the payment date and the reference are checked in code (`parseTopup`) before any read; the account is the body's email, and only its own record and lock are touched.
4. Every change to a pack's credits happens under the per-user ledger lock in one save with the balance: activation and void (`addTopup`, `voidTopup`), spending (`debit`, `settleChatOverflow`), refunds (`returnCredits` with the charge's receipt), renewal and plan changes (`setMonthly`), bonuses and "+ credits" (`addMonthly`, never a pack's credits), and expiry (`settleTopups` on read, `saveSettledTopups` by the daily cron).
5. What the account sees of a pack leaves out what was paid, the payment reference, the payment id and who activated it: the billing page shows `topupView` (`id`, `credits`, `left`, `at`, `expiresAt`) and `GET /api/profile` `topupBalance` (`credits`, `left`, `expiresAt`); the founder sees the rest in the console. The payment reference is never written to `admin:log`.
6. A pack's payment and the partner's commission are decided in code (rules 454-455): "Add top-up" records the payment through `recordPayment` and `settle`, the same commission decision as any payment (attribution, 30 days, 4 months, self and team referrals), under the console's payment id, so a retried request never records it or its commission twice; voiding the pack voids the payment and the commission (`voidPaidTopup`), and the Payments route cannot void a live pack's payment or record a top-up's. No owner, member, model output or request body can mark a payment as a top-up's or move a commission.
7. A refund of unused credits (rules 460-464, founder decision of 2026-10-02) is the founder's alone and is bounded in code: only a live, paid, not yet refunded pack with credits left, only those credits (taken under the ledger lock, so a racing charge never spends them and an in-flight charge's credits are never refunded), only the amount `unusedRefundSar` computes from the pack (never one from the request), and only when the credits left are the ones the founder was shown (`left_changed`); the commission cut is computed from the one refund record, so it cannot be taken twice, and a refund that stopped halfway is finished from the pack's own record ("Finish refund", or any later payment change through `settle`), so no commission stands on refunded money (rule 465). No owner, member, chat tool or model output can ask for or record a refund in the app: the owner asks support, and the billing page and the terms only say that it can be asked for. A refunded pack is not shown to the account (`openLots`), and the transfer's reference, like the payment's, is never logged.
