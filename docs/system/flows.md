## 1. Signup (email first, then name and password)

Files: `src/app/[lang]/app/signup/page.tsx`, `src/components/app/AuthForm.tsx` (`SignupForm`), `src/lib/auth-client.ts`, `src/app/api/auth/signup/route.ts`, `src/lib/auth.ts`, `src/lib/referral.ts`, `src/lib/track.ts`.

1. Page redirects an already signed-in user to `/{lang}/app` (`signup/page.tsx:11`).
2. Client step 1: email is validated with `^[^\s@]+@[^\s@]+\.[^\s@]+$` (`AuthForm.tsx:34`). Step 2 asks for name and password; the client requires length >= 8, one letter, one digit (`passwordChecks`, `src/lib/auth-client.ts`). Each rule shows a check or cross and the words "met"/"not met" for screen readers, one `aria-live` line counts them ("2 of 3 password rules met"), and the submit button is `aria-disabled` (still focusable, described by the rules list): pressed early it names the unmet rules in a `role="alert"` message and moves focus to the password (rule 434, 2026-10-01). Query params read: `ref` (referral code), `url` (site for onboarding), `next` (kept only when `safeNext` accepts it: a same-site path, rule 543, `src/lib/safe-next.ts`).
3. `POST /api/auth/signup` with `{email, name, password, lang, ref, phone?}`.
4. Server: rate limit 5 per IP per hour (429 `slow_down`); normalizes email (trim, lowercase); `validEmail` (the address regex, and no `:` or `#`); `strongPassword` (8+, a letter, a digit); optional phone needs >= 9 digits. Then, inside `withLoginLock(email)` (`lock:login:{email}`, the lock invite acceptance takes too), `getLogin(email)`: an owner account or a member login at the address gets 409 `exists`, the same answer for both (2026-09-29); otherwise `createUser`.
5. `createUser` (`auth.ts:155`): bcrypt cost 10, plan `trial`, 50 credits, `trialEnds` = now + 3 days, name clipped to 80 chars. Writes `user:{email}`, adds to `users:all`. It also stamps `sessionsAfter` = `createdAt`, so a session issued for an earlier account at the same address never works again. Member logins are never created here (section 26).
6. `registerCode` writes `ref:{CODE}`. If `ref` matches `^[A-Za-z0-9]{6,10}$`, `applyReferral` runs (section 15).
7. `createSession(email)` sets cookie `nashr_session`. `track(email,"signup")`.
8. Errors (rule 433, 2026-10-01): the form posts through `postJson`, which never throws, and clears its busy state in `finally`. A 429 says "too many sign-ups from this connection, try in an hour", a dropped connection says to check the connection, a 5xx or an HTML error page says the problem is on our side; known codes (`exists`, `bad_email`, `weak_password`, `bad_phone`) keep their messages, and an unknown code never reaches the screen raw. Client navigates to `next`, or `/{lang}/app/start?url={url}` when a site URL was carried (the owner reviews and confirms it there before anything is read, section 6), else `/{lang}/app/start`. A carried URL never starts onboarding by itself: until 2026-09-28 it went straight to `/{lang}/app/chat?onboard={url}`, so a link like `/en/app/signup?url=attacker.example` chose the new workspace's brand.
9. Rate limit 5 per IP per hour; no email verification. Cloudflare Turnstile (2026-10-01, rule 426) when `TURNSTILE_SITE_KEY` and `TURNSTILE_SECRET_KEY` are both set: the form shows the widget on step 2 and sends its token as `turnstile`; the route verifies it (`captchaRefusal`, `src/lib/turnstile.ts`) after the input checks and before `getLogin`, so without a passing token no account is created and no address is confirmed to exist (400 `captcha_failed`, 503 `captcha_unavailable` when Cloudflare does not answer). A refused submit resets the widget (tokens are single use). With either key unset nothing is checked.

## 2. Login

Files: `login/page.tsx`, `AuthForm.tsx` (`LoginForm`), `src/lib/auth-client.ts`, `src/app/api/auth/login/route.ts`.

1. `POST /api/auth/login {email, password}`.
2. Rate limits 20 per IP and 8 per login per 15 min (429 `slow_down`). `getLogin(email)` finds the login: the owner record `user:{email}` or a member record `member:{email}` (the owner record wins should both exist). bcrypt compares against THAT login's own hash, so the owner's password never opens a member login and a member's never opens the owner's. Unknown login or bad password: 401 `invalid` (same response for both).
3. Owner: 403 `suspended` (only after a correct password). Member (2026-09-29): 401 `invalid` when the owner record is missing, `memberOf` does not name it, or the member was accepted before the owner record was created; 403 `suspended` when the owner is suspended; 403 `disabled` when the member is paused by the seat limit (section 26 step 13).
4. `createSession(login email)`, `track(owner email, "login")` (analytics count accounts), `{ok:true}`. Client goes to `next` when `safeNext` accepts it (a same-site path: no control character, no backslash, a single leading `/`, same origin once parsed; rule 543) or `/{lang}/app`. Every later request resolves a member session to the owner's account again (section 5).
5. No lockout beyond the rate limits.
6. The form (rule 433, 2026-10-01) says "wrong email or password" only for a 401; 429 says to wait 15 minutes, 403 `suspended` and `disabled` have their own messages, a dropped connection and a 5xx each say what happened, and the button always comes back.

## 3. Logout

`src/components/app/LogoutButton.tsx` posts to `POST /api/auth/logout` (`src/app/api/auth/logout/route.ts`), which calls `destroySession()` (`auth.ts:241`): deletes `session:{token}` and the `nashr_session` cookie. It does not touch `nashr_admin`, `nashr_return` or `nashr_impersonate`.

## 4. Password reset

### Forgot
Files: `forgot/page.tsx`, `AuthForm.tsx` (`ForgotForm`), `src/app/api/auth/forgot/route.ts`, `src/lib/notify.ts`, `src/lib/rate.ts`.

1. `POST /api/auth/forgot {email, lang, turnstile?}`. Bad email format: 400. Then the per-IP limit (8 per 15 min), so junk tokens cannot buy unlimited siteverify calls. With both Turnstile keys set, the token is verified next, before the per-address limit and the lookup (rule 427): a missing or rejected token is 400 `captcha_failed` whether or not the address has an account, and does not use up the address's three links.
2. Rate limits: 8 per 15 min per IP (before the token check) and 3 per 15 min per email (after it). When exceeded the route still answers `{ok:true, mail}`.
3. `getLogin(email)`. When the login exists and mail can reach a customer (`mailReady()`: `RESEND_API_KEY` and `MAIL_FROM` both set): a 32-byte hex token is generated, its SHA-256 is stored as `pwreset:{hash}` with `{email, kind}` (`kind` is `owner` or `member`) for 3600 s, and a link `${appOrigin()}/{lang}/app/reset#t=...` is emailed (the token in the URL fragment, never a query string, so it stays out of server, proxy and edge logs and the Referer header; rule 546) to the login's own address. A member whose owner record is gone gets no link and the same answer.
4. Response is always `{ok:true, mail:boolean}`; `mail` says whether email delivery is configured, not whether the account exists. The forgot page shows a WhatsApp link when `SUPPORT_WHATSAPP` is set.

### Reset
Files: `reset/page.tsx`, `AuthForm.tsx` (`ResetForm`, the same rules display and error handling as signup, rules 433-434), `src/app/api/auth/reset/route.ts`. `ResetForm` reads the token from `location.hash` once, removes the fragment with `history.replaceState`, and posts it in the JSON body; a `?token=` query is never read (rule 547).

1. `POST /api/auth/reset {token, password}`. Rate limit 10 per 15 min per IP (429).
2. Password rule server-side: length >= 8, one Unicode letter, one digit. Token must match `^[a-f0-9]{64}$`.
3. Looks up `pwreset:{sha256(token)}`. Missing: 400 `bad_token`. Single use: `pwresetuse:{hash}` is taken with SET NX (1 h) before the record is deleted, so two requests racing on one link cannot both pass (the second gets 400 `bad_token`).
4. `getLogin(record.email)`: the login must still be of the kind the record names (a record without `kind`, written before teams, is an owner's), else 400 `bad_token`, so a member removed since, or an address that changed sides, gets nothing. A suspended owner (for either kind) gets 403 `suspended`; the token is spent either way.
5. Owner: the new hash, `revokeSessions` and `revokeTeamSessions` in one save inside `withUserLock`, then `revokeAllMcp` and `createSession(owner)`. Every owner session, every member session and every MCP token dies with the old password; each member signs back in with its own.
6. Member (2026-09-29): under `withMemberLock` the member record alone gets the new hash and `sessionsAfter = now`, which signs out that member's other devices; the owner's hash, sessions and tokens are untouched. Then `createSession(member)`, unless the member is seat-paused: the password is set but the answer is 403 `disabled` with no session.

### Change password while signed in
`PATCH /api/account {currentPassword, newPassword}` (`src/app/api/account/route.ts`): session and `forbid(u, "password.change")` (every login; refused to a founder impersonating); `strongPassword`; the current password is checked against the ACTING login's own hash, never the account's for a member (401 `wrong_password`). Owner: new hash and `revokeSessions` inside the user lock, then `revokeAllMcp`. Member (2026-09-29): the member record only, under `withMemberLock`, with its own `sessionsAfter = now`; the owner's hash, sessions, MCP token and connected apps are untouched. Either way this device's session is destroyed and a new one issued for the same login, so only the other devices are signed out.

## 5. Session handling and cookie names

| Cookie | Set by | Value | Attributes | Lifetime |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `nashr_session` | `createSession` (`src/lib/auth.ts:234`), `api/admin/impersonate/route.ts` (restores the founder's saved token) | 32-byte hex token; server side `session:{token}` holds `{email, at, imp?}`: the LOGIN email (owner or member), the issue time, and on an impersonation session the founder behind it (`imp`) | httpOnly, sameSite lax, `Secure` unless plain http to the loopback interface, path `/` | 30 days (cookie and Redis TTL); 2 hours for an impersonation session, and one opened from a key session also ends with it |
| `nashr_admin` | `createAdminSession` (`src/lib/auth.ts`), called by `api/admin/login/route.ts` | A random 32-byte hex id naming `adminsession:{sha256(id)}` (never the key) | httpOnly, lax, Secure by transport | Cookie: 12 hours (the absolute cap, not renewed). Record: 15 minutes, renewed at most once a minute by each admin request and by each request through a customer impersonation it opened, never past 12 hours from the key entry |
| `nashr_return` | `api/admin/users/[email]/route.ts:120` | The founder's own session token, saved while impersonating | httpOnly, lax, secure in production | 2 hours |
| `nashr_impersonate` | `api/admin/users/[email]/route.ts:121` | Email of the impersonated user (display and audit only; `byOf` never reads it: who acted comes from the session record's `imp`) | httpOnly, lax, secure in production | 2 hours |
| `nashr_lang` | `src/components/LangToggle.tsx:8` (client JS) | `ar` or `en` | not httpOnly, lax | 1 year |

`currentUser()` (`src/lib/auth.ts:248`, `resolveSession`; rewritten 2026-09-29): cookie, `session:{token}`, then in one parallel round `user:{login}`, `member:{login}` and `activebrand:{login}`.

- Owner login (`user:` exists; it wins should both exist): `null` when suspended or when the session predates `sessionsAfter`. Otherwise the record with `brandId`, `scope` and actor `{kind:"owner", role:"owner"}`, or `{kind:"founder", role:"owner", impersonating: imp}` when the session carries `imp`.
- Member login: `null` when the member is disabled, its role or `memberOf` is malformed, the session carries `imp`, or the session predates `member.sessionsAfter`. Then the owner record and the owner's brand list are read in one round, and `null` again when the owner is missing or suspended, the session predates `owner.teamSessionsAfter`, or the member's `acceptedAt` is earlier than the owner's `createdAt`. Otherwise the OWNER's record with actor `{kind:"member", email, name, lang, role}` and scope `scopeFor(owner, brandId)`, where `brandId` is the member's own active brand when it is one of the owner's active brands, else `default`.

Membership is re-read on every request; a session never caches the owner. `saveUser` strips `actor`, `scope` and `brandId`. Sessions are not rotated and not bound to IP or user agent; they stop working through the revocation stamps (Revocation, below), suspension (read time), the login being removed, or the account being wiped (the record is gone).

## 6. First-run onboarding inside the chat

Files: `start/page.tsx`, `src/components/app/DictionaryStart.tsx`, `src/components/app/StartLaunch.tsx`, `src/lib/dictionary.ts`, `src/app/api/onboard/confirm/route.ts`, `src/lib/onboard-confirm.ts`, `chat/page.tsx:35-50`, `src/components/app/ChatPanel.tsx:483`, `src/app/api/onboard/route.ts`, `src/lib/crawl.ts`, `src/lib/onboard.ts`, `src/app/api/onboard/plan/route.ts`, `src/components/app/OnboardCards.tsx`.

Steps 1-6 (brand name and sector, five voice words, a sample plan and drafts tagged «مثال», the approve tap, the analysis and measurement preview) live in the page's reducer: nothing is sent or saved, and leaving loses them. That save is create-only (`create: true`, rule 527): when a brand was built meanwhile, in another tab or by another admin, the route answers 409 `exists`, keeps that brand whole and the page opens `/{lang}/app` with it. A manual alternative under the title on every step links to `/{lang}/app/onboarding` (brand form, `PUT /api/brand`). A URL carried in from a link (signup `?url=`, start `?url=`, chat `?onboard=`) only pre-fills the field and shows a review note ("Check this link before Noor starts"); nothing is read until the owner presses Go. Since 2026-09-30 (rule 246) the start page passes `setupCost = onboardCost(u.brandId)` and the balance to the wizard: for the first brand (the default scope) it says setup is free; for an added brand it states the 9 credits for the build from the URL (the manual form is free) and the balance before Go, and when the balance is short it says so, turns Go off and offers "View plans" (the credits paywall). When a charge for this brand is already held (`User.onboardPaid[scope]`, a run that did not finish), it passes `setupPaid`: the wizard says the setup is already paid for and keeps Go on at any balance, as the server does.
2. Go posts `{url}` to `POST /api/onboard/confirm`: session and admin+ (`onboard`) required, URL trimmed and clipped to 300 characters (`onboardUrl`), length >= 4 else 400 `bad_url`, 409 `already_onboarded` when the workspace has a brand. For an added brand without a held charge (`User.onboardPaid[scope]`) it answers 402 `trial_over` or `no_credits` with `need` and `credits` and writes nothing (the wizard raises the paywall and says nothing was charged). Otherwise it writes `onboardok:{scope}` = the URL (1 day) and nothing else: no crawl, no model call, no charge, and answers `{ok, cost}` (0 or 9), plus `paid: true` when a charge is held. Since 2026-10-04 (rule 522) the body may also carry `answers`, the first-login game's (brand name, sector, five voice words, language; `parseAnswers` in `src/lib/dictionary.ts`): when sent they must be a finished game or the request is refused 400 `bad_answers` before anything is written; valid ones are stored as `onboardgame:{scope}` beside the URL for the same day, and a confirm without them deletes any left by an earlier one. The session cookie is SameSite=Lax, so another site cannot submit it for a signed-in owner. The wizard then routes to `/{lang}/app/chat?onboard={url}` (also on 409, where the chat opens as usual).
3. The chat page without a brand reads `onboardok:{scope}` and hands that URL to `ChatPanel`. The `?onboard=` value itself is never used to build: without a confirmation the page redirects to `/{lang}/app/start?url={value}` so the owner reviews it.
4. `ChatPanel` posts `{url}` to `POST /api/onboard`. Server requires a session, admin+ (`onboard`) and a URL of length >= 4, then, in this order: 409 `already_onboarded` when `brand:{scope}` exists; 403 `unconfirmed` unless the URL equals `onboardok:{scope}` (checked in code, before the paused check, the daily allowance, the lock and the crawl, so an unconfirmed URL costs nothing); 503 `paused`; for an added brand without a held charge, a 402 JSON answer `{ok:false, error, need, credits}` when the trial is over (`trial_over`), Noor's monthly cap in that scope would be passed (`agent_cap`, with `agent`, `spent`, `cap`) or the balance is under 9 (`no_credits`), before the daily allowance and the lock so a certain refusal costs none of the three tries; 429 after three runs a day per account; lock `onboardlock:{scope}` taken with an owner token (`db.lockOwned`, 330 s = `maxDuration` + 30, so a second run can only start once the first is over or was killed), a second concurrent run gets 409 `busy`; the run releases it with `unlockOwned`, which deletes it only while it still holds that token. Under the lock the brand and the confirmation are read again (409 or 403, lock released). Then, for an added brand (since 2026-09-30): `chargeOnboard(u.email, 9, {scope, agent: "noor", cap})` under the account lock reuses a held charge (`User.onboardPaid[scope]`, restarting its clock) or, with none, refuses an ended trial and charges, writing the debit and the hold in the same save; a refusal releases the lock and answers the same 402 JSON before any stream, and a new charge is followed by the agent-cap warning. The first brand charges nothing. Mock mode charges too. `ChatPanel` shows the paywall and "nothing was charged" on a 402, and on a 429 a daily-limit message that says this try charged nothing and a setup already paid for stays paid.
5. SSE stream (10 s ping). Steps: intro message, `crawl(url)` of the confirmed URL (server-side fetch of the user URL, 9 s timeout, follows redirects, `crawl.ts:45`), then `buildFromSite`: the crawled snapshot is sent as one `<site_snapshot>` block (`asData`) under a data-not-instructions line, the owner's hints outside it (`onboardUserMessage`, rule 574), in three parallel `claude-opus-5` calls from the same snapshot (core: brand, brand.md, plan, goal, summary; identity: guidelines, opportunities; market: research, strategy), one retry per part; the status line advances as each part lands. The game's answers are read under the lock from `onboardgame:{scope}` (`confirmedAnswers`, validated again), never from the request: when present they replace the request's `hints` for the model, and their name, industry and tone are written over the built brand before it is saved, so the owner's own words win over the crawl (rule 523).
6. Writes: `saveBrand(scope)`, each returned file via `writeFile(scope, ...)`, `preferences.md` if absent, `onboardplan:{scope}`, deletes `onboardok:{scope}` and `onboardgame:{scope}` and drops the held charge (`settleOnboard`): one confirmation, one build, one charge. The `done` event carries the balance, which the chat's credit pill takes. Every assistant message is persisted to `chat:{scope}` via `push`. A failed or paused run keeps the confirmation, so "refresh to try again" works for a day.
7. Credits (since 2026-09-30, rule 246): the first brand's onboarding charges nothing; an added brand's charges `ONBOARD_EXTRA_BRAND_COST` (9, `src/lib/pricing.ts`) to Noor in its scope, as in step 4. When the run fails before `saveBrand` (a model error, `ModelPaused`, a crawl failure) `refundOnboard` gives the held 9 back and drops the hold in one save, so it happens once: a second refund finds no hold and gives nothing, and a refund whose save was written but reported an error has still dropped the hold, so the next run pays afresh rather than building free. ## 13. MCP server and OAuth 2.1 authorization

### Personal token
`GET /api/mcp/token` returns only `exists`; `POST` revokes then creates `nashr_` + 24 random bytes base64url, stores `mcptoken:{sha256}` and `mcptoken-of:{email}` → that sha256 with no TTL, and returns the token once (it cannot be read back, rules 552, 555); `DELETE` revokes the personal token only. All three are owner only (`mcp.token`, 2026-09-29): a member and a founder impersonating get 403. The slot is keyed by the acting login, which past the gate is the owner.

### OAuth 2.1
1. Discovery: `/.well-known/oauth-authorization-server` and `/.well-known/oauth-protected-resource` (rewrites). Issuer and endpoints derive from `APP_ORIGIN`.
2. Client registration: `POST /api/oauth/register` is open to anyone. Keeps up to 10 `http(s)` redirect URIs, issues `client_id`, and a `client_secret` only when `token_endpoint_auth_method = client_secret_post`. Rate limited to 30 per IP per hour (429 `slow_down`); no expiry.
3. Authorize: `/oauth/authorize?client_id&redirect_uri&response_type=code&code_challenge&code_challenge_method&state&scope`. Anonymous users are redirected to `/ar/app/login?next=...`. The page checks client, exact `redirect_uri` membership, `response_type=code`, presence of `code_challenge`; shows Allow/Deny with the client name supplied at registration.
4. Approve: `POST /api/oauth/approve` (form post, session required) re-validates client and redirect URI, requires `code_challenge_method=S256`, issues a 10 min single-use code bound to client, redirect URI, challenge, email and `scope` (free text from the request, default `noor`). Deny redirects with `error=access_denied`. Owner only (`oauth.approve`, 2026-09-29): a member and a founder impersonating get 403, and the consent page shows them no form, only who is signed in.
5. Token: `POST /api/oauth/token` (JSON or form; client auth via Basic, body secret, or none for public clients; a secret is compared with `sameSecret`; 60 requests per IP per 15 minutes, checked first, 429 `slow_down`). `authorization_code`: the code (stored as `oauthcode:{sha256(code)}`) is taken with GETDEL, so it is single use under concurrency (rule 549), then client id, redirect URI and PKCE S256 are verified. Issues access token `nashr_...` stored as `mcptoken:{sha256}` → `{email, grant}` for 30 days and refresh token `nashr_r_...` in `oauthrefresh:{sha256}` for 90 days; the grant `mcpgrant:{email}:{id}` holds both hashes.
6. Refresh: `grant_type=refresh_token` rotates: the old refresh record is taken with GETDEL (rule 548), the grant must exist and list it (rule 550), the grant is rewritten with SET XX (rule 551), and a new pair is issued; client id must match. The previous access token is deleted and also refused at once, because the grant no longer lists it. A refresh token with no grant is refused.
7. `GET /api/mcp/grants` lists the account's grants and `DELETE ?id=` or `?all=1` revokes (Revocation, below); both are owner only (`oauth.grants`) since 2026-09-29.

### MCP calls

### MCP `save_draft` (2026-09-29): drafts from the owner's own assistant
Lets an owner write with their own Claude or ChatGPT subscription and still review in Nashr. `{type, body, title?, channel?, notes?, lang?}`, one item per call (`src/lib/external-drafts.ts`; action `drafts`, any role, and an MCP bearer is always the owner):

Names and text are stripped to one plain line (`clean`). Missing name column or metrics: 422 with a reason code.
3. `analyze`: the metric defaults to what the file supports (ROAS, then cost per result, CTR, open rate, conversion rate) and can be switched. A row is ranked only above the metric's sample minimum (500 impressions for CTR, 30 clicks for cost per result, 50 spend for ROAS, 30 clicks for conversion rate, 100 sends for open rate). Findings: top and bottom three, best against the median, wording patterns (offer, number, question, Arabic, short) compared by pooled metric when both groups have three or more ads and differ by 20% or more, best and weakest placement, and spend with no result when it is 10% or more of spend. Confidence is "medium" at ten times the sample minimum with five or more ads, otherwise "low"; never "high".
4. `savePerf` stores the snapshot in `perfimport:{scope}` and writes `performance-learnings.md`, which `memoryBlock` includes, so every later generation, the weekly plan included, reads it. The file states that quoted names are data, not instructions, and that findings are comparisons, not promises. The reports card links to chat with prompts to write variants of the winners or plan next week.
5. No model call and no credits. `DELETE` removes the snapshot and the file. An upload replaces the last snapshot and the file, so replacing is deleting: `POST` needs admin+ (`performance.import`) and `DELETE` needs admin+ (`performance.delete`). A connected ad account's sync (section 21) reads the account's own numbers and stays open to every login. A member sees the findings with a note instead of the upload and remove controls.

A follow-up (`chased`, the page's "Followed up" / «تابعت», or `todo_update {chased: true}` when the owner says they followed up) sets `lastChasedAt`, adds a note of kind `chased` («تمت المتابعة»; never "sent") and restarts the clock; it never closes the item, and on the owner's own item it is 409 `not_theirs`. The audit line's action is `chased`.
6. Closing. Chat and MCP can only propose: `todo_propose_done {id, evidence}` sets `proposedDone`, ends any snooze, and the item waits under "needs your confirmation". The evidence is the agent's own words: the page shows it as that agent's claim («ريم تقول: …», «سامي يقول: …», «مساعدك يقول: …», "Reem says: …", marked as not checked) and next to it, under "Checked by the app" / «تحقّق التطبيق», what code read from the stored linked task (`linkFactText` over `facts`: still waiting for approval, rejected on a date, approved by the owner on a date or by a rule, and where it was published), or "Nothing in the app confirms this" when the item has no link; `agenda_get` writes the same as "claimed done by … (a claim, not checked); checked by the app: …". The owner completes from the page with a written result (`complete {result}`; the Done button opens a required field), or confirms a proposal (`confirm_done`, the evidence becomes the result) or sends it back (`reject_done`, a note of kind `not_yet` records the evidence; `noteText` words it in the reader's language). These, `delete` and `restore` are refused for any actor other than the owner on the web route (`owner_only`).
7. The one automatic close: an item whose `links.taskId` names an inbox task of the same scope (set by the owner, or by chat or MCP at creation to a pending task, so an approval the owner makes after the link) closes with the result "linked item approved" when that task is `approved` (publishing approves too), found when the agenda is read (page, route, `agenda_get`, brief) or by the daily job, and re-checked under the item lock (`closeLinked`, audit actor `rule` or `cron`). The page lists it under "done" with the checked fact (when the owner approved it and where it was published).
8. Delete and restore: `delete` sets `deletedAt` and `expiresAt` (30 days) and moves the id from `sec:open` to `sec:deleted`; the page shows "Deleted. Undo" and a restorable list; `restore` puts it back where it was (open or done) until `expiresAt`, then answers 410; a restore that reopens an item counts against the 500 open items like a new one (409 `open_limit`). The daily cron's `purgeExpired` removes at most 200 expired items per scope per day with their `sec:deleted`, `sec:all`, `sec:done` and `sec:open` entries, for every brand scope: the active ones through `secretaryDaily`, archived brands and suspended accounts through `purgeIdle` (`dailyRoster().idle`).
9. Brief: `runDaily` adds «مهامك (ريم): N متأخرة، N لليوم، N بانتظار غيرك» (and the numbers to follow up and to confirm; since 2026-10-03 on a Ramadan day a contact's chases whose window opens later today, with the time, section 39 step 6) with the first three overdue or due-today titles, stored as `Brief.agenda`; the brief email carries it, and the "important" preference sends when a to-do is overdue or due today.
10. Overdue alerts (`followUps` → `overdueTodoAlerts` in `src/lib/notifications.ts`): one `todo` notification from Reem per overdue item, linking to `/{lang}/app/todo`, on the local day it fell behind and on the 1st, 3rd and 7th day after (`overdueAlerts`, `alertStep`: day 0 → step 0, days 1-2 → 1, 3-6 → 3, day 7 → 7, later → none; a missed day inside a window is caught up once). Each step is claimed once with SET NX on `notifseen:{email}:todo-due:{scope}:{id}:{dueAt ms}:{step}` (7 days, set again by every daily run while its step holds), so a new due date starts afresh and two overlapping runs send it once. At most `SEC_ALERTS_PER_DAY` (5) a brand a local day, critical and earliest first, counted in `sec:alerts:{scope}:{day}` (2 days); a claim beyond the allowance is handed back (its marker deleted) so the item can still go on another day of its window. The seventh day's alert says it is the last; after it the item is only in the morning brief. Every other follow-up keeps its own fingerprint, now claimed the same atomic way (`pushNotif`). Since 2026-10-03 this holds as written for the owner's own items only; what someone else owes follows the chase rule, counted in working days for a contact and never sent on a quiet day, with the marker `todo-due:{scope}:{id}:{anchor ms}:{step}` (section 39 step 7).
11. The page `/{lang}/app/todo` ("مهامي" / "My to-dos", in the sidebar under Approvals) is rendered on the server from `todoPage(sc(u))`: sections per bucket, each row with its title, due date formatted in the brand's zone, who owes it, when it is expected and the last follow-up, badges, its last three notes (private ones included: this is the owner's page) and the next step, and Done (result required), Snooze (2 hours, tonight 8 PM while it is still ahead, tomorrow 9 AM, local), Reschedule (a datetime-local input read as local time), "Followed up" on someone else's item, and Delete (with Undo, a toast pinned to the bottom of the screen for 8 seconds; restoring from the Deleted list clears it); "needs your confirmation" rows show the claim and the checked fact and get Confirm / Not yet; then the add form, the last 10 done (with the checked fact of a linked item) and the restorable deleted items. The subtitle and the date labels name the zone («بتوقيت دبي»). Every action is one request followed by a refresh of the server render. There is no alert at the due time, and reminders on WhatsApp or Telegram are not available; the page says both.
12. Account deletion: `wipeAccount` deletes every key `secretaryKeys(scope)` names for every brand scope: each id in `sec:all`, the open, done, deleted and id sets, the audit log and the alert counters of the last days (since 2026-10-03 also `sec:cal`, `sec:away` and Reem's away proposals, section 39 step 11). The `notifseen:` alert markers expire within seven days: a daily run sets a step's marker again while its step holds, and no run reaches a deleted account.

## 26. Teams: members, invites and seats (2026-09-29)

Files: `src/lib/team.ts`, `src/lib/roles.ts`, `src/lib/auth.ts` (`resolveSession`, `getLogin`, `PLAN_SEATS`, `seatLimit`), `src/app/api/team/*`, `src/app/[lang]/app/team/page.tsx` (`TeamPanel`, `teamPerms`), `src/app/[lang]/app/join/page.tsx` (`JoinForm`, `inviteToken.ts`), `src/app/api/admin/plan/route.ts`, `src/lib/account.ts`. Design: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-29-teams-design.md`. Roles and gates: `permissions.md` 0.1.

1. Model. The account stays the owner's `User` at `user:{owner}`: plan, credits, brands, scopes, governance, usage and cron are unchanged. A member is a separate login at `member:{email}` (`{email, name, lang, passHash, memberOf, role, invitedBy, acceptedAt, sessionsAfter, disabled?}`), listed in `members:{owner}`. It never passes through `saveUser` or `createUser`, never enters `users:all`, and gets no trial credits, referral code or signup tracking. An email is one login across `user:` and `member:`: signup and accept both create logins under `lock:login:{email}`.
2. Members page (`/{lang}/app/team`, every role; "Members" / "الأعضاء"). Server-rendered from `teamView(u)`, the same body `GET /api/team` returns: the acting person, seats used of the limit, the roster (owner first; each member's role, `acceptedAt` and `disabled`) and, for admin and owner, the pending invites. The controls come from `teamPerms(u)`, which is `can()`; after every change the panel reloads `GET /api/team`.
3. Invite (`POST /api/team/invite {email, role, lang?}`). Needs `team.invite.member` (admin+); `role: "admin"` needs the owner; a founder impersonating cannot invite. Rate limits `team:invite:acct:{owner}` 20 a day (5 on a trial, which anyone can open with one signup), `team:invite:ip:{ip}` 30 an hour and `team:invite:to:{sha256(address)}` 5 a day across all accounts, counted on every attempt, because each answer says whether an address is free and each emailed invite is mail from the product's own domain to a stranger. `createInvite`: the role must be `admin` or `member` (400 `invalid_role`); the email is normalised and must pass `validEmail` (400 `invalid_email`); the acting member's record must still hold its role (403). The owner's or the inviter's own address is 409 `unavailable`; then, under `lock:team:{owner}`, an existing owner or member login anywhere, or a pending invite for the address in this team, gets the same 409 `unavailable`, and seats used + 1 over the limit gets 409 `{error:"seats", used, limit}`. The token is 32 random bytes (64 hex); only its SHA-256 is stored, as `invite:{hash}` (`{email, role, owner, invitedBy, createdAt, expiresAt, lang}`, TTL 7 days) plus an entry in `invites:{owner}`. The link is `${appOrigin()}/{lang}/app/join#t={token}`: the token is in the fragment, so it never reaches a server or an edge log. When `mailReady()` the link is emailed in the invite's language from a fixed template that names only the inviting login's address (never a display name or brand name, which are free text the sender chose), and the link goes to the invited address or nowhere: when that send fails the invite is withdrawn under `lock:team` (`discardInvite`) and the answer is 502 `{error:"send_failed"}` with no link, so an inviter cannot take an address they do not control by making the send fail. Only when mail is not set up at all does the response carry `link` once for the inviter to pass on (the Members page then says a link is created, not that an email is sent). It can never be shown again (only the hash exists); to resend, cancel and invite again. The account's bell gets an item that names neither the invited address nor the role (the bell is shared by every login, and pending invites are admin+) and says whether an email went out. Response: `{ok, invite: {id, email, role, expiresAt}, emailed, link?}`, where `id` is the hash.
4. Cancel (`DELETE /api/team/invite {id}`). Needs `team.invite.cancel` (admin+; an admin cancels `member` invites only; not a founder impersonating). The id must name an invite of this account (else 404); under `lock:team:{owner}` the invite is read again and, if still there, it and its index entry are deleted (404 when an accept holding the lock used it first).
5. Join page (`/{lang}/app/join`, public, listed in the app layout's public paths). `JoinForm` reads the token from `location.hash` (`readInviteToken`: 64 hex or nothing), clears the fragment with `history.replaceState`, and never puts the token in a query string. It keeps the token in `sessionStorage` for the tab (`rememberInviteToken`), so switching the page language, which reloads without the fragment, still finds it; it is forgotten after a successful accept and whenever the server calls it invalid, and the page works without storage. A signed-in visitor is shown who they are signed in as and must log out first; nobody is joined into an existing session. Signed out, the form posts `{token}` to `POST /api/team/accept` for a preview.
6. Preview (`POST /api/team/accept {token}`). Rate limit `team:peek:ip:{ip}` 30 per 15 min. `peekInvite` returns `{team, inviter, email, role, expiresAt}` for a live invite whose owner exists and is not suspended and whose inviter still passes the accept's check (the owner, or an enabled admin of this team inviting a plain member); anything else is 400 `invalid`. Nothing is consumed.
7. Accept (`POST /api/team/accept {token, name, password, lang?}`). Rate limit `team:accept:ip:{ip}` 10 per 15 min. `acceptInvite`: a live invite (400 `invalid`); a name, control characters removed and cut to 80 (400 `invalid_name`); `strongPassword` (400 `weak_password`); bcrypt runs before any lock. Then single use: `inviteuse:{hash}` is taken with SET NX (30 s; a concurrent accept gets 400 `invalid`). Under `lock:team:{owner}` everything that decides the answer is read again, so a cancel, a plan change, a demotion or an account deletion that finished while the accept waited is seen: the invite must still be stored AND in `invites:{owner}` (a cancelled one is 400 `invalid`, never counted as a new seat); the owner exists, is not suspended and is not newer than the invite (an address registered again); an invite sent by a member needs that member to be still an enabled admin of this team and the role to be `member`; and the seats are counted against the owner's limit as it is now, the invite's own seat counted once (409 `seats`). Under `lock:login:{email}` the address must still be free across `user:` and `member:` (409 `unavailable`). The member is written with `acceptedAt = sessionsAfter = now` and the language from the request, else the invite's; the invite and its index entry are deleted. The route then destroys any session on the device, creates one for the member and puts a bell item on the owner. The new login lands in the owner's workspace (section 5).
8. Member login, forgot, reset and password change act on the member record only (sections 2 and 4): the member's own hash and its own `sessionsAfter`; the owner's hash, sessions, MCP token and grants are never touched. A seat-paused member cannot sign in (403 `disabled`).
9. Remove (`DELETE /api/team/member {email}`). Needs `team.remove.member` (admin+); removing an admin needs the owner; a founder impersonating cannot. The member must belong to this account (404). Under `lock:team:{owner}` and then `lock:member:{email}` (the lock every member-record writer holds), the record is read again and, while it still belongs to this account, `member:{email}`, `activebrand:{email}`, the roster entry and every pending invite that member sent are deleted. A profile, password or reset save in flight therefore finishes first or finds nothing: it can never restore a removed login. The member's sessions stop at their next request, because `currentUser` finds no member record (and would refuse one off the roster anyway).
10. Leave (`POST /api/team/leave`). Member logins only (`team.leave`; the owner, a token and a founder get 403). The same deletion as a removal, then `destroySession()`.
11. Role change (`PATCH /api/team/member {email, role}`). Owner only (`team.role`). `admin` or `member` only; the owner role cannot be assigned, removed or transferred. Written under `lock:team:{owner}` and `lock:member:{email}`; the new role applies at the member's next request. Demoting an admin deletes the invites that admin sent, which could never be accepted afterwards yet would each hold a seat for up to 7 days.
12. Seats. The limit is `seatLimit(owner)` (`src/lib/pricing.ts` since 2026-09-30): `User.seats` when it is a positive number (floored), else `PLAN_SEATS[plan]` (trial 3, starter 1, pro 3, business 7), and Agency Business's 7 unless the founder sets `seats` for its contract; the owner included. A retired `enterprise` record (25 seats) reads as agency, so without a seat override it falls to 7: members above that stay enabled until the next plan or seat change runs `enforceSeats`, while new invites and accepts are refused. Seats used = 1 + members not disabled + unexpired invites. The billing page shows used of the limit to every role.
13. Seats after a plan change. `POST /api/admin/plan` (founder console) runs `enforceSeats(owner)` after any plan or `seats` change, up or down, under `lock:team:{owner}`, relative to who is active now: over the limit, the newest ENABLED members get `disabled: "seats"` (kept, never deleted) and every pending invite beyond the room left is cancelled, the newest first; under it, paused members come back oldest first, but only into seats no pending invite holds. Re-applying the same limit changes nothing (the team is never re-ranked as a whole, so a member the owner brought in after a downgrade is not swapped for one paused earlier). Removing a member does not bring a paused one back by itself; the next limit change does. A disabled member's login answers 403 `disabled` and its sessions stop at their next request. The response and the `admin:log` entry list who was paused and restored and how many invites were cancelled. Trial to Starter therefore pauses up to two members. No other code changes a plan today (`permissions.md` 5.32).
14. Account deletion (`DELETE /api/account`, owner only, or the console's `delete`). `wipeAccount` deletes the account's keys, `user:{owner}` included, and then runs `wipeTeam(owner)` under `lock:team:{owner}`: every member record of this account (each under its own `lock:member`), each member's `activebrand:`, every invite, `members:{owner}` and `invites:{owner}`. An accept that held the team lock first has its member deleted here; one that takes it afterwards finds no owner. A member login never survives to point at an address someone registers again, and a member record older than a re-registered owner is refused anyway (section 5).
15. Owner reset and founder reset both stamp `teamSessionsAfter` on the owner record, in the same locked save as the password, which signs every member out; each signs back in with its own password. A member's own reset or password change stamps only its own `sessionsAfter`. The owner's password change from Settings does not sign members out.
16. Shared and per login. Credits and the plan are the account's, so a member's generations spend the owner's balance under the same per-agent caps. The bell (`notifs:{owner}`) and its read state, the chat ceiling and analytics are per account. The active brand is per login: a member switching or adding a brand moves only `activebrand:{member}`, and a member's stored brand is used only while it is one of the owner's active brands; an admin archiving the brand the owner has open resets the owner's key too.
17. Pages by role. Owner-only and admin+ screens render read-only for lower roles with a one-line note: billing (no pay links, WhatsApp activation or Agency contact link; plan, credits, seats and the four plan cards for all), governance (its change history stays listed, without Restore; section 30), the scheduled tasks page ("Run now" stays), the brand file, brands (switching stays), integrations (requesting stays), MCP (no token or grant controls), refer (nothing read). The sidebar shows the acting person's name, email and role and a Members link for everyone; the trial banner and the limit dialog tell other roles to ask the owner. The working screens offer a plain member only what the server lets a member do: the inbox has no Approve or Publish for a member (an edit is saved without approving, and an approved item goes back to pending), and a chat card says an admin approves; the files page has no catalog import and no edit or delete on core memory; reports offer no CSV import or removal of imported results; the start page tells a member who sets the brand up (and the chat's first-run thread sends them there); the chat's WhatsApp card is shown only to the owner, whose phone it saves. A refused call says the role does not allow it, never "check your connection". The profile page hides the password and delete forms from a founder viewing the account (`GET /api/profile` returns `may`), and the MCP page tells a founder why there is no token.

### 36.1 Timed reminders
1. Setting. `remindAt` is one instant on an item: from the owner in the app (the add form's "Reminder" field and a row's "Reminder" panel, `remindAt` in the POST or PATCH body; null clears it) or from an agent (`remind_at` on `todo_create` / `todo_update`, `clear_remind: true`). Read in the brand's zone like a due date; a date alone is 08:00 local that day (`REMIND_DAY_AT`: at or before the 05:00 UTC daily run in every Gulf zone, so even the fallback fires it that morning); it must be in the future (`remind_past`, 400).
2. Index. `indexWrites` (every write path: create, change, the linked close, the fire) calls `dueWrites`: an open item with a reminder is in `sec:due:{scope}` at `remindAt` ms, and then its scope in `sec:duescopes` at that score with ZADD LT (a later reminder never raises the scope's score); any other item that had or has a reminder (done, deleted, cleared) is removed. The purge also removes it. A sweep never removes an id: it leaves when the item's own write clears `remindAt`.
3. The tick. An Upstash QStash schedule (created by the founder in the Upstash console, every 15 minutes) calls `POST /api/cron/secretary`. The route first refuses oversized input unread (a body over 64 KB, by its declared length or while reading: 413; an `Upstash-Signature` header over 4096 characters: 401), then accepts either the CRON_SECRET bearer (`sameSecret`) or a valid `Upstash-Signature` (`verifyQstash`: HS256 with the current or next signing key, `iss`, `sub`, `nbf`/`exp`, the body hash). Anything else is 401 and nothing runs; only those failed calls count against the per-IP limit (`allow`, 60 a minute, then 429), so callers sharing QStash's egress addresses can never make the real tick answer 429.
4. The sweep (`sweepReminders(now, {fromQstash, deadline})`): up to 200 scopes from `sec:duescopes` with score <= now, grouped by account, 8 accounts at a time. Per account the user and the brand list are read once: a missing account's scopes leave the index; a suspended account's scopes, and the scopes of a brand that is not active (archived), are looked at again 6 hours later with their reminders still due. Per scope (all of an account's at once, each isolated: `Promise.allSettled`, the zone read falls back to Riyadh) up to 50 due ids are read, and each is leased with `SET NX sec:firing:{scope}:{id}` for 330 s (longer than the longest caller, the daily cron's 300 s): one run at a time works on it, and the lease is released when the attempt ends (a killed run's expires). `fireReminder` re-reads the item under its lock (30 s) and fires only when it is open with a `remindAt` at or before now and still the instant the sweep read. Delivery comes first: one `todo` notification (href `/{lang}/app/todo`, fingerprint `todo-remind:{scope}:{id}:{remindAt ms}`), then, only when that push was new, one email when `RESEND_API_KEY` is set and the scope's email preference is not `none`; then the item: a `reminded` note («طلبت تذكيرك اليوم» / "You asked to be reminded"), `remindAt` cleared, `rev` bumped, then its index moves and the audit `remind` by `cron`. An item with no reminder due (closed, deleted, gone, cleared) leaves `sec:due`; one whose reminder moved is re-indexed at its new time; a busy lock leaves the id due. The scope is then rescored to its earliest remaining reminder (or removed), and read once more so a reminder added meanwhile is never dropped; a failed rescore is logged, not thrown.
5. Late, never lost. A failure anywhere (a store error, the bell write, the item write, a kill) leaves the id due and the next sweep retries it: a bell write that fails releases the fingerprints it claimed before the error goes on, and a retry after a run stopped between the delivery and the item's write finds the fingerprint claimed, so there is no second bell entry and no second email, and the item is written. Nothing is sent on WhatsApp or Telegram. The bell's list is written under the account's bell lock (section 36.7). A deadline bounds each run: no account starts and no lease is taken after it (the tick: 40 s after it started, of its 60 s; the daily route: 60 s after it started), and what is left stays due. `sendEmail` gives up after 10 s (false, logged as refused), so a hung send never holds a lease or a slot.
6. Honesty. A QStash call writes `sec:tick:last` (TTL 2 h); `timedReminders()` is true while it is under an hour old. Only then do tool answers say the reminder "comes at {time}" and the page show «تذكير: …» / "Reminder: …"; otherwise they say it arrives with the next morning brief after that time, at the brief's local time in the brand's zone (`briefClock`: 05:00 UTC plus the zone's offset, 08:00 in Riyadh, Kuwait, Doha and Manama, 09:00 in Dubai and Muscat; never a fixed "08:00"), and the page fills the same time into `remindBrief`, `timedOff` and its note. Fallback: the daily cron's first hop runs the sweep (not on chained hops, and without writing the tick), again while a sweep read a full 200 scopes (at most 4 more times, within its 60 s), and its time comes out of the batch's 240 s start budget; so without QStash a reminder is late, never lost.

Hijri dates in the brand's zone (rule 470). `hijriDate(d, zone)` (`seasons.ts`, one cached Umm al-Qura formatter per zone) gives the Hijri date on the brand's wall clock, so the Hijri day turns at the brand's midnight and not at the server's (UTC on Vercel). `activeSeasons(now, zone)`, `isRamadan(now, zone)`, the evening wrap's times and the calendar below all read it.
2. The settings (rule 474): `sec:cal:{scope}`, one `SecCalendar`, absent for every default: `weekend` (0 to 3 contiguous weekdays, Saturday to Sunday wraps; null for the zone's: Friday and Saturday, Saturday and Sunday in the UAE), `ramadanHours` (at most two "HH:mm" windows on the quarter hour, `from` before `to`, not overlapping; null for 10:00-15:00; `[]` for no restriction), `hijriFix` (per Hijri year, the owner's sighting: the local day Shawwal or Dhu al-Hijjah starts, within a day of Umm al-Qura's, for the current or the next Hijri year; a new `ramadan1` is `bad_request` and one stored before is dropped and never read, ruling R30), `holidays.add` and `holidays.remove` (at most 30 each: "MM-DD" every year, "YYYY-MM-DD" that day, "H-MM-DD" a Hijri day every year, "H-YYYY-MM-DD" that Hijri day; a new removal takes only days the defaults produce, and one already stored stays even when no default produces it any more, ruling R28), `updatedAt` and `updatedBy` (the acting login). `parseCalendar(body, now, zone, login, stored)` checks them field by field and refuses a field it does not know; stale values (a past Hijri year's fix, a dated value before today-30) are dropped, not refused, so settings read and saved back unchanged still save (ruling R8). Stored Ramadan hours are checked again when the calendar is built: hours that are not valid windows read as the default, so a hand-edited record cannot stop the agenda.
3. The calendar (rules 472-473). `workCalendar(cal, zone, now)` builds the `CalData` the agenda reads, for the brand's country (`countryOf(zone)`: Riyadh SA, Dubai AE, Kuwait KW, Doha QA, Manama BH, Muscat OM) from its `COUNTRY_CAL` row, each checked on 2026-10-02 against the source the row cites: the Eid spans (SA four days from the day after 29 Ramadan and four from Arafah; AE 30 Ramadan in a 30-day month to 3 Shawwal, and Arafah to 12 Dhu al-Hijjah; OM 29 Ramadan to 3 Shawwal and 9-12 Dhu al-Hijjah; KW 1-3 Shawwal and 9-12; BH 1-3 Shawwal and 10-12; QA three working days from 1 Shawwal and from 10 Dhu al-Hijjah), the other official private-sector holidays, and each law's days in lieu (SA a working day for each weekend day inside a holiday, an Eid's after it, a one-day holiday's on the nearer side of the weekend; QA the next working days; BH each Friday, and a holiday that falls on another; OM one day when an Eid's first day is a Friday and one for another holiday with weekend days in it; AE and KW none). Umm al-Qura is read for every local day from today-30 to today+400, and 35 days beyond on each side (501 days), so an Eid at an edge comes out whole; `hijriFix` moves its whole month (Shawwal or Dhu al-Hijjah; Ramadan always starts on Umm al-Qura's day, ruling R30). No Ramadan runs past 30 days (a corrected one starts later instead), and SA's and AE's Eid al-Fitr starts the day before the effective 1 Shawwal when Ramadan, counted from its Umm al-Qura start, ran 30 days or more, else on 1 Shawwal (ruling R26: Eid announced a day after a 29-day Ramadan leaves the Saudi days off where they were). Then the owner's removals (a removed day earns no day in lieu and never holds one; removing a day in lieu drops it), the days in lieu, and the owner's added days. Weekend days stay out of `quiet`, which gives each day's reason (`eid_fitr`, `eid_adha`, `holiday` with its name, `added`); `CalData` also holds the effective weekend and week start, the Ramadan days, the Ramadan hours and `briefAt` (05:00 UTC plus the zone's offset). The result is memoised (an LRU of 200 by zone, local day and a hash of the settings), so a save applies at the next read.
4. Where it is read (rules 471, 478). `calFor(scope, now)` reads the brand file, `sec:cal` and `sec:away` in one round. `agendaFor` (the page, `GET /api/secretary`, `agenda_get`, `agenda_next`, the brief, the alerts, the wrap), `createItem` and `changeItem` (a new chase clock, `expectBy`), the PATCH route (`parseOwnerChange`: "next week" starts on the brand's week start) and the chat and MCP tools compute on it, and `chat()` reads `sec:cal` for the NOW line's week start. Working days (`addWorkingDays`, `workingDaysBetween`) skip every quiet day, so Eid never counts toward a chase clock's two working days. A stored value the calendar cannot be built from gives the defaults, and only the error's name is logged (ruling R13). `GET /api/secretary` sends the agenda without the calendar and the away map (ruling R11).
5. The chase rule (rules 475-477): `chaseMoment(item, now, cal, away)` for every item someone else owes, starting from `chaseAt` (the due time, else `expectBy`, else two working days after creation; after a follow-up, two working days after it). An agent's item is never moved. A contact away on that day (the away entry of `partyKey(owedBy)`, its `until` on or after that day) is chased from `briefAt` on the first working day after the absence (ruling R10); a moment on a quiet day moves to `briefAt` on the next working day, and one in Ramadan's off-hours to the next window start that day, else to `briefAt` the next day: that is the anchor. Once it has passed, the chase still waits while today is quiet or outside Ramadan's hours. `classify` puts the item in `waiting` while its moment is ahead, even past its due date, then in `chase`, or `overdue` when the due time has passed. While it waits, the reason line (`nextStepParts`, `nextStepText`; ruling R9) says «أحمد: غياب لين الخميس 8 أكتوبر» / "أحمد: away until Thursday, October 8", «المتابعة الأحد 4 أكتوبر — عطلة نهاية الأسبوع» / "Follow up Sunday, October 4, the weekend" (or the holiday's own name), or «تابعه بعد 10:00 (ساعات رمضان)» / "Follow up after 10:00 (Ramadan hours)", in Latin digits; the page shows it under the WhatsApp link, and `agenda_get` after the next step, with the contact's name as a JSON string.
6. The brief and the wrap (rule 480). On a Ramadan day the morning brief counts a contact's debt held for Ramadan hours later today as a chase and says when (an agent's item keeps no Ramadan hours and waits, as on any day, ruling R16): «1 حان وقت متابعتها (اليوم بعد 10:00)» / "1 to follow up (today after 10:00)". The evening wrap lists a debt someone else owes as "fell overdue" when its chase moment's anchor passed since the previous wrap (ruling R12), and its "first for" list is the agenda at `firstWorkingMoment(09:00 tomorrow)`, which the line names: «غداً 9 ص» / "tomorrow at 9 AM", «الأحد 8 ص» / "Sunday at 8 AM" after a Riyadh Thursday, «غداً 10 ص» in Ramadan (ruling R14).
7. Alerts (rules 479-480). `overdueTodoAlerts` passes the overdue, chase and waiting entries to `overdueAlerts(entries, now, zone, cal)`. The owner's own items: only when overdue, calendar days from the due time, any day, the marker as before. An agent's: once its chase moment has passed, calendar days from its anchor, any day (ruling R16). A contact's: once its anchor has passed, or while it is held for Ramadan hours later today (ruling R29: the run is once a day, so a contact's debt chaseable later today, even one due at 14:00 inside the window, is alerted at the morning run with its time; only a contact's: an agent's item is alerted once it is due, ruling R16); the steps (0, 1, 3, 7) count working days from the anchor's local day, and an alert is sent only on a working day when the agenda says chase or the debt is held for Ramadan hours («تابعه اليوم بعد 10:00» / "Follow up today after 10:00"); otherwise the step holds: nothing is sent and its marker is set again (SET XX, 7 days), so no step is sent twice across a long Eid (ruling R15). The marker is `notifseen:{email}:todo-due:{scope}:{id}:{anchor ms}:{step}`. The first alert sent after a contact's absence ended in the last 7 days, for an item that existed by its last day, says «انتهى غياب {name} — وقت المتابعة» / "{name} is back: time to follow up", once per absence (`todo-back:{scope}:{id}:{until}`, SET NX, 7 days). At most 5 alerts a brand a local day, as before. The calendar reaches 30 days back (ruling R17), so an anchor an Eid moved stays moved through step 7, unless the brand added more than about 21 quiet days to that month (not pinned). Known edge case (ruling R31): when every Ramadan window ends before the morning run, contacts' steps hold for the whole of Ramadan.
8. Contacts who are away (rules 481-483): `sec:away:{scope}`, one field per contact, `partyKey(label)` («أحمد», «احمد» and «إحمد» are one), holding `{label, until, note?, by, via, at}` with `until` the last away day, local and inclusive. `PUT /api/secretary/away {label, until, note?}` marks a contact away or replaces the entry, and `DELETE {label}` ends the absence now (`until` becomes yesterday, so the back alert follows; ending it again changes nothing, ruling R21) or, with `remove: true`, deletes the entry. Both need `todo.write` (any member) and the `sec:write` rate, run under `withLock(scope, "away")` and leave one `away` or `away_end` audit line with the login, the label and the day, never the note. Refused: the owner, an owner alias or an agent (`not_contact`), a last day before today or past today + 366 (`away_range`), a 201st field, ended ones counted (`away_limit`), a label whose key the store cannot hold (`bad_request`, ruling R22). `GET` lists every stored entry, ended ones marked, so the owner sees what holds the cap (ruling R20). The daily job removes entries ended over 30 days ago (`cleanAway`, ruling R19); the cron's outcome line counts them, `todo:{purged}p{closed}c{awayCleaned}a`. The page's «الغائبون» / "Away" panel lists them with "End now" or "Remove", its name field suggests the open items' contacts, and a contact's row offers «غائب حتى…» / "Away until…" with the name filled in.
9. Reem's away cards (rules 485-486). When the owner says in chat that a contact is away until a day, or is back, Reem calls `contact_away {name, until}` or `{name, clear: true}`. It writes nothing to the away list: `proposeAway` checks it as `PUT` or `DELETE` would, reads the away list and the open items in one round, and saves a proposal (`secawayprop:{scope}:{id}`, 7 days; its id first in `secawayprops:{scope}`) with the name as typed, the contact it matches, the day, and how many open items that contact owes. The reply shows it as a card (at most 5 a reply, `AWAY_CARDS_PER_REPLY`: a sixth `contact_away` call in the turn is refused); a person with `todo.write` presses Save or Dismiss (`POST /api/secretary/away/proposals/{id}`, the id checked with `AWAY_PROPOSAL_ID`), once: the outcome `secawayprop-done:{scope}:{id}` is claimed with SET NX, and Save runs `setAway` or `endAway` again with every check, its audit line naming the login and ending "proposed in Reem's chat ({id})"; a Save the checks refuse frees the claim; while a Save runs, another Save or a Dismiss is 409 `busy` and the card asks to try again (a decided card is 409 `already_decided`). A reloaded thread shows each card's state (open, saved, dismissed, expired). The tool is offered only in Reem's chat turns, and `runTool` refuses it to any other agent, a turn with no agent, MCP and a token; MCP never lists it.
10. The Working days panel and route (rules 484, 487). `GET /api/secretary/calendar` (any member, `sec:read`) and `PUT` (admin+, `brand.write`, 30 an hour, under `withLock(scope, "cal")`, where it reads the stored settings so the removals they hold are kept, ruling R28, one `calendar` audit line) answer `{ok, calendar, effective, upcoming, eids}`: the saved settings or null, what is in force (`effective`: the country, the zone, the weekend and the zone's own default weekend, the week start, the Ramadan hours; ruling R23), the next 365 days off with their names and the next Ramadan, and this and next Hijri year's Eid with the `hijriFix` value each "a day earlier / later" button saves and its days off. The page's «أيام العمل» / "Working days" panel renders the same view, from the records the page's agenda was read with (`todoPage`: the brand file, `sec:cal` and `sec:away` are read once a render): the weekend with the country's default marked (and, for a brand whose city names Sharjah, that Sharjah's government weekend includes Friday), the Ramadan hours, each Eid with its days off and its buttons (a Saudi brand is told the Eid al-Fitr holiday starts the day after 29 Ramadan, so its days off may not move when Eid is a day later, ruling R26), the next 12 months of days off with a switch each, the added days and the days turned off (a stored removal no default produces any more included, so it can be turned on), the forms to add one, all labelled «متوقعة — تأكد من الإعلان الرسمي» / "Expected; check the official announcement", in Latin digits. An admin edits; a member reads. Each save is the whole settings object with one change, one request at a time. None of it can be changed from chat or MCP.
11. Account deletion (rules 478, 485, 489): `secretaryKeys` lists `sec:cal`, `sec:away`, each away proposal's record and outcome by the ids in `secawayprops:{scope}`, and that index, for every brand scope. The `todo-due` and `todo-back` markers expire within seven days.
12. Maintenance, each January: Oman's announced holidays. Oman's Council of Ministers announces each year the days its official holidays are actually taken, and they replace the decree's dates for that year. When the list is out, add the year to `COUNTRY_CAL.OM.omanYear` in `src/lib/secretary-calendar.ts` (each "MM-DD" with the holiday id it stands for), update that row's `source` line with the announcement and the day it was read, add the new year's case to the rule 473 tests and row 473 of `tests.md`, and merge it like any change. Without it, the decree's dates apply that year (Accession Day 01-11, 1 Muharram, 12 Rabi al-Awwal, 27 Rajab, National Day 11-20 and 11-21), with the decree's day in lieu. A brand can also correct a single day itself on the Working days panel.

## 40. Reem's recurring items and the deletion of their closed copies (phase 3 A2, 2026-10-03)

Spec `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-02-secretary-reem-phase3a-design.md` sections 4-6, as amended on 2026-10-03 (rulings R32-R54 of `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-03-reem-phase3a2-recurring-purge.md`). Files: `src/lib/secretary.ts` (`parseRepeat`, `settleRepeat`, `spawnNext`, `deleteNextCopy`, `changeItem`, `closeLinked`, `indexWrites`, the tools, `todoPage`), `src/lib/secretary-agenda.ts` (`SecRepeat`, `gregorianNext`, `repeatText`, `resultText`), `src/lib/secretary-calendar.ts` (`nextOccurrence`), `src/lib/secretary-series.ts` (warn, purge, keep, the series rows; server only), `src/lib/chat.ts`, the routes `api/secretary/series/[seriesId]/keep` and `export`, the page `[lang]/app/todo` (`TodoPanel.tsx`, `SeriesPanel.tsx`) and the daily cron. Tests: rules 490-509 of `tests.md` (`tests/secretary-repeat.test.ts`, `tests/secretary-series.test.ts`). No credits, no model call, no new environment variable. Every key is built from the caller's scope.

1. The rule. An item with a due date can repeat: `repeat` is a `SecRepeat` with `every` (day, workday, week, month, year, hmonth, hyear), `interval` (1 to 99; a workday rule is always 1; a Hijri year at most 10), `weekdays` (week), `monthDay` (1 to 31 or -1 for the last day; 1 to 30 or -1 for a Hijri month), `remindBefore` (0 to 10080 minutes), `until` (a local day, inclusive) and `count` (1 to 999 copies in all). The code adds the `anchor` (the local day of the item's due date when the rule was set), the brand's `weekStart` and, for a timed item, `time` (R36): a copy is built from the rule's time, and an all-day item stays all-day. `parseRepeat` checks every range; a bad value is 400 `repeat_invalid`, and an update that leaves a repeating item with no due date is 400 `repeat_needs_due` (R38). An `until` before the item's own due day is 400 `repeat_until_before_due` (field `repeat`), on create, update and reschedule: the series would end before it began. Setting a rule on an item with no date gives it the first date the rule produces from today. The first time a rule is set the item gets a series `{id, n: 1}` (R42), which it keeps after the repeat is stopped, so its history stays exportable.
2. The next date. `nextOccurrence(rule, prevDue, now, zone, cal, n)` returns the first date on the rule's grid (counted from the anchor, a day 31 clamped to a short month per candidate) that is after the copy being closed and not in the past, so a missed date never piles up: a timed copy must be after now, an all-day copy on today or later. A workday rule skips the brand's quiet days (section 39); an `hmonth` or `hyear` rule counts Hijri months on the calendar that includes the owner's `hijriFix`, reading the anchor's Hijri day by pure Umm al-Qura. It returns null, and the series ends, when `n` has reached `count`, the date is after `until`, or the bounded search finds nothing.
3. Setting and stopping. The owner sets a rule on the To-do page (the Repeat panel sends the whole rule with `PATCH {repeat, rev}`), Reem in chat and the owner's MCP assistant with `repeat` on `todo_create` and `todo_update`. The tool schema is strict-safe (R45: a nested object with `required: ["every"]`, plain integers, no `minimum` or `maximum`; every range check is in `parseRepeat`). `runSecretaryTool` refuses `repeat` and `stop_repeat` from any agent but Reem and from any chat call with no agent, before the allowance is spent (R46); MCP is allowed. Stopping (`repeat: null`, `stop_repeat`), ending with a `count` not above the copies made or an `until` before the next date, and `skip` are the owner's in the app: Reem and MCP get `owner_only` (403, field `repeat`). A reminder set on a repeating item is stored in the rule as `remindBefore` only when it is 0 to 10080 minutes before the due date (R37); otherwise it applies to that copy only. Editing the time of day of a copy through an update (not the reschedule form) moves the rule's time too, so the next copy keeps it (R53); rescheduling moves one copy only.
4. Closing a copy opens the next. `complete`, `confirm_done`, `skip` (owner, admin+, `todo.close`; the result is the constant `skipped`, shown as a word in the page, the CSV and the tool answers through `resultText`) and the linked close (an approved inbox item closes its linked copy, `closeLinked`) all end in `spawnNext`, under the closed copy's item lock. It claims the id `sec:spawn:{scope}:{closedId}` (SET NX, 2 days; a retry reuses the claimed id), writes the closed copy first with `nextId`, then the new copy (R51): its title, details, kind, direction, party, phone, priority, critical flag and rule; the date and time from the rule; its own reminder when `remindBefore` leaves it in the future; rev 1, the same source, series `n` plus one; no links, notes, result or proposal. A crash between the two writes leaves a done copy whose `nextId` names a missing record, and the owner's retried close finishes the spawn and answers with the closed copy; a failed second write with no retry stops that series until the owner closes the copy again (accepted, no repair sweep). A spawn skips the open cap and the create allowance (the open count is unchanged) and the range check on its date (R40; rescheduling a copy keeps it). Every close writes `closedBy` (who closed it: the login, or the actor).
5. Reopen (R32, R34, R48, R49, R52). Reopening a closed copy that opened a next copy deletes that copy through `deleteNextCopy` (a soft delete with the usual 30-day restore, audit `series_reopen` by the rule; since 2026-10-05 a call of `removeBySystem`, section 42), but only while it is untouched (R35: open, with no note except its own reminders, `rev` equal to 1 plus the reminders that fired). `nextId` and the claim are cleared only when the next copy was really deleted, or is missing or soft-deleted; when it was kept (edited, or already closed) they stay, so closing the reopened copy again opens nothing. The reopen answers 503 `lock_timeout` with nothing written when the next copy's lock times out or a read fails. When another copy of the series is open afterwards (the edited next copy, or one further on) the reopened copy gets the `two_open` note «النسخة التالية مفتوحة ومعدّلة — صار عندك نسختان» / "The next copy is open and was edited; there are now two". A reopen that deletes an open next copy is not counted against the open cap.
6. The series' indexes. A closed copy of a series is in `sec:series:{scope}:{seriesId}` (score = close time; the series view and the CSV read it), the series is in `sec:serieslist`, and the copy is in `sec:sdone` (the warn window) unless the series is kept (R43). `changeItem` and `closeLinked` read `sec:serieskeep` (`SISMEMBER`, under the lock, only when a copy of a series enters done) and pass `kept` to `indexWrites`. Leaving done (reopen, delete) removes the copy from all three; a restore puts it back.
7. The daily step (`secretaryDaily`, after the existing purge and away clean-up, only for an active brand of an active account; each step's failure is logged by name and retried the next day). First `purgeSeries`: for each copy in `sec:spurge` whose score is due (at most 200 a run, 8 at a time, each under its item lock) it reads the copy again and deletes it only while it is still closed, has a series that is not kept, is in `sec:spurge` with a due score, and was closed at least 90 days ago; one that is no longer eligible only leaves the window (closed again, it goes back to `sec:sdone`). One `series_purge` audit line carries the count, and the daily cron's outcome reads `todo:{p}p{c}c{a}a{w}w{d}d`. Then `requeueSeries` puts back into `sec:sdone` a closed copy of an unkept series that is in neither window (a crash, or an un-keep that died half-way) and drops ids with no record. Then `warnSeries`: the copies in `sec:sdone` within 7 days of their 90-day mark (at most 200 a run). One notification per brand and local day names at most 5 series, with how many copies of each and the date from which they are deleted, and "and k more" for the rest; its text is in the owner's language and ends with what they can do (keep the history, or download it). The email follows unless the owner's email preference is none. Only after the notification was pushed do the named series get a marker `sec:swarn:{scope}:{seriesId}` (30 days) and their copies move to `sec:spurge` with the score `max(closed + 90 days, now + 7 days)` (R44): a rerun the same day (the day's fingerprint is claimed) and a failed push move nothing. A series with a live marker is not named again: its later copies move silently to `max(closed + 90 days, warned + 7 days, now + 7 days)`, so a daily series does not ring the bell every day, and the 7 days between a warning that named a series and the deletion of any of its copies hold. When the marker lapses the series is named again, about monthly for a daily series. Ids that cannot be warned about (no record, not closed, no series, a kept series) leave `sec:sdone` as they are met (R50). Deletion reads only `sec:spurge`, so a failed warning never lets a deletion through.
8. Keep. `PUT /api/secretary/series/{seriesId}/keep {keep}` (permissions row 138): `keep: true` (any member with `todo.write`) adds the id to `sec:serieskeep` (at most 500 a brand, 409 `keep_limit`), takes the series' copies out of both windows and deletes the marker; `keep: false` (admin and the owner, `todo.close`) puts them back into `sec:sdone`, so they are warned about again before any deletion. A series that was never warned about is kept without anything else changing. The page's series view (`/{lang}/app/todo?series={seriesId}`, linked from the notification and from every closed copy's History) shows the series' latest 200 closed copies, a Keep history switch, a Download CSV link and a not-found state for an id that is not the brand's.
9. Export. `GET /api/secretary/series/{seriesId}/export[?lang=en]` (permissions row 139): a CSV (byte order mark written as an escape, CRLF) of title, due, closed at, result and closed by, newest close first, up to 5000 rows and a final "truncated" row beyond that. The result column shows `skipped` as a word in the page's language, and every cell goes through `csvSafe`.
10. What the customer sees. Repeat, Skip and Stop repeat on a to-do (a member sees Repeat and Stop repeat; Skip is admin and owner only), a badge naming the rule in words (`repeatText`, for example «يتكرر كل يوم عمل» / "Repeats every working day"), a note under the reschedule form saying a reschedule moves this copy only, History on a closed copy, and the series view. The keep switch's hint says closed copies are deleted some time after they close and that the owner is warned first; the words do not name a number of days. Reem's playbook (`content/skills/secretary.md`), the help page and the MCP page describe `repeat` and `stop_repeat` in the same terms.
11. Account deletion (`secretaryKeys`): lists `sec:serieslist`, `sec:sdone`, `sec:spurge`, `sec:serieskeep`, each listed series' `sec:series` and `sec:swarn`, and `sec:spawn` for every id in `sec:all`, for every brand scope. `purgeExpired` (the 30-day purge of soft-deleted items) also takes a deleted closed copy out of the series sets and removes an emptied series from `sec:serieslist` with its marker.
12. Known limits (accepted for A2): a restore of a deleted next copy can leave two open copies without the `two_open` note; a stale close of an old closed copy after its series ended and was purged can open a new copy; an owner's re-close of a done copy whose next copy was deliberately deleted opens a new copy; the series view and the CSV read the whole series set on each request, and `requeueSeries` reads every unkept series set once a day (fine for hundreds of series, to be capped or made weekly for thousands); the warning counts copies of the first 200 due rows only.

## 41. Reem's calendar subscription feed (phase 3 B1, 2026-10-05)

Spec `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-05-secretary-reem-b1-ics-feed-design.md` (rulings B1-R1 to B1-R14); plan `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-05-reem-b1-ics-feed.md`. Files: `src/lib/feed-token.ts` (token, records, revoke; imports `./db`, `./parallel` and `node:crypto` only, so `team.ts` and `account.ts` can revoke), `src/lib/secretary-feed.ts` (`feedHolds`, `feedItems`, `buildIcs`, `icsEscape`, `icsFold`), `src/app/api/secretary/feed/route.ts` (management), `src/app/api/secretary/feed/[token]/route.ts` (the public file), `src/components/app/FeedCard.tsx`. No new environment variable: the link's origin is `appOrigin()` (`src/lib/origin.ts`), never the request's URL or `Host` header. Rows 140-143 of `permissions.md`, rules 530-539, 577-585 of `tests.md`.

1. What it is. Each login (an owner, an admin or a member) can make one private link, add it once to a calendar app (iPhone, Google, Outlook) and see its own open, dated items there. One direction only: nothing a calendar app does changes an item. The link is bound to the brand that was active when it was made (`sc(u)` at creation) and is never a brand-wide link.
2. The token. 32 random bytes in base64url, 43 characters (`FEED_TOKEN`, `^[A-Za-z0-9_-]{43}$`). Only its SHA-256 in hex is stored; the plain token exists in the one answer that creates it and is never stored or logged, so a lost link is renewed, not recovered. Keys: `sec:feed:{hash}` holds `{login, scope, lang, createdAt}`; `sec:feedof:{login}` holds the hash of that login's current link, and a record that `sec:feedof` does not name is dead (`resolveFeed` returns null for it, which closes the race of a revoke that gave up waiting for the lock while a create was still running); `lock:feed:{login}` (10 s) serialises create and revoke. No expiry.
3. Making and stopping it. `POST /api/secretary/feed {lang?}` (`feed.manage`) creates or renews: a held lock answers 409 `busy`, otherwise the new record and `sec:feedof` are written and the old record deleted, and the answer holds `{ok, url, webcal}` with `Cache-Control: no-store`. `lang` is only `ar` or `en`; anything else is the acting login's own language, and the record is written for the acting login and `sc(u)`, never a value from the body. `DELETE` stops the link and is idempotent. `revokeFeed` takes the lock with up to 20 tries 50 ms apart and then revokes anyway: a held lock never keeps a link alive (B1-R10). `GET /api/secretary/feed` answers `{ok, exists, createdAt, sameBrand}` and never the link, its hash or its scope: `sameBrand` is false when the stored link was made in another brand than the one `sc(u)` shows (B1-R16). POST and DELETE share one bucket per login (`sec:feed:{login}`, 20 an hour, 429 `too_many`); the status read has its own, `sec:read:{login}` (`SEC_READS_PER_HOUR`), so opening My to-dos never spends the writes. A founder impersonating an account cannot create, renew or stop its feed (`feed.manage`, `IMPERSONATION_BLOCKED`); the status read is allowed.
4. The public file. `GET /api/secretary/feed/{token}` (one trailing `.ics` is stripped) has no session; the token is the credential. In order: the shape check (a malformed value is a 404 with no store read and no rate count); the IP limit `feed:ip:{ip}`, 120 an hour, before any store read; `resolveFeed` (the record, and `sec:feedof` must name its hash); the link limit `feed:t:{hash}`, 60 an hour; `feedHolds`; then the items read with the record's own scope (`rec.scope`), never a value from the request. Every failure is the same empty 404 (no body, no hint which). Two refusals are not 404: the IP limit and the per-link limit answer 429 with `Retry-After: 3600`. The per-link 429 shows a holder of a live token that the record exists: a named, accepted exception (spec B1-R6; section 6 of the spec and `permissions.md`, "Calendar feed").
5. What `feedHolds` checks on every fetch (the session-free half of `resolveSession`, so a login that no longer belongs to the brand stops getting the file). The login exists (`getLogin`). An owner is not suspended. A member is on the owner's roster (`memberOf` set and not the member's own address), not disabled, an admin or a member, the owner exists and is not suspended, and the member's `acceptedAt` is not before the owner's `createdAt`. `rec.scope` is one of the owner's brands (`scopeFor`, compared with the owner's stored email exactly) and that brand is active (`listBrands`). The record must not be older than the login's life: a member's `acceptedAt`, an owner's `createdAt`, so a link that outlived a failed revoke does not come back when the address is taken again (B1-R14). Nor older than the login's last password event (B1-R15): the owner's `sessionsAfter`; for a member, the member's `sessionsAfter` and the owner's `teamSessionsAfter`. A password change, reset or founder reset stamps these, so a revoke that failed after the password was saved cannot leave a link working; an owner's password change stamps only the owner's `sessionsAfter`, so members' links stay, while an owner reset or founder reset also stamps `teamSessionsAfter`. A missing or unparsable date on either side is not a reason to refuse. Suspension and a paused seat do not delete the record: the file is refused at read time and comes back when the login is active again.
6. The content window. The brand's open items that have a due date, read through `listOpenItems` (the same set the to-do page shows any member), due from 14 days before now to 365 days after, nearest first (then by id), at most 500 events with no marker beyond that. Done, deleted and undated items are not in it, and neither are items snoozed now (`isSnoozed`, the predicate the to-do page's `classify` uses; a proposed-done item is never snoozed; B1-R17). A recurring series shows only its one open copy, as a plain event: no `RRULE`, no `VALARM`, so Hijri and "every N" rules stay Reem's. A due date outside the window is simply not in the file.
7. The event. `UID` is `{itemId}@thakeen`; `SEQUENCE` is the item's `rev`; `DTSTAMP` and `LAST-MODIFIED` are `updatedAt` (falling back to the due time, then to now, when it does not parse); `STATUS:CONFIRMED`. `SUMMARY` is the title. `DESCRIPTION` is one line, who acts and the app link: `todo.feed.mine` (عليّ أنا / Mine) or `todo.feed.waiting` with the party's name (ننتظر {name} / Waiting on {name}), then ` - ` and `{origin}/{lang}/app/todo`; `URL` is that to-do page. A timed item is `DTSTART` in UTC (`...Z`) with `DTEND` 30 minutes later. An all-day item is `DTSTART;VALUE=DATE` on its local day in the brand's zone with `DTEND` the next day, so an item due on 5 October in Riyadh is `20261005`. No note, description, phone number or inbox link of an item is read into the file. Every text value is cleaned (line breaks become a space; control and direction-override characters removed) and escaped (`\`, `;`, `,`), lines are folded at 75 octets without splitting a UTF-8 character, and lines end with CRLF, so a title can never add a line or a property.
8. The header and the headers. `X-WR-CALNAME` is "ذكين: مهامي" or "Thakeen: my to-dos" in the language stored with the link; `REFRESH-INTERVAL;VALUE=DURATION:PT1H` and `X-PUBLISHED-TTL:PT1H` are hints only: calendar apps poll on their own schedule and may be late, and the card says so. The response is `text/calendar; charset=utf-8`, `Cache-Control: private, max-age=300`, `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`, `X-Robots-Tag: noindex, nofollow`, and sets no cookie.
9. Where a link ends. Stop and Renew delete the old record at once. `dropMember` (removal by the owner or the founder, and a member leaving), `wipeTeam` and `wipeAccount` revoke the member's or the account's links. A member's own password reset or change revokes that member's link only. An owner's password reset (`/api/auth/reset`) and the founder's reset of an owner (`/api/admin/users/[email]`) revoke the owner's link and every roster member's, since they sign everyone out; an owner's password change revokes only the owner's (members keep their sessions and their links). The read-time stamp check of item 5 ends the same links when the revoke call itself fails. Suspend and seat pause delete nothing (item 5). A role change needs nothing: the feed asks for no more than `read`, and `feedHolds` runs on every fetch.
10. The card. «اشترك من تقويمك» / "Subscribe from your calendar" on My to-dos (`/{lang}/app/todo`, `TodoPanel`, below the Away panel; B1-R9: not the publishing calendar page, which is about approved posts), for every role. The status loads from `GET /api/secretary/feed`, and a failed read shows the reason with "Try again". No link yet: one "Create my link" button. After creating or renewing: the https link and its `webcal://` twin once, each with a Copy button, one line per app (iPhone: Settings > Apps > Calendar > Calendar Accounts > Add Account > Other > Add Subscribed Calendar; Google: Other calendars > From URL, which does not accept `webcal://`; Outlook: Add calendar > Subscribe from web), and a warning that anyone with the link sees the titles of the dated tasks and that it should be renewed if shared by mistake. The link lives only in the component's state: not in local storage, not in the URL, not in the console, and gone when the page is left. After that the card says a link exists since the date, with Renew and Stop (Stop asks first); when the link was made in another brand than the one shown it adds one line, "made in another brand; renew to move it here" (`todo.feed.otherBrand`), since Renew moves the link to the brand shown now. Words are in `appDict[lang].todo.feed`, in both languages, digits Latin.
11. Known limits (accepted for B1): the platform's own request logs (Vercel) record paths, so the token appears there; renew the link if it was shared by mistake (`permissions.md`). Calendar fetchers that share addresses (Google's) could reach 120 an hour for one address at scale and see 429 (B1-R6, tracked). A corrupt member record or a store error on the public route is a 500, not the empty 404. A create that stalls past a revoke's 20 tries and then writes after the revoke leaves a live link that `sec:feedof` names (B1-R10, B1-R18); `feedHolds` still kills it for a removed or suspended login and, once a password event stamped later than the record, for that too. A link is tied to the host (`appOrigin()`) in force when it was made: if the product moves, the old host must keep serving the feed, not redirect (B1-R18). A calendar app shows only the next copy of a series, never future ones.

## 42. Bookings in from Cal.com and Calendly (phase 3 B2a, 2026-10-05)

Spec `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-05-secretary-reem-b2-bookings-design.md` sections 1-2 and its rulings. Files: `src/lib/booking-token.ts` (the booking link: token, record, signing key), `src/lib/secretary-booking.ts` (signature checks, parsers, `applyBooking`), `src/lib/secretary.ts` (`removeBySystem`, the `bookClaim` and `bookIds` keys, `secretaryKeys`). Rules 586-599 of `tests.md`. No credits, no model call. Items 1-8 describe what happens once a delivery is verified and parsed (the webhook route, `permissions.md` row 144); items 9-10 are the owner's management of the links, item 11 where a link ends and item 12 the card.

1. Scope. Every key and every item write uses the link record's `scope` and `provider` (`BookRecord`, resolved from the token's hash), never a value from the payload. The payload gives only the external ids, the guest's name, the event-type name and the start and end instants. The account whose allowance a creation spends is the scope's owner (`scope` up to `#`).
2. Parsing. `parseCalcom` and `parseCalendly` turn a delivery into `create`, `move` (with `oldExternalId`), `cancel` (the id only; its start and end are never read) or `ignore`. An instant must carry `Z` or an offset; one without is `ignore`, so nothing is read in the server's zone. A Calendly `invitee.created` with `old_invitee` is a `move`; the old invitee's `invitee.canceled` with `rescheduled: true` is `ignore`.
3. Create. A start more than a day back or more than 366 days ahead is `noop`. The index `sec:bookext:{scope}` gets `{provider}:{externalId}`, then the claim `sec:book:ext:{scope}:{provider}:{externalId}` is taken with SET NX for 400 days, its value a new item id (`newSecId`, the `sec:spawn` pattern). A delivery that finds the claim taken (a replay, a concurrent copy, a retry after a run that died) continues with the id the claim names; a claim marked `cancelled` (item 5) creates nothing. Then, under that item's lock: the claim must still name the id, and when the record exists nothing is created (`noop`; its open indexes are written again, `reindexOpen`, in case the earlier run died between the record and its indexes). Otherwise the link's own cap `book:new:{hash}` (60 a day, before `createItem`, so the 61st spends none of the owner's allowance), then `createItem` at that id (`opts.id`) as `{actor: "rule", via: "cron", source: "booking"}` in the brand's zone and Working days, where the owner's 200 creations a day and 500 open items still apply. The item: a task the owner owes, titled «اجتماع مع {الاسم}» / "Meeting with {name}" in the link's language (an empty name is «ضيف» / "a guest"), the guest as `owedTo` unless the name reads as the owner or an agent, the event-type name and «حُجز عبر Cal.com» / "booked via Calendly" as the description, due at the start (the brand zone's local time), reminded at the end only while the end is ahead. Name and event-type name go through `cleanLine` (one line; controls and direction overrides removed). No email, phone, link, note or reason is stored. A refusal (a cap or a validation) deletes the claim so a later delivery can try again; a lock timeout, a 503 or a throw keeps it, the answer is `busy`, and the provider's retry finishes the creation at the same id. So one booking makes at most one item, and a retry is never dropped.
4. Move. The claim of the previous id is read first, then this id's (one round trip). The item it names is updated as the rule (`changeItem`: due and reminder; a reminder that would be past is cleared) under its lock, and this id's claim is taken with SET NX for the same item (a claim that names another item is never overwritten), so a later cancel or move under it finds the same item. A claimed id with no record (a creation that died or is still running, or an item purged after its 30 days) is written at that id with the new times, as item 3; when it turns out to exist by then, the move is applied to it once more. Neither id names an item: the move creates, as item 3, and both ids are claimed for the one item (a previous id marked `cancelled` keeps its mark: Cal.com's "request reschedule" cancels the old booking first and the guest's new booking then names it, so the new booking is created). This id marked `cancelled`: `noop`. An item the owner completed is left as it is (`noop`). An item that was removed (the old booking's cancel came first, as in that same Cal.com flow, or the owner deleted it) stays removed, and the new id gets a fresh item as item 3 under its own claim, so a replay of the move finds that claim and makes no second item. A replayed move is applied again: a Calendly one is refused by the signature after 5 minutes, but a Cal.com reschedule carries no timestamp, so a replay can move the item back to that delivery's time.
5. Cancel. The claim names the item; `removeBySystem(scope, id, rule/cron, "booking_cancel", only open)` soft-deletes it under its lock (30-day restore, audit line, out of the open and due sets). When the claimed item was never written, the claim is marked `cancelled` under that same lock (`missing`), so a creation still to run finds it and creates nothing. A booking never seen (no claim) gets its claim taken as `cancelled` (indexed, SET NX, 400 days), so a create retried or delivered after the cancel creates nothing; a creation that took the claim in between is removed as above. All `noop`, as is a closed or already removed item. `removeBySystem` is the system's delete: the owner-only check (`OWNER_ONLY`) applies to people and tools, not to code acting on a verified delivery; a series reopen uses it too (section 40 item 5).
6. Results. `created`, `moved`, `removed`, `noop`, or `busy` when an item lock timed out or the store failed; the route answers `busy` with 503 so the provider retries. The route's other answers (an empty 404 up to and including a bad signature, 429, 400, 500; with 503 also for a store failure before the signature) are in `permissions.md` row 144 and "Booking webhook" item 4.
7. Account deletion. `secretaryKeys` reads `sec:bookext:{scope}` and lists every claim it names and the index itself, for every brand scope.
8. Known limits (accepted for B2a): a creation that dies after the item's record and indexes but before its audit line leaves the item without its `create` audit line (the retry finds the item and writes none); a cancel that arrives under a different id than the one a running creation claimed (for example the previous id of a move still being created) does not stop that creation; the link's daily counter is spent even when `createItem` then refuses.
9. Managing the links (`src/app/api/secretary/booking/route.ts`, `permissions.md` rows 145-147, rules 596-597). The owner, an admin or a member opens the links card and manages their own link per provider (`calcom`, `calendly`; one live link per login per provider). The record names the acting login and `sc(u)`, never a request value. Status (`GET`, `read`) lists `{provider, createdAt, sameBrand}` and nothing else: never the link, key, hash or scope. Cal.com: `POST {provider: "calcom", lang?}` mints the link (`createBook`, replacing the old one) and answers `{url, secret}` once, `Cache-Control: no-store`; the owner pastes the url as the webhook address and `secret` (the derived signing key `bookingKey(hash)`, never stored) as its secret. `DELETE ?provider=calcom|calendly` stops one provider's link, idempotently (a stopped link gives the webhook's empty 404); a missing or unknown provider is 400. Writes (POST and DELETE) share one bucket of 20 an hour per login; a founder impersonating gets 403 (`booking.manage`).
10. Calendly setup (`calendlySubscribe`, `src/lib/secretary-booking.ts`). Calendly has no paste-a-URL form on the plans that allow webhooks, so the owner pastes a personal access token and the app subscribes for them: `POST {provider: "calendly", token}` (a non-empty string of at most 2,000 characters, else 400 `bad_token`, before anything is made). The record is created first (`createBook`), so the address Calendly will call (`{appOrigin()}/api/secretary/booking/{token}`) and the signing key (`bookingKey(hash)`) are the real ones; then, through `safeFetch` (https only), `GET https://api.calendly.com/users/me` for `resource.uri` and `resource.current_organization` (Calendly's subscription call requires `organization` even for `scope: "user"`) and `POST https://api.calendly.com/webhook_subscriptions` with `{url, events: ["invitee.created", "invitee.canceled"], organization, user, scope: "user", signing_key}`; the `signing_key` sent is the key `verifyCalendlySignature` later checks. On any failure the record is revoked (`revokeBook(login, "calendly")`), so a failed setup leaves no link, and the answer is `422` with a typed error: `calendly_plan` when the subscription is refused with 402 or 403 (webhooks need Calendly's Standard plan or above), otherwise `calendly_rejected` (a 401 or 400, a malformed answer, a 5xx, a network error or the 10-second timeout). Success answers `{ok: true}` only. The token is used in memory for those two calls and appears nowhere else: never stored, logged, put in a URL, returned in an answer or carried by an error (the catch never reads the thrown message). A held create lock is 409 `busy` with no Calendly call.
11. Where a booking link ends (rule 598). Stop and Renew delete the old record; every other end is the calendar feed's (item 9 of its section) with `revokeBook`/`revokeBooks` beside `revokeFeed`/`revokeFeeds` in the same `Promise.all` at each call site, for every provider of the login. `dropMember` (removal by the owner or the founder, and a member leaving), `wipeTeam` and `wipeAccount` (first step, and again at its end for the owner) delete the member's or the account's link records and pointers (`sec:book:{hash}`, `sec:bookof:{login}:{provider}`). A member's own password reset (`/api/auth/reset`) or change (`PATCH /api/account`) revokes that member's links only; an owner's reset and the founder's reset of an owner (`/api/admin/users/[email]`) revoke the owner's and every roster member's; an owner's password change revokes only the owner's. The claims (`sec:book:ext:{scope}:*`) and their index belong to the brand scope, not to a login: removing a member keeps them, and account deletion removes them through `secretaryKeys` (item 7). A revoke that fails is a 500 as for the feed (the password and sessions are already saved), and `loginHolds` refuses the record at read time meanwhile (permissions.md, Reem phase 3 B2a item 5).
12. The card (rule 599). «الحجوزات» / "Bookings" on My to-dos (`BookingCard`, below the calendar feed card, every login: the same `booking.manage` as the routes, so a founder impersonating sees the refusal in words). Two rows. Cal.com: Connect (or Renew) POSTs the page's language and shows the URL and the signing key once, from that answer only, with Copy buttons, the three steps as an ordered list (Settings, Developer, Webhooks, New; paste the URL and the key as the secret; tick Booking created, Booking cancelled and Booking rescheduled), the private-link warning and the note that Renew ends the old URL and key; after the page is left the row reads «Connected since {date}» with Renew and Stop, never the URL or key (`GET` sends neither). Calendly: a password field for the personal access token with the line "used once to set up the connection, never saved" and the plan note (Standard or higher); the field is emptied before the request leaves, on success and on refusal; the typed refusals `calendly_plan` and `calendly_rejected` (422) are shown in words; Stop and Renew say the old Calendly webhook stays at Calendly until the owner deletes it (B2-R14). A link made in another brand shows one line saying so (`sameBrand`). When a Calendly renewal fails the row shows what the server holds (`rowAfterFailedRenew`): a 400, 403, 409 or 429 is answered before the old link is touched, so the row stays Connected; a 422 comes after the old record was replaced and the new one revoked, so the row is no longer connected and the line that the old webhook stays at Calendly remains; any other outcome (a server error, no answer) reads the status again. The error text stays visible in every case.

## Revocation (2026-09-18)

- Password change (`PATCH /api/account`): new hash and `sessionsAfter = now` saved inside the user lock, the caller's session is destroyed and a new one issued. Every other session of the account fails at its next request. This is the owner's change; it does not sign members out. A member's change is its own (section 4).
- Password reset (`POST /api/auth/reset`) and founder `resetPassword`: the same, plus `revokeAllMcp` (personal MCP token and every OAuth grant).
- Calendar feed and booking links (2026-10-05, rules 582 and 598): wherever a feed link is revoked a booking link is too (`revokeBook`/`revokeBooks` next to `revokeFeed`/`revokeFeeds`): same logins, same ordering. See the booking flow, item 11.
- Connected apps: `GET /api/mcp/grants` lists OAuth grants of the signed-in account; `DELETE /api/mcp/grants?id=` revokes one, `?all=1` all. A grant id of another account is a 404. Refreshing a token keeps the grant and deletes the previous access token.
- Teams (2026-09-29): a member's password change or reset stamps that member's own `sessionsAfter` (its other devices only); an owner reset and a founder `resetPassword` also stamp `teamSessionsAfter` on the owner record, which signs every member out; a removal, a member leaving and a seat pause end that member's sessions at their next request, because `currentUser` re-reads the member record; `createUser` stamps `sessionsAfter` at creation, so a session from an earlier account at the same address never revives. Listing and revoking grants is owner only (`oauth.grants`).

One offer per account: never stacked.
6. Billing. While the offer is open (no payment recorded, and for the early-partner offer not past its last day), the owner sees the banner with, for the early-partner offer, "The offer ends on {day}" / "ينتهي العرض في {day}", and the note that the discount comes off the price before VAT and VAT is computed on the discounted price (rule 409); and "15% off your first month: {list − 15%, nearest riyal} instead of {list} + VAT" on each self-serve card (`firstMonthPriceSar`, `src/lib/pricing.ts`), and the button opens WhatsApp (`SUPPORT_WHATSAPP`) with the account email, plan and discounted amount; the fixed-price pay link stays as "pay the full price". Members see nothing of it.
7. Record a payment (founder console, user card → `POST /api/admin/payments/{email}` `record`). Amount in SAR excluding VAT, plan, date, reference, and a `pay_` id the form keeps until it succeeds (a retry records once). Under the customer's lock: the first payment uses the unused offer (`usedAt`, the expected discounted price stored with the payment) unless it is dated before the offer was granted or, for the early-partner offer, after its last day (the day the money arrived counts, so money that came in time and is recorded later still uses it; the console says when an unused offer has ended), and `firstPaidOn` becomes the earliest payment that stands. Then `settle` re-takes the decision for every payment of the customer, so a backdated payment moves the others with it. A payment earns the partner a commission when the account is attributed, its partner exists, neither account has the other on its roster, the first payment is dated on or after the attribution day (else `before_attribution`: the customer paid before being referred) and on or before the attribution day + 30 days (else "lapsed", for every payment, and the attribution stays on record), and this payment is dated before the first payment's day 4 calendar months later (clamped to month end). The commission is 15% of the amount (rounded to the halala). The payment records are written first, each marked with its commission or the reason for none; then, under the partner's lock, `partner:ledger:{partner}` is compared with them and `c:{paymentId}` rows are written or voided to match, so a request that failed halfway is repaired by the next one. Ledger rows carry the customer's label, never the email. Payments whose answer changed are returned as `reclassified` and logged. Recording does not change the plan; activation stays `POST /api/admin/plan`.
8. Void a payment (`void`): marked voided with the reason, then `settle` as in step 7: its commission gets `voidedAt`, and if it was the first payment the first payment day moves to the next one standing, so a later payment past the 30 days loses its commission too. When no other payment stands, `firstPaidOn` is cleared and the offer that payment used is open again.
   - Top-up packs (since 2026-10-02, rules 454-455, section 38 step 10): a pack is a customer payment. The console's "Add top-up" records its payment through step 7's `recordPayment` (with `topup: {lot, credits}`, never the first-month offer, never the plan's first payment day) under the request's payment id, so its commission follows the same decision and is written once; the pack's "Void" voids that payment first (its commission gets `voidedAt`), then the pack. The Payments form records plan payments only, and `void` on a live pack's payment is 409 `topup_live`. Since 2026-10-02 (rule 462, section 38 step 12) the pack's "Refund unused" records a refund of its unused credits on that payment (`Payment.refund`), and `settle` cuts the commission by commission × refunded / paid, once; refunded in full, the payment keeps no commission (`notCounted: "refunded"`).
9. Payouts. Balance = commissions not voided − payouts not voided (their commission amounts only). Commission is 15% of the payment before VAT (rule 409). Thakeen bears the taxes (rule 412): the console flags a partner who ticked "I am VAT-registered" and gave the number (rule 416; a tax number alone, which may be an income-tax id, flags nothing) ("VAT-registered: add VAT on payout") and one living outside Saudi Arabia ("withholding tax may apply — ask your accountant"), and the payout form records the commission amount, the VAT paid on top and the withholding tax borne (both optional, the amounts actually paid; no rate is coded; text that is not a number, such as "1,500", is sent as typed and refused with `bad_vat` / `bad_wht`, never dropped: rule 417, `taxField`), date and reference. The two taxes never change the balance; the console totals them per partner and the partner sees the VAT added to each payout. Below SAR 200 it is "pending", from 200 "payable". Before the first transfer the founder checks the bank details with the partner by phone (the number on the application) and presses "confirm" (`confirm_bank` with the `savedAt` of the version shown; details changed since are not confirmed); every save by the partner clears the confirmation, and the console shows "unconfirmed, saved on …" and flags a partner whose account was created after the approval (whoever signed up first with that address holds it). `POST /api/admin/partners` `payout` (a `po_` id the form keeps until it succeeds, amount, date, reference) is accepted only to confirmed details and against a payable balance, never past it, under the partner's lock, so two at once cannot both pay it and a retry records once (`already`). The founder transfers by hand first, then marks it paid. A payout entered by mistake is voided (`void_payout`, with a reason, logged): kept on record, its amount back in the balance.
10. The partner page reads `partnerDashboard`: referrals as `C-` plus six characters of the attribution's random `ref` (never the name or email, and not recomputable from a guessed address), signup day, status (trial while inside its 30 days, paid, lapsed) and the months with a counted payment; commissions; payouts; the IBAN masked. Bank details (`PUT /api/partner`): owner only, refused while impersonating, any country's IBAN (rule 411: ISO 3166 country code, the registry length for common countries with all six GCC states, mod-97), the country the partner lives in, an optional VAT or tax registration number and a separate "I am VAT-registered" box (only `true` counts and it needs the number, 400 `vat_number_required`), never logged; the founder sees them in full on the Partners tab. The page links the programme terms and says Thakeen bears VAT on the commission and withholding where it applies, and the partner stays responsible for their own income tax.
11. Account deletion (`wipeAccount`): the account's payments, and for a partner its record (bank details), code, referral set and ledger; the account also leaves its own partner's referral set. The partner's ledger keeps the deleted customer's commissions under the label only, and the console, which maps labels to emails through the referral set, shows no address for it.
