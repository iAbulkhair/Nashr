Status: proposed on 2026-10-18; decisions 1-12 accepted at their defaults by the founder the same day ("do your default"). Not built. Wave 3a of the standing order "best of supastarter in all aspects" (2026-10-16): supastarter's organizations, members, invitations and active-organization switching, ported as a PATTERN onto the Redis stack. No supastarter source, no Prisma, no Better Auth. Written against `main` `615b0624` plus the account-id spec (`docs/specs/2026-10-17-account-id.md`, carried on this branch from `f704becb`). Line numbers are on `615b0624`.

Rules reserved: 2110-2139. This file is a spec only: no code changes, nothing on production. The flows section number and permission-row numbers are assigned when it is built; `docs/system/tests/2110-multi-org.md` lists the rules when they are pinned (rule 788).

## ملخص للمؤسس

- **اليوم:** كل دخول مربوط بمساحة عمل واحدة فقط. المالك هو الحساب نفسه، والعضو سجلّه فيه اسم مالك واحد. لهذا موظف الوكالة ما يقدر يدخل مساحة عميلين بنفس البريد، وصاحب حساب ما يقدر ينضم لفريق حساب ثاني (الدعوة ترجع «غير متاح»).
- **الحل:** نفصل ثلاثة أشياء: **الشخص** (البريد وكلمة المرور والتحقق بخطوتين)، و**مساحة العمل** (الخطة والرصيد والبطاقة والبراندات، وهي الحساب الحالي)، و**العضوية** (هذا الشخص في هذه المساحة بهذا الدور). شخص واحد له عضويات كثيرة، ويبدّل بينها من قائمة أعلى الشريط الجانبي.
- **العزل بين العملاء يبقى كما هو:** المساحة النشطة تُقرأ من الجلسة فقط، وفي كل طلب نتأكد من جديد أن الشخص عضو فيها. ما نأخذ رقم مساحة من الطلب أبداً.
- **الفوترة لكل مساحة** كما اليوم: الرصيد والخطة والمقاعد والبطاقة لا تنتقل بين المساحات. موظف الوكالة يأخذ مقعداً في مساحة العميل.
- **ما يتغيّر للعملاء الحاليين: لا شيء** حتى الإصدار الثاني. أربعة إصدارات حسب قاعدة 679: توسيع (يقرأ القديم والجديد، والسلوك كما هو)، ثم سكربت يبني الفهارس بموافقتك، ثم تفعيل، ثم تنظيف.
- **يُبنى فوق مواصفة رقم الحساب** (account id). فيها ثلاث نقاط لازم تتعدل، مذكورة في قسم «Changes forced on the account-id spec».
- **القرارات الـ12 في آخر الملف اعتمدتَها بالخيار الافتراضي** (2026-10-18). ما يحتاجك بعدها في قائمة مهامك: دمج إصدارات النشر، وموافقتك قبل تشغيل سكربت الفهرسة على البيانات الحقيقية.

## Goal

A person signs in once and works in every workspace they belong to: an agency's staff inside several client workspaces, a client's own staff, a freelancer with their own workspace who is also invited into a customer's. They switch workspaces from a switcher. Every workspace keeps its own plan, credits, seats, brands, data and team, and no request can reach a workspace its person is not a live member of, rollbacks included.

## Words

The code word "workspace" already means a brand's scope (`wsKey`, `workspaceScope`, `src/lib/keys.ts:13-30`). To keep the code unambiguous:

| Concept | Code name | Interface word | Record |
|---|---|---|---|
| A human who signs in | person (`PersonId`, `pid`) | الحساب الشخصي / your login | `person:{pid}` (new) or a legacy record, section 2 |
| The billed tenant: plan, credits, seats, brands, card | account (`AccountId`, `aid`) | مساحة العمل / workspace | `user:{aid}` (today's `User`) |
| A person inside an account, with a role | membership | عضوية | `mbr:{aid}:{pid}` (new) or a legacy `member:` record |
| One brand inside an account | brand, scope | البراند | unchanged |

This spec says "account" for the tenant and "workspace" only for what the customer sees.

## Today

- **One login, one account.** An owner login IS the account: `user:{email}` holds both the credentials (`passHash`, `sessionsAfter`, `devicesAfter`, `emailVerifiedAt`) and the tenant (plan, credits, billing, `brandCap`, `seats`) (`src/lib/auth.ts:61-225`). A member login is `member:{email}` with ONE `memberOf` and ONE `role` (`src/lib/team.ts:35-51`).
- **No second membership.** `createInvite` answers `unavailable` for any address that is already an owner or a member anywhere (`team.ts:228-229`), and `acceptInvite` refuses the same under `lock:login` (`team.ts:299-301`). So an owner can never join another team, and a member can never join a second one.
- **Resolution.** `resolveSession` (`auth.ts:506-551`) reads `user:{login}`, `member:{login}` and `activebrand:{login}` in one round. An owner resolves to its own record; a member to `getUser(memberOf)` after a second round (owner, brand list, roster). The scope is `scopeFor(owner.email, brandId)` (`brands.ts:65-67`), and `sc(u) = storedScope(u.scope ?? u.email)` (`auth.ts:227`).
- **Per-login state** keyed by the login address: `activebrand:{login}` (`keys.ts:133`), `sec:feedof:{login}` and `sec:bookof:{login}:{prov}` (`src/lib/feed-token.ts:16`, `booking-token.ts`), `devs:`, `mfa:`, `googlesub:`.
- **Roles.** One table, `MIN_ROLE` (`src/lib/roles.ts:9-144`), three roles. `can()` (`roles.ts:187-196`) refuses a member actor holding "owner" and a non-member actor holding anything else. The owner role belongs to the `user:` record only and is never assignable (teams design section 5; "ownership transfer" is listed under "Later").
- **Seats** per account: `seatLimit` (`src/lib/pricing.ts:129-133`), 3/1/3/7 for trial/starter/pro/business, Agency = Business unless `User.seats`. Counted under `lock:team:{owner}` (`team.ts:227-232`). **Brands** per account: `brandCapFor` 1/1/2/5, Agency 10 (`pricing.ts:140-145`).
- **Agencies today** put every client in ONE account as brands (cap 10, raised per deal). That stays the cheapest path and does not need this spec. This spec is for the cases it cannot serve: the client owns and pays for its own account, a person needs their own account AND a seat elsewhere, or one person works for several agencies or clients.
- **Billing and credits** are fields of the account record; the Paymob card is `paymob:card:{account}`. MCP tokens and OAuth grants are per account and minted by the owner only (`src/lib/mcp.ts:16-41`, `MIN_ROLE["mcp.token"]`). Connectors are per brand scope (`conn:{scope}:{provider}`); `composioUserId(scope)` is an HMAC of the scope (`src/lib/connectors/composio.ts:97`).
- **Audit.** Brand activity is per scope (`activity:{scope}`, `src/lib/activity.ts`). There is no record of team changes (invites, joins, role changes, removals) beyond the member record itself. Staff actions go to `admin:log` (`src/lib/admin-audit.ts`).
- **Deletion.** `wipeAccount` deletes every member LOGIN of the account (`wipeTeam`, `team.ts:512-524`): a member exists only for its one account.
- **Console.** `workspaceView(chosen)` (`src/lib/admin-workspace.ts:51-74`) opens the account an address belongs to: the owner record, or the one owner named on a member record.

## Design

### 1. Model

Three record types, and the rule that ties them:

- **Person** (`PersonId`): credentials, the sign-in address, name, language, two-step state, session stamps. It holds no plan, no credits and no data.
- **Account** (`AccountId`, today's `User`): the tenant. Gains `ownerPid?` (section 3). Its `email` becomes the account's billing and contact address, kept equal to the owner person's address (section 12).
- **Membership** of person P in account A, with a role `admin` or `member`. The owner is not a membership row: ownership is `ownerOf(A) = A.ownerPid ?? A.id-or-email` (section 3), so no membership write can ever create an owner (rule 2115).
- **Reverse index** `orgs:{pid}`: the accounts P owns or is a member of. It is a hint for listing only. Every decision re-reads the forward records (rule 2136).

```
login:{email} ─► person P ─┬─ owns ─────────► account A1 (A1.ownerPid = P)
                            ├─ mbr:{A2}:{P} ─► account A2 (role admin)
                            └─ mbr:{A3}:{P} ─► account A3 (role member)
orgs:{P} = {A1, A2, A3}     members:{A2} ∋ P, members:{A3} ∋ P
```

### 2. Person ids and where credentials live

- **New persons** (from release M2): `pid = "p_" + base32(crypto.randomBytes(15))`, 26 characters, the same alphabet and source as the account-id spec's `a_` ids. Never in a URL, never shown.
- **Legacy owners:** `pid` = the account id (the frozen first email, account-id spec section 1). The owner's account and person share one id.
- **Legacy members:** `pid` = the member's address at the moment the conversion script runs, frozen from then on (like a legacy account id).
- **`getPerson(pid)`** is the one reader (release M1, an adapter):
  1. `person:{pid}` when it exists;
  2. else the legacy owner record `user:{pid}`, reading only the credential fields;
  3. else the legacy member record `member:{pid}`.
- **Writes** of credential fields (password, name, language, two-step, session stamps) take `lock:person:{pid}` and write `person:{pid}`. Until the contract release (M4) they ALSO write the same fields to the legacy record the person came from, under that record's own lock, so a rollback to M1 or M2 reads a current password (rule 2132).
- The conversion script (S1, under Releases) creates `person:{pid}` for every legacy owner and member, so after it runs every read takes step 1.

### 3. Accounts and ownership

- `User.ownerPid?: PersonId`. Absent on legacy accounts, where the owner is the person with `pid = acct(u)` (account-id spec `acct`). Set on every account created from M2 and on any account after an ownership transfer (section 11).
- `ownerOf(u): PersonId = u.ownerPid ?? acct(u)`. The only function that answers "who owns this account".
- New accounts (M2) are created by a person (signup, or "create workspace", section 10): `aid = "a_…"` (account-id spec), `ownerPid = pid`. No credentials are written to the account record.

### 4. Memberships

- `mbr:{aid}:{pid}` holds the `Membership` (shape in section 6). `members:{aid}` (the existing family, `keys.ts:166`) holds pids. For a legacy team those members ARE pids already, because a legacy member's pid is its address.
- **`getMembership(aid, pid)`** (M1 adapter), in one round:
  1. `mbr:{aid}:{pid}` AND `members:{aid}` contains `pid`: the membership;
  2. else the legacy `member:{pid}` record when its `memberOf === aid` AND `members:{aid}` contains `pid`: read as `{role, acceptedAt, invitedBy, disabled}` (rule 2134);
  3. else none.
- **`roleIn(account, pid)`**: `"owner"` when `ownerOf(account) === pid`, else the membership's role, else null.
- A membership row without its roster entry, or a roster entry without a row, grants nothing (today's `onRoster` rule, `team.ts:152`, kept).

### 5. Key families (through `src/lib/keys.ts` only)

A new group `personKey` beside `wsKey`, `accountKey` and `globalKey`, with two branded types modelled on `Scope` (`keys.ts:22`): `PersonId` (made only by `getLogin`, the session, a stored membership or roster) and `AccountId` (made only by the session after the membership check, a stored pointer, or `users:all`). A plain `string` from a request does not type-check in any builder (rule 2137).

| Key | Type | Holds | Group |
|---|---|---|---|
| `person:{pid}` | json `Person` | credentials and profile | personKey |
| `lock:person:{pid}` | lock | person writes | personKey |
| `orgs:{pid}` | set of `AccountId` | reverse index, hint only | personKey |
| `lastorg:{pid}` | string `AccountId` | last workspace chosen in the switcher | personKey |
| `mbr:{aid}:{pid}` | json `Membership` | role, status, dates | accountKey |
| `members:{aid}` | set of `PersonId` | the roster (existing family, meaning widened) | accountKey |
| `teamlog:{aid}` | capped list | membership events (section 13) | accountKey |
| `xfer:{aid}` | json, TTL 7 days | a pending ownership transfer | accountKey |
| `activebrand:{pid}:{aid}` | string | the brand last chosen by P in A | personKey |
| `sec:feedof:{pid}:{aid}` / `sec:bookof:{pid}:{aid}:{prov}` | string | P's calendar feed and booking links in A | personKey |

- `activebrand:{login}`, `sec:feedof:{login}` and `sec:bookof:{login}:{prov}` stay readable in M1-M3 as the fallback for a person's HOME account (the legacy owner's own account, or a legacy member's `memberOf`), and go in M4.
- Login-keyed families of the account-id spec class B (`login:`, `devs:`, `mfa:`, `googlesub:`, `emailchange*`, `verify:*`) become person-keyed in meaning; for legacy persons the key bytes are the same.
- Invites stay `invite:{sha256}` and `invites:{aid}`.
- No family uses SCAN or KEYS. Every list comes from a set or a stored pointer.

### 6. Stored shapes (zod, `src/lib/stored.ts` style)

Every schema is `z.looseObject`, optional for anything a later release adds, so a read-modify-write never strips a newer field (rule 679, rules 2035-2039).

```ts
export const PersonRow = z.looseObject({
  pid: z.string().min(1),
  email: z.string().min(3),                    // the sign-in address, normalized
  name: z.string().catch(""),
  lang: z.enum(["ar", "en"]).catch("ar"),
  passHash: z.string().optional(),             // absent for a Google-only person
  emailVerifiedAt: z.string().optional(),
  sessionsAfter: z.string().optional(),
  devicesAfter: z.string().optional(),
  suspended: z.union([z.literal(true), z.looseObject({ until: z.string() })]).optional(), // person-level, section 14
  trialUsedAt: z.string().optional(),          // decision 4
  createdAt: z.string().catch(""),
});

export const MembershipRow = z.looseObject({
  aid: z.string().min(1),
  pid: z.string().min(1),
  role: z.enum(["admin", "member"]),           // never "owner": a row claiming it does not parse and grants nothing
  acceptedAt: z.string().min(1),
  invitedBy: z.string().catch(""),             // a PersonId; "" for converted rows whose inviter is unknown
  disabled: z.literal("seats").optional(),     // enforceSeats, as today
});

// Session record (auth.ts Session) gains, both optional (expand):
//   pid:  PersonId              the person signed in
//   acct: AccountId             the ACTIVE account, re-verified on every request (section 7)
// Invite gains: aid (the account, beside the legacy `owner`), invitedByPid.
// User gains: ownerPid?: PersonId.
```

A membership row that fails the schema reads as "no membership" and is logged without values (`parseStored`), so a malformed row fails closed.

### 7. Sessions and the active workspace

- **Sign-in** resolves the address to a person (`getLogin`, account-id spec section 2, returning a person), checks the password and two-step as today, then picks the active account: `lastorg:{pid}` if it still holds (owner or live membership), else the account the person owns, else the oldest live membership. The session record is `{email, pid, acct, at}`. No live membership and no owned account: `acct` is absent (the "no workspace" state below).
- **Every request** (`resolveSession`), round 2 in ONE parallel round, because `pid` and `acct` both come from round 1:
  `getPerson(pid)`, `login:{email}`, `user:{acct}`, `mbr:{acct}:{pid}` (+ the legacy `member:{pid}`), `sismember members:{acct} pid`, `brands:{acct}`, `activebrand:{pid}:{acct}` (+ the legacy `activebrand:{email}`).
  The session resolves only when: the login still maps to `pid` (account-id rule 1984); the person is not suspended and the session is not older than its stamps; the account exists, is not suspended, is verified (rule 767 kept); and `roleIn(account, pid)` is not null with the membership enabled. Then `u = account`, `u.actor = {kind, pid, email: person.email, name, lang, role}` with `kind = role === "owner" ? "owner" : "member"`, `u.brandId` the active brand if it is an active brand of `acct`, else "default", and `u.scope = scopeFor(acct(u), brandId)`.
  That is two rounds for every kind of login; a member pays one round less than today (rule 2135).
- **A session written before M1** (no `pid`, no `acct`) resolves as today and gets `pid`/`acct` derived from the legacy records in the same rounds. It is never rewritten on read.
- **A membership removed, disabled or left** ends that account for every session at the next request. The session then resolves to nothing for app routes; the app layout sends the browser to `/{lang}/app/workspaces`, which re-picks an account through the switch route. Signing in again is not needed.
- **"No workspace" state.** `currentPerson()` (new, beside `currentUser()`) resolves a session with a valid person and no valid account. Only these may call it (pinned like rule 752): the workspaces page, `GET /api/workspaces`, `POST /api/workspaces`, `POST /api/workspaces/switch`, the join page and accept route, the person's own security settings, and sign-out. Every other route keeps `currentUser()`, which answers null without an account.

**Switching** (`POST /api/workspaces/switch {handle}`):

- `handle` is a public, opaque name for an account: `wsHandle(aid) = "w_" + base32(HMAC-SHA256(CONNECT_SECRET, "ws:handle:v1:" + aid))[:16]`. It is derived, so there is no index to look it up by, and it never contains an address (rule 2114).
- The route reads `orgs:{pid}`, computes the handle of each entry, keeps the one that matches, then re-checks `roleIn` on the forward records. A handle that matches nothing, or matches an entry whose membership is gone, answers 404 `not_found`, the same answer as a made-up handle (rule 2112).
- On success it rewrites the session record's `acct` (`SET XX`, TTL kept) and `lastorg:{pid}`. It never writes `activebrand`.
- This is the only place a value from the request names an account, and only as a selector among the person's own memberships: the same exception Part C of the brand-workspaces design makes for a brand id (C7). CLAUDE.md's tenant-isolation line gains that sentence when built.

**Two tabs** (the defect Part C fixes for brands, `2026-10-09-admin-shell-and-brand-workspaces-design.md` C8): with the account in the session, tab 1 on A and tab 2 switching to B would send tab 1's next save into B. Guard (rule 2113):

- The app layout renders the active account's handle into the page. The client fetch helper sends it as `x-thakeen-ws` on every `/api` call (one helper; Part C plans `src/lib/api-client.ts` for its brand header, and both headers go through it).
- The route wrapper (`src/lib/api-handler.ts`, `auth: "user"`) compares it with `wsHandle(acct(u))`. A mismatch on any method answers 409 `workspace_changed` and does nothing; the page shows "this tab belongs to another workspace" with a reload button. No header (MCP, cron, an old page) passes, as Part C does for its header on GET; once every route is on the wrapper, a state-changing browser request without it answers 409 too.
- Decision 7 keeps the account out of the URL for now. If the founder chooses the URL, Part C's `/{lang}/app/b/{brandId}` becomes `/{lang}/app/w/{handle}/b/{brandId}` and the header guard stays as the second check.

### 8. What `sc(u)` becomes, and why isolation stays provable

`sc(u)` keeps its signature and body: `storedScope(u.scope ?? acct(u))`, where `u` is the ACTIVE account. The ~150 call sites do not change.

The argument, each step pinned by a test:

1. A request's `u` is made only by `resolveSession`, `userFromBearer`, cron iteration of `users:all`, or a founder impersonation. None reads an account id from the request (rules 1980 and 2112).
2. `resolveSession` takes `acct` from the session record, which only sign-in and the switch route write, and both write it only after `roleIn(account, pid)` holds. It re-checks `roleIn` on the forward records on every request (rule 2111), so a stale session or a stale `orgs:{pid}` entry gives nothing (rule 2136).
3. `u.scope` is built from `acct(u)` and a brand id that is checked to be an active brand of that same account.
4. `Scope` values come only from `sc(u)`, `workspaceScope` over the account's own brand list, and `storedScope` over our own records (rule 2031, the ratchet). `AccountId` and `PersonId` get the same treatment (rule 2137).
5. **Property test** (rule 2110): random graphs of persons, accounts, brands and memberships; random sessions; random requests with forged headers, bodies, handles and brand ids. For every request, every key it touches belongs to an account where the session's person holds a live membership or ownership AT THAT MOMENT. It runs over the existing workspace routes through the route wrapper's test harness.
6. **Round trip** (rule 2122): a person in three accounts writes in each; every read in each account returns only that account's data, and a charge in A never moves B's credits.

### 9. Invitations

The invite record and token stay (`team.ts:52-60`, SHA-256 in the index, the token only in the link's fragment, 7 days). What changes in M2:

- **Inviting an address that already has a person is allowed.** `createInvite` stops answering `unavailable` for existing logins. It refuses only an address that is already the owner or a live member of THIS account (`already_member`, which reveals nothing outside the inviter's own team). The answer to an address with a person and one without is the same, so the route stops being an existence probe (rule 2116).
- **Accepting, the person exists:** the join page asks them to sign in (the token stays in the fragment across sign-in). `POST /api/team/accept` then requires `currentPerson()` whose verified address equals `invite.email`. It never creates a membership for an existing person by address alone, and never asks for a new password (rule 2116). Under `inviteuse` → `lock:team:{aid}` → `lock:person:{pid}`: re-read the invite, the account, the inviter and the seats (as today), write `mbr:{aid}:{pid}`, `sadd members:{aid} pid`, `sadd orgs:{pid} aid`, drop the invite, write a `teamlog` row. The session's `acct` moves to the new account.
- **Accepting, no person yet:** today's flow: name and password create `person:{p_…}` and the membership. No account, no trial credits, not in `users:all`, no referral code (as today's members, rule 2117).
- **Role** comes from the invite only. Nothing carries over from the person's roles elsewhere (decision 5).
- **Seats** are counted per account under `lock:team:{aid}` exactly as today. A person uses one seat in each account they belong to (rule 2118).
- **Invites waiting for a person** (M3): `invitesfor:{email}` (set of invite ids, TTL 7 days) lets the switcher show "2 invitations". It is written with the invite and read only for the signed-in person's own verified address.

### 10. Creating a workspace

`POST /api/workspaces {name}` (M2), behind `currentPerson()`:

- the person's address is verified;
- the rate rule `wscreate` (3 per person per day, `src/lib/rate.ts`);
- the cap on owned accounts (decision 3; default 3, the founder raises it per person);
- a new account `a_…` with `ownerPid = pid`, written under `lock:login:{email}` and `lock:person:{pid}`, and added to `users:all` and `orgs:{pid}`;
- the trial only when `person.trialUsedAt` is unset; it is set in the same write. Otherwise the account starts on plan `trial` with 0 credits and a "choose a plan" banner (decision 4, rule 2123).

Signup itself becomes "create a person, then create their first workspace" in one request.

### 11. Leaving, removal, ownership transfer, deletion

- **Leave** (`team.leave`, members only, as today): under `lock:team:{aid}`: delete `mbr:{aid}:{pid}` (and the legacy `member:{pid}` when this is its home account, in M1-M3), `srem members:{aid}`, `srem orgs:{pid}`, delete `activebrand:{pid}:{aid}`, revoke the feed and booking links of `(pid, aid)`, drop invites the person sent in this account, write `teamlog`. The PERSON stays (rule 2124). The owner cannot leave: they transfer or delete.
- **Removal by owner or admin:** the same steps and today's role limits (`removeMember`, `team.ts:409-420`).
- **Seat enforcement** pauses memberships, not persons (`enforceSeats` works on the roster as today).
- **Ownership transfer** (M3): the owner names an enabled admin of the account. The admin confirms from a mailed single-use link (`xfer:{aid}`, 7 days). Under `lock:team:{aid}` → `lock:user:{aid}`:
  - set `ownerPid` to the new owner and the account's contact `email` to their address;
  - turn the old owner into an `admin` membership;
  - clear the saved card and stop renewals at the period end, so the new owner is never charged on the old owner's card (decision 8);
  - end MCP tokens and OAuth grants (they were the old owner's);
  - write `teamlog` (rule 2128).
- **Account deletion** (`wipeAccount(aid)`): deletes what it deletes today, keyed by the account and its scopes. For members it removes MEMBERSHIPS, not persons: every `mbr:{aid}:*`, the `orgs:{pid}` entry of each member and of the owner, the per-`(pid, aid)` state, and the legacy home `member:{pid}` records whose `memberOf === aid`. A member with other memberships keeps working there; one with none lands on the workspaces page (rule 2126). Today's `wipeTeam` deletion of member logins is replaced.
- **Deleting a person** ("delete my login", M3): refused while the person owns any account (transfer or delete it first). Otherwise, under each account's team lock in turn (`pmap`, never `await db.*` in a loop): drop every membership, then the person, `login:{email}`, `orgs:`, `lastorg:`, `devs:`, `mfa:`, `googlesub:` and the sessions (rule 2127).
- **A person with no workspace** is kept and can create one or accept an invite (decision 10).

### 12. Billing, credits and plan caps

- **Per account, unchanged** (decision 1): plan, credits, ledger, holds, top-ups, `seats`, `brandCap`, the Paymob card and renewals stay fields and keys of `user:{aid}`. The active account pays for every charge in the request; `sc(u)` and `charge()` already key by it.
- **Who pays:** the owner (`billing`, `billing.pay` stay owner-only). Billing mail goes to the owner person's address. `User.email` stays the account's billing address and is synced from the owner person on an address move (account-id section 6) and on a transfer. Paymob `billing_data.email` reads it.
- **Seats:** an agency's staff take seats in the client's account, paid by the client's plan (decision 2). There is no cross-account seat pool and no parent/child account in this spec.
- **Several owned accounts** (decision 3): each has its own plan, card and invoice. Founding seats and the price lock stay per account (`founding:seats`, `pricelock:accounts` hold account ids).
- **Trial abuse:** one trial per person (`trialUsedAt`); the signup IP throttle stays.
- **Margin and cost views** (`admin-stats`, `margin.ts`, `evals.ts`) iterate `users:all` = accounts: unchanged.

### 13. Audit log

- `teamlog:{aid}`: a capped list (`pushCapped`, cap 500), one row per membership event: `invited`, `invite_cancelled`, `joined`, `role_changed`, `removed`, `left`, `paused_for_seats`, `resumed`, `ownership_offered`, `ownership_transferred`, `workspace_created`. Each row is `{at, kind, actor: {pid, email-of-the-day}, target: {pid?, email-of-the-day}, role?, via: "web" | "console" | "system"}`. It never holds a token, an invite id or a password.
- Read by owner and admin through a new action `team.log.view: "admin"` on the Team settings page. Not in the brand activity log (that is per scope; this is per account), not in the CSV export of activity.
- Staff actions on memberships (console) also write `admin:log` as today; the `teamlog` row says `via: "console"` and never names the staff member to the customer.
- Writes happen under the same `lock:team:{aid}` as the change they record, so the log never shows a change that did not happen (rule 2129).

### 14. Founder console

- **People lookup** (M3): `GET /api/admin/people/{email}` behind `isAdmin` and `staffMay("workspace.view")`. It resolves the address through `getLogin` and returns the person (no hash, no two-step secret) and each account it owns or belongs to: handle, name, role, plan, paused, last sign-in. Reads in `Promise.all`/`pmap` (rule 2130).
- **Workspace view** (`admin-workspace.ts`) keeps its URL. `ownerOf(chosen)` becomes "the person at this address, then the account they own, else their oldest membership", and the view lists the other memberships of that person so staff can open each one. Members show `alsoIn: n` (other accounts), never the names of those accounts to a customer.
- **Impersonation** opens one chosen account as its owner (`actor.kind: "founder"`, `pid` = the owner's), exactly as today. The session's `acct` is that account and it cannot switch (the switch route refuses `imp` sessions).
- **Suspension:** account suspension (`isSuspended(u)`, rules 2070-2071) blocks that account for every member and every token, and no longer blocks the owner's person in OTHER accounts. A new person-level suspension (`PersonRow.suspended`, read only through `isPersonSuspended`) blocks the person everywhere (decision 9, rule 2131).

### 15. MCP tokens, OAuth grants and connectors

- **MCP tokens and OAuth grants stay per account and owner-only** (decision 12). A token record names the account (account-id class C), `userFromBearer` resolves it with `getAccount` and sets the actor to the owner's person. A person who owns two accounts mints one token in each. A token never follows the browser's switch: an AI client bound to account A can never reach B (rule 2120).
- Member tokens stay "later" (teams design section 8).
- **Connectors, Composio, calendars, ad and store connections** are per brand scope: unchanged. A new account's scopes are `a_…` scopes, so `composioUserId` is the HMAC of a new value, as for any new account (rule 2121).
- **Calendar feed and booking links** become per `(pid, aid)` (section 5): a person in two accounts can have one feed in each. The token URLs of existing links keep working (the legacy keys are read as the home account's).

### 16. Interface

- **Workspace switcher** at the top of the sidebar, above the brand switcher. The current workspace's name (the default brand's name, else the owner's name) and the person's role chip. The menu lists the person's workspaces with role, plus «مساحة عمل جديدة» and, in M3, «دعوات بانتظارك (2)». Picking one calls the switch route and opens that workspace's inbox. Pattern from supastarter's organization select, rebuilt with Thakeen's components and tokens (no new UI package).
- With a single workspace (every customer until M2) the switcher shows nothing new.
- **`/{lang}/app/workspaces`**: the list, create, and the "no workspace" state.
- **Team settings:** the roster and invites as today, the "Team log" tab (owner and admin), and "Transfer ownership" (owner, M3).
- Copy follows the in-app dictionary and the founder's dialect review; no customer names, logos or numbers (CLAUDE.md).

## Changes forced on the account-id spec (`docs/specs/2026-10-17-account-id.md`)

1. **The login index names a person, not an account.** `login:{e}` becomes `{kind: "person", pid}`. If account-id R1 ships first with `{kind: "owner", id}`, multi-org M1 reads that as `pid = id` (a legacy owner's person id IS its account id), so nothing is rewritten. Recommendation: R1 writes `{kind: "person", pid}` from the start; it is the same bytes of work.
2. **Members get frozen ids and an index too.** The account-id spec keeps members at `member:{login}` (class B) and resolves them in `getLogin` step 2. With multi-org, memberships point at the person id, so a member's id must survive an address change exactly like an owner's. The conversion script also writes `login:{memberEmail} = {kind: "person", pid: memberEmail}`. `moveMemberLogin` becomes the same move in place as the owner move (index, then person record), and `getLogin`'s legacy member step needs the same guard as rule 1981 (the record's own `email` equals the address asked for).
3. **The session's `acct` means "the active account", not "the login's account".** Rule 1984's check "the login still maps to `acct`" becomes "the login maps to `pid`, and `pid` owns or is a live member of `acct`". The session gains `pid`.
4. **The owner move moves a person.** Account-id section 6 step 3 ("save `user:{id}` with `email: to`") becomes: save `person:{pid}` (and, until M4, the legacy record's credential fields), then sync `email` on every account the person owns.
5. **Class B "per login" families split in two.** Per person: `devs:`, `mfa:*`, `emailchange*`, `googlesub:`, `verify:*`. Per person AND account: `activebrand:`, `sec:feedof:`, `sec:bookof:`.
6. **Merge the two "enable" releases.** Account-id R2 makes new signups `a_` accounts with credentials on the account record. If it ships alone, every new account becomes one more legacy-shaped person to convert later. Recommendation (decision 11): ship account-id R2 together with multi-org M2, so a new signup is born as `person:{p_…}` plus `user:{a_…}`.
7. **Rule 1980** ("no route takes an account id from the request") stays true: the switch route takes a handle, which is not an account id, and uses it only as a selector among the person's own memberships.
8. **Decision D3** (the old address inside key names) now covers member addresses too: a legacy member's frozen pid is its address in `members:`, `mbr:` and `orgs:` key names. The same answer applies (accept it; Phase C if a privacy reason appears).
9. **`wipeAccount`** (account-id section 10) no longer deletes member logins (section 11 above).

Nothing in the account-id spec's R1 has to wait for this spec, provided item 1 is taken.

## Size and risk

- **M1 is large:** about 1.2k-1.8k lines across about 60 files. `resolveSession`, `getLogin`, `createSession`, `team.ts` (rebuilt on `getMembership`; every function keeps its signature where it can), `brands.ts` (active brand), `feed-token.ts`, `booking-token.ts`, `account.ts` (`wipeAccount`), `admin-workspace.ts`, `devices.ts`, `keys.ts`, `stored.ts`, the password and profile writers, plus tests.
  - Risk in production is low by construction: with `MULTI_ORG = false` each person has exactly one home and the adapters return what the legacy records hold.
  - Risk in code: a site that assumes one membership. Mitigation: the test helper `newUser`/`newMember` (`tests/helpers/session.ts`) gives every test person a `p_` id and a SECOND membership in an unrelated account, so the ~8k existing tests run with "more than one workspace" and any site that picks the wrong account fails a round trip.
- **S1 is small** (about 200 lines), low risk: `SET NX`-style writes, idempotent, dry run first.
- **M2 is medium-large** (about 1k lines: two routes, the switcher, the workspaces page, the guard in the wrapper and the client helper, accept for an existing person, signup split, copy). Risk medium, concentrated in accept and switch; rules 2112, 2113 and 2116 pin them.
- **M3 is medium** (about 600 lines). Transfer is the riskiest piece (money and ownership); rule 2128.
- **M4 + S2 are small.**
- **Redis cost:** round 2 of a session grows from 3 commands (owner) to about 8 parallel commands in one round trip; a member saves one round trip. The switch route costs one `orgs` read plus one parallel round. No new per-request round trip.
- **Order with other work:**
  - M1 touches `auth.ts`, `team.ts` and most account routes. It must not run beside account-id R1 (do R1 first) or beside a wide route-wrapper batch.
  - **Wave 3c (passkeys)** should be keyed by the person (`passkeys:{pid}`), so it waits for M1. Otherwise passkeys land on `user:`/`member:` records and need converting.
  - Part C (brand in URL) can land before or after M2. If after, decision 7's URL option is cheap to take then.

## Test rules to pin (2110-2139)

- **2110.** Isolation property: over random graphs of persons, accounts, brands and memberships, and requests with forged headers, bodies, query values, handles and brand ids, every key a request touches belongs to an account the session's person owns or is a live member of at that moment.
- **2111.** `resolveSession` re-checks the membership on every request: removed, left, paused or account-suspended means the next request is not in that account. A person-suspended session resolves nowhere.
- **2112.** The active account comes only from the session. The switch route accepts a handle only among the person's own forward-verified memberships; an unknown, foreign or stale handle answers the same 404. A source scan finds no route reading an account id or pid from the request.
- **2113.** `x-thakeen-ws` different from the session's active account answers 409 `workspace_changed` and writes nothing, on every route that calls `sc(u)` (coverage test over the wrapper). The proxy strips the header on non-`/api` paths.
- **2114.** `wsHandle` is `w_` + 16 base32 characters from an HMAC, never contains an address, and is never looked up through an index.
- **2115.** A role belongs to a membership: admin in A is nothing in B. `can()` reads the membership's role; "owner" comes only from `ownerOf`, and a membership row claiming "owner" does not parse.
- **2116.** Inviting an existing person is answered like inviting a new address. Accept by an existing person needs their signed-in session with the verified address equal to the invite's; no membership is ever created by address alone.
- **2117.** Accepting as a new person creates a `p_` person and a membership: no account, no credits, not in `users:all`, no referral code.
- **2118.** Seats are counted per account under `lock:team:{aid}`; one person uses one seat in each account; `enforceSeats` pauses memberships, never persons.
- **2119.** The active brand, feed and booking links are per `(pid, aid)`. Switching accounts never moves another account's brand; the legacy per-login keys are read only for the home account.
- **2120.** MCP tokens and OAuth grants resolve to one account; a token never follows a switch; an owner of two accounts holds two independent tokens.
- **2121.** Connections and `composioUserId` are per scope and unchanged for existing accounts.
- **2122.** Round trip across three accounts: data, credits, holds, card and plan never cross; a charge in A leaves B unchanged.
- **2123.** Create workspace: verified person only, the rate rule, the owned-accounts cap, the trial once per person, the new account in `users:all` and `orgs:`.
- **2124.** Leave and removal delete the membership, the roster entry, the reverse entry and the per-`(pid, aid)` state; the person survives; the owner cannot leave.
- **2125.** Today's role limits on invite, cancel, role change and removal hold per account (an admin of A removes only plain members of A).
- **2126.** Deleting an account removes its memberships and keeps its members' persons; a person in three accounts keeps the other two; no other account's keys are touched.
- **2127.** Deleting a person is refused while they own an account; otherwise every membership, the index, sessions, devices and two-step state go, with no `await db.*` in a loop.
- **2128.** Ownership transfer: only to an enabled admin, confirmed by the new owner's single-use link, the card cleared and renewals stopped at the period end, tokens and grants ended, the old owner kept as admin, a `teamlog` row.
- **2129.** Every membership event writes exactly one `teamlog` row under the same lock; the log is capped, read by owner and admin only, and never holds a token or an invite id.
- **2130.** The console people view and workspace view are behind `isAdmin` and `staffMay("workspace.view")`, read only, never show a hash or a secret; an impersonation session cannot switch accounts.
- **2131.** Account suspension blocks only that account; person suspension blocks the person in every account and every resolver.
- **2132.** M1 writes only shapes older builds ignore; with `MULTI_ORG` off every existing test passes unchanged; home-membership and credential writes are mirrored to the legacy records.
- **2133.** S1 is idempotent, dry run by default, reports conflicts without overwriting, and makes no `await db.*` inside a loop.
- **2134.** Adapter precedence: `person:` over legacy records; `mbr:` over a legacy member record; a legacy member record counts only when its `memberOf` is the account asked for and the roster lists it.
- **2135.** Every kind of session resolves in two rounds; a session written before M1 resolves as today.
- **2136.** `orgs:{pid}` is a hint: an entry without a forward membership grants nothing and is skipped in lists (a forged entry gives no access).
- **2137.** The new families are built only through `keys.ts` with branded `PersonId`/`AccountId`; the raw-key ratchet does not grow.
- **2138.** M4: the legacy reads are gone, sessions without `pid` are refused, and S2 leaves no `passHash` on account records.
- **2139.** Lock order `inviteuse` → `lock:team:{aid}` → `lock:login:{email}` → `lock:person:{pid}` (→ `lock:user:{aid}` for a transfer) is the only order used. An accept, a removal and a leave racing on one membership never leave a row without a roster entry or the reverse.

## DECISIONS FOR THE FOUNDER (accepted at the defaults below, 2026-10-18)

The founder answered "do your default"; each default below is now the decision. Founder-only steps that remain (release PR merges, the yes before S1 and S2 write production data, raising an owned-workspace cap, revisiting decision 2 after the first five agencies) are on the founder checklist.

1. **Billing per workspace or per login?** Default: per workspace, as today. Each workspace has its own plan, credits, seats, card and invoice; a login pays nothing by itself.
2. **Agency staff seats.** Default: an agency's staff take ordinary seats in the client's workspace, paid by the client's plan. No separate "external collaborator" seat yet; revisit after the first five agencies. (Alternative: up to 2 free collaborator seats per workspace for invited outside people.)
3. **Can one login own several paid workspaces?** Default: yes, up to 3 owned per person (you raise it per person from the console), each with its own plan and card.
4. **Trial for a second owned workspace.** Default: one trial per person ever; a second owned workspace starts with 0 credits and must pick a plan before it spends.
5. **Role carry-over between workspaces.** Default: none. Every invite sets its own role, and admin in one workspace means nothing in another.
6. **An invited person who already has a login.** Default: they sign in and press accept; nobody is added to a workspace without their own click.
7. **How the active workspace is chosen.** Default: kept in the session, with the per-tab guard (`x-thakeen-ws`). Putting the workspace in the URL (`/app/w/{handle}/…`) waits until the brand-in-URL work (Part C).
8. **Ownership transfer.** Default: built in M3, only to an existing admin who confirms by email; the saved card does NOT transfer and the plan runs to the end of its period.
9. **Suspension scope.** Default: suspending a workspace blocks only that workspace; a new "suspend person" blocks one login everywhere.
10. **A login left with no workspace** (left, removed, or its only workspace deleted). Default: the login is kept; they can create a workspace (trial only if never used) or accept an invite.
11. **Release order with the account-id spec.** Default: account-id R1 first, then multi-org M1; account-id R2 ships together with multi-org M2, so new signups are born as person + workspace once.
12. **MCP tokens.** Default: one token per workspace, minted by its owner, never following the browser's switch; tokens for members stay "later".
