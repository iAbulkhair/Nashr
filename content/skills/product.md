# Product, product marketing and retention playbook (Layan)

## Principles
- Discovery before delivery: no PRD for a problem no customer described in their own words.
- Outcomes over features: every roadmap item has a metric, baseline and target.
- Ability beats motivation: make the first valuable action easier before adding nudges.
- Time to value is the retention lever; measure it in days.
- Retention caps growth: fix the leaky bucket before buying acquisition.
- Proactive beats reactive: save before the cancel click, not after.
- Segment everything: free vs paid, new vs tenured, COD vs prepaid, city.
- Tag every guess inline as Assumption or Open question.
- Say no with evidence and a home: "not now", where it lives, what changes the answer.
- Write for a founder with ten minutes: one page, Arabic-ready wording.

## Discovery and JTBD
Cycle of 2 to 3 weeks: frame, interview, synthesise, test, decide. Interview 5 to 8 customers (30 to 40 min, call or WhatsApp voice, consent to record); stop when the same pain appears in 3+ interviews.

Script: (1) "Tell me about the last time you [did the job]." (2) "What happened right before you looked for a solution?" (3) "How do you handle it today? What did you try and why did you stop?" (4) "What does this cost you each week?" (5) "How did that feel? Who notices when it goes wrong?" (6) Switching forces: push, pull, habit, anxiety. Past behaviour only, never "would you use X". Keep verbatim Arabic quotes.

5 whys: when a customer names a feature, ask why until you reach the job ("know what to reorder before Ramadan"). Write jobs as verb + object + context, solution-agnostic; split functional, social, emotional.

Opportunity solution tree: one measurable outcome on top; 3 to 7 opportunities phrased "I struggle to..."; score importance x (1 minus satisfaction); 3+ solutions per top opportunity; one cheap experiment each (concierge, prototype, fake door, WhatsApp poll) with a pass threshold; revisit weekly.

Synthesising 10 to 30 items: one line each with source, segment, date; tag themes (max 8, 2+ items each); count frequency, rate intensity 1 to 5; keep 1 to 3 quotes per theme; rank by frequency x intensity x fit; flag quick wins; state what the sample cannot tell you.

## PRD
One to three pages, living, no pixel specs; use the skeleton below. The problem cites evidence; scope lists what is out and why; every requirement has testable acceptance criteria; metrics include a guardrail plus baseline and target date; risks cover value, usability, feasibility and viability with owners; open questions carry owner and deadline; the launch plan names tier, channels and kill criteria. Close with a self-check: weakest section, top three assumptions with a validation plan, next step.

## Prioritisation and roadmap
RICE = (Reach per quarter x Impact 0.25 to 3 x Confidence 50 to 100%) / Effort in person-weeks; use with usage data. ICE = Impact x Confidence x Ease, 1 to 10 each; use pre-PMF. Score with engineering, never solo; record overrides with reasons.

Now = this quarter, committed, 3 to 5 items with owners; Next = high confidence; Later = exploration, no dates. Sequence by dependencies and immovable dates (Ramadan, platform deadlines) before score. Name one shock absorber that moves first if anything slips. Pick 1 to 2 quarterly themes; each epic is a hypothesis with a metric.

Capacity: a 2 to 5 person team plans 60 to 70% on roadmap work, the rest on bugs and support. Sizes: S 1 to 2 weeks, M 3 to 4 weeks, L 2 to 3 months; L gets a thin first slice.

Saying no: restate the job behind the request, show its rank and why, offer a workaround or revisit date, log it. Never promise dates gated on third parties.

## Product marketing
Positioning: "For [segment] who [job], [product] is a [category] that [benefit], unlike [alternative] which [gap]." Messaging hierarchy: one promise, three pillars (benefit, proof, customer phrase), then feature claims; keep Arabic and English words-to-use and words-to-avoid lists.

Launch tiers: T1 major (new product or pricing; 4 to 6 weeks, all channels, pre-mortem); T2 feature (2 weeks; WhatsApp, email, in-app, social); T3 minor (changelog only). Rigour scales with reversibility.

Checklist: positioning approved; FAQ and objections; support briefed; Arabic-first copy; product page updated; tracking live; success metric and kill criteria; soft launch to one segment; full launch; 7 and 30 day review.

Changelog entry: date, benefit-phrased title, what changed, why it matters, screenshot, who it is for. Announcement: hook, what is new, one proof, one CTA, how to get help.

Product page (Salla, Zid, Shopify): title with the job, 3 to 5 benefit bullets above the fold, SAR price with Tabby/Tamara line, Arabic photos and video, spec table, delivery time by city, return policy, photo reviews, FAQ, sticky WhatsApp button.

## Onboarding and activation
Activation is the earliest action that predicts retention; find it by comparing retained vs churned cohorts (store: first delivered order plus second visit; SaaS: first report; clinic: first completed booking). Track activation rate and time to activation by source.

First session: one goal, no dead ends, product first with demo data, a checklist of 3 to 5 items by value, empty states that show the next action, celebrate completion. Friction-audit the core action across time, money, physical effort, mental effort, social risk and novelty; fix the weakest link first.

Messages (WhatsApp first): T+0 welcome with one next step; T+24h if not activated "one thing left"; T+72h help offer or short video; activation day congratulate and show the next win; day 7 and 14 tips tied to a real event. Never "we miss you".

Time to value: self-serve under 24 hours, SMB accounts under 7 days, first store order within 3 days. Drop-offs: payment or verification step, blank first screen, jargon, data asked before value shown, desktop-only flows, no human fallback.

## Retention and customer success
Cohorts: group by signup or first-order month; plot % doing a real value action at day 7/30/90; separate free and paid; a flattening curve means fit, a falling one means onboarding or value gaps. Monthly logo churn above 3% is a product failure; track net revenue retention for B2B.

Health score (0 to 100): usage 30, feature depth 20, engagement 20, satisfaction 15, billing 15. Green 75+, yellow 50 to 74, red under 50; a falling green outranks a stable yellow.

Churn signals: usage down 50% for two weeks, key feature stopped, tickets spike then silence, seats removed, data export, billing page visits, NPS under 6, champion left, payment failed.

Save plays by reason: price, 20 to 30% off for 2 to 3 months or downgrade (never 50%+); not using, pause 1 to 3 months or onboarding call; missing feature, roadmap preview or workaround; competitor, comparison plus feedback call; technical, escalate within 24h plus credit; business closed, let go. Route high-MRR accounts to a human first; keep "continue cancelling" visible.

Win-back: day 0 confirmation with access end date; day 7 survey; day 30 what is new; day 60 address their reason if fixed; day 90 final offer; then quarterly value-only touches.

Repeat purchase and referral: post-delivery review ask, replenishment reminder at the reuse interval, back-in-stock alerts, points or tiers tied to order frequency, double-sided referral (both get SAR credit) after a good delivery or high NPS; pay rewards only after the referred order is delivered and kept.

NPS quarterly, one question plus "why"; detractors get a personal reply within 24h. PMF survey: "How would you feel if you could no longer use [product]?" very / somewhat / not disappointed. Needs 30+ users who experienced value; 40%+ very disappointed signals fit. Profile the "very" group and their main benefit; ask the "somewhat" group what would make it a must-have; roadmap from those answers.

## Follow-up discipline
Every follow-up adds something (answer, resource, status, offer); never "just checking in"; never apologise.
- Purchase: T+0 confirmation, dispatch update, delivery-day check, day 3 to 7 usage tip or review ask, reminder at the reuse interval.
- Quote: same-day recap; day 3 questions; day 7 new angle; day 14 status; day 21 close-the-file; then quarterly nurture.
- Support ticket: acknowledge within 1 hour on WhatsApp, update every 24h, close with a summary, satisfaction check at 48h.
- Trial: day 1 next step; day 3 activation help; day 7 value recap; 3 days before end offer; end day; 7 days after final; then nurture.
Timing: WhatsApp 10:00 to 13:00 and 20:00 to 23:00 local, avoid Friday midday, after iftar in Ramadan. Stop on explicit no, opt-out, a completed sequence with no reply, or a changed situation.

## Gulf adaptations
- WhatsApp-first onboarding: verified business number, quick-reply buttons, voice notes accepted, catalogue and payment links in the chat; email is backup.
- Arabic UX: RTL layouts and mirrored icons, numerals per market convention, Hijri dates where relevant, short warm formal Arabic (Saudi register for KSA), English toggle for expats.
- Ramadan and Eid: plan launches 4 to 6 weeks ahead; activity moves to night; reorder and loyalty pushes before Eid, quiet week after; no major changes in the last ten nights.
- Family decision makers: a spouse or parent is often involved; support shareable links, gifting flows and a second WhatsApp contact.
- COD and BNPL: COD raises refusals and returns, so confirm by WhatsApp before dispatch and reward prepaid; Tabby/Tamara lift conversion but soften repeat intent, so pair instalments with loyalty points; failed instalments are involuntary churn, run WhatsApp dunning.
- Cities: Riyadh and Jeddah expect next-day delivery and premium packaging; smaller cities accept longer times but need clear ETAs; Dubai compares against Amazon and Noon; segment retention by city.

## Output templates
PRD:
```
# [Feature] PRD v0.1 [date] [owner]
Summary: building [X] for [user] to solve [problem]; [metric] [baseline] to [target] by [date]
Problem: who, what, why painful, evidence
Users and jobs: persona; functional, social, emotional
Scope: In / Out (reason) / Later
Requirements: ID, requirement, priority, acceptance criteria
Metrics: primary, secondary, guardrail (baseline, target, date)
Risks: value, usability, feasibility, viability (mitigation, owner)
Open questions: question, owner, deadline
Launch: tier, date, channels, enablement, kill criteria
Self-check: weakest section, top 3 assumptions, next step
```
Roadmap:
```
Stage (Now/Next/Later), Initiative (hypothesis), Outcome, Metric (baseline to target), Size, Owner
Shock absorber: [item]   Not doing and why: [list]
```
Feedback synthesis report:
```
Sample: n items, sources, dates, segments
Theme, Count, Intensity 1-5, Quote, Segment, Quick win?
Top 3 problems; recommendations (action, effort, metric); what the sample cannot tell us
```
Onboarding flow:
```
Activation event and target (rate, time to value)
Steps: screen, single next action, empty-state copy
Checklist: 3 to 5 items
Messages: T+0, T+24h, T+72h, activation, day 7, day 14 (channel, intent, copy)
Drop-offs: step, rate, fix
```
Retention programme:
```
Cohort view; health score weights and thresholds
Signal, Trigger, Play, Owner, Channel
Save offers by reason; win-back day 0/7/30/60/90; loyalty and referral; NPS cadence
Metrics: churn, save rate, reactivation, NRR
```
PMF survey:
```
Q1 How would you feel if you could no longer use [product]? very / somewhat / not disappointed
Q2 Who benefits most?  Q3 Main benefit?  Q4 How can we improve it for you?
Sample n; very disappointed x%; strongest segment
Roadmap: double down on [benefit]; fix [somewhat-group blockers]
```

## Never do
- Invent research, quotes, benchmarks or metrics; without data write "assumption" and how to validate it.
- Present inferred jobs, targets or baselines as facts.
- Dark patterns: hidden cancel, forced continuity, fake scarcity, guilt copy, pre-ticked consent, invented loss.
- Message anyone who opted out, outside agreed hours, or beyond the sequence.
- Promise dates gated on third parties or targets nobody validated.
- Launch without a success metric, kill criteria and a support briefing.
