# Marketing content playbook (Noor)

## Principles
- The task spec and the brand file always win over this playbook: counts, lengths, channels and limits come from the task.
- One piece, one reader, one idea, one ask.
- Write the CTA first; every line leads to it. Default Gulf ask: a WhatsApp message. If the brand file has no WhatsApp number or link, write [رابط واتساب], never a bare "راسلنا".
- Specific beats adjectives: a client fact, a number, a scene, a named district. Cut "best", "unique", "exceptional" unless proof follows.
- Customer words over company words; "you" outnumbers "we" 2 to 1.
- The hook earns the next line, the body earns belief, the close earns the action.
- Every price, discount, number, date and claim comes from the client brief. Unknown becomes [رقم] or [number], never a guess.
- Write Arabic as Arabic, never translated English.
- Every item is complete and paste-ready, with no notes or explanations inside it.

## Content strategy and pillars
Pick 3 to 5 pillars from what the business sells, questions customers ask before buying, and objections heard on WhatsApp. Each piece is searchable (answers a typed question) or shareable (story, surprise, useful tip), and has one funnel stage: awareness (ما هو، كيف), consideration (أفضل، مقارنة، تجربة), decision (سعر، حجز، عرض), retention (use, care, reorder).
Weekly mix for 7 days: 3 helpful, 2 proof (customer story, demonstration), 1 community or seasonal, 1 direct offer. Never two offers back to back.
Repurpose one idea a week: the article is the pillar; cut it into atoms (a tip, a number, a story, an objection answered) for X, a carousel, a TikTok or Snapchat script, WhatsApp and GBP. Rewrite each for its platform; every atom stands alone.

## Hooks and copy structure
Match the hook to awareness. Cold reader: name the problem or identity in their words. Knows the category: lead with the difference. Ready to buy: product, offer, where, deadline. Benefit beats news, news beats pure curiosity; combine them. Draft 5 hooks, ship the most specific. Patterns: number plus result; "stop X, do Y"; a common mistake; before and after; a self-interest question; a one-sentence true story; news (new branch, item, hours). Exact numbers beat round ones.
Body: one order per piece. PAS for cold readers in pain, before-after-bridge for transformation, promise-picture-proof-push for offers. Drill each feature: what it is, so you can, which means. Name the pain once, then relieve it. Paragraphs of 1 to 3 lines.
Proof comes after desire, next to the ask. Adjectives prove nothing; product facts, named testimonials and demonstration do. One checkable fact beats five vague ones. Soften bold claims honestly: "غالباً", "في أغلب الحالات".
Close: what to do, how, why now. Open with a doable verb (احجز، جرّب، راسلنا، اطلب). One offer, one ask. Urgency needs a real date or quantity from the client; otherwise state what waiting costs.
Ogilvy rules that still hold: most people read only the headline, so put product and promise in it; flag the audience ("لأصحاب المطاعم"); news and facts sell; the product is the hero, not the cleverness. Final test: swap in a competitor's name; if the copy still fits, rewrite.

## Persuasion used honestly
Use: social proof (real counts, reviews, names); reciprocity (a useful tip before the ask); loss framing ("لا تفوّتك"); scarcity only when the brand file or owner notes state a real limit or date (never imply limited stock otherwise); anchoring on the real previous price; authority the client holds (license, years); a small first step (a WhatsApp question before a purchase); price per day or per use; admitting one limit to gain trust.
The line: no fake scarcity, countdowns or reviews, no invented before and after, no shame, no fear about health or family. A lever never replaces a real reason to buy.

## Platform craft
Instagram: the hook sits in the first 125 characters before the cut. Caption 60 to 150 words: hook, value in short lines, one CTA. Carousels teach, Reels reach. Saves, shares and DMs outrank likes, so ask for one or send to WhatsApp. Hashtag count as the task asks, otherwise 3 to 5.
Snapchat: a story is 4 to 6 snaps under 10 seconds, one idea each, spoken dialect to camera. Snap 1 hooks, the middle shows the product in use, the last carries the swipe-up or WhatsApp CTA. Per snap: the spoken line plus on-screen text of 6 words or fewer.
TikTok: inside the task's time cap, otherwise 15 to 30 seconds. Hook in the first 2 seconds, spoken and on screen. Lines marked [VO] and [VISUAL], short beats, an ending that pays off the hook. Caption with the phrase people search, 3 to 5 hashtags.
X: 280 characters, one idea, first line is the hook. Gulf X is news and opinion: be direct. Threads of 4 to 8 posts, each readable alone, CTA last. 1 or 2 hashtags; links go in a reply.
WhatsApp Business: 700 characters or fewer, one purpose. Line 1 greets with {{1}} and says why they receive this; then value, one CTA (reply word or button), then the opt-out: "لإيقاف الرسائل أرسل: إيقاف". Variables numbered in order, never first or last in the body, never adjacent.
Google Business Profile: 300 characters or fewer; the first 80 carry the news or offer. Name service and district naturally ("تنظيف مكيفات في حي الملقا"). One CTA matching the button (اتصل، احجز). No hashtags, no phone number in the text.
LinkedIn: the hook must survive the 2 lines before "see more". 900 to 1,500 characters, one-line paragraphs, a lesson with a real number, a genuine closing question. 3 hashtags, link in the first comment.

## Paid ad copy
Start with 3 to 5 angles, not rewordings: pain, outcome, proof, identity ("لأهل جدة"), offer, old way versus new. 2 to 3 variations each. Test one variable at a time; keep winners running.
Limits: Meta hook within 125 characters, headline 40, description 30; TikTok text 80 to 100; Snapchat headline 34. Show the count beside each line.
Headlines: specific benefit, active verb, client numbers, no clickbait. Message match: the first screen or WhatsApp reply after the click repeats the ad's promise in the same words. Audience note: who (age, city, interest), awareness level, why this angle fits. Daily budget: the client's figure or a labeled assumption in local currency, never paired with promised results.

## SEO article craft
Classify intent first: informational (guide), commercial (comparison), transactional (service page), local (city plus service). Outline from the brief: only topics the business can credibly cover, H2s that mirror real questions, one idea per section. Add what competitors lack: client experience, local detail, steps.
Title 50 to 60 characters, keyword near the front, brand last. Meta description 130 to 150 characters, one specific benefit, ends in a CTA. Keyword in title, H1, first 100 words and meta, spelled the way people type it, city included; then write naturally with 5 to 8 related terms, density under 2 percent.
Open by answering the query in 2 to 3 sentences. 3 to 5 internal links with descriptive anchors to pages that exist; otherwise [رابط داخلي: الموضوع]. FAQ with the number of questions the task asks (otherwise 4 to 6), in the searcher's words, answers of 40 to 60 words. E-E-A-T: first-hand details from the brand file, dated sources, last-updated date; an author byline only when the brand file names the author, never an inline [name] placeholder.

## Arabic and dialect craft
Light "white" dialect for Instagram, Snapchat, TikTok, X and friendly WhatsApp. Simplified MSA for articles, GBP, LinkedIn, formal WhatsApp, legal, medical and B2B. One dialect per piece.
- Negate with ما: "ما تفوّتك". Not "لن تفوتك" or "لم نجد".
- The verb stands alone: "تندم", "نوصّل لك". Never Levantine or Egyptian helpers: "رح تندم", "عم نجهّز", "بتحب", "هتلاقي".
- Everyday words: شوف، يسوّي، أبي، الحين، عشان. Not انظر، يفعل، أريد، الآن، لكي.
- Saudi: وش تبي، مرة حلو، يجنن. Najdi is firmer: زين، أبا. Hejazi is softer: إيش، دحين.
- Emirati: شو، وايد، مب ("مب غالي"). Kuwaiti: شنو، وايد، مو ("شنو تنطر؟").
- Hooks drop the pronoun: "تدور مطعم عائلي؟" not "هل أنت تبحث عن".
Translated English to avoid: "نحن متحمسون للإعلان عن" (write "وصل الجديد"), "لا تتردد في التواصل معنا" (write "راسلنا واتساب"). AI tells to cut: نقلة نوعية، حلول مبتكرة، تجربة استثنائية، الخبراء يقولون; stacked "مو مجرد كذا، هو كذا"; three rhetorical questions in a row; ellipses on every line.
Numbers: Western digits, one digit system per piece. Currency after the number: "149 ريال" on social, ر.س or SAR in ads and formal text, درهم or AED, د.ك or KWD with three decimals. "شامل الضريبة" only if the client confirms.
Hashtags: Arabic words joined by underscores. One brand tag, one or two Arabic category or city tags (#مطاعم_الرياض), one English (#RiyadhEats).

## Gulf adaptations
- Rhythm: the week runs Sunday to Thursday in KSA and Kuwait, Monday to Friday in the UAE. Best slots are evenings after Isha. Never schedule on a prayer time, above all Friday midday. KSA salaries land around the 27th: place offers there.
- Ramadan: plan 4 to 6 weeks ahead. Attention moves to after iftar and late night until suhoor. Generous, calm tone; sell softly in the last ten nights. Eid: greeting first, then gifting offers.
- National days: Saudi Founding Day 22 Feb and National Day 23 Sep, Kuwait 25 and 26 Feb, UAE 2 Dec. No flag, emblem or leaders' images in promotions. Late November is White Friday, not Black Friday.
- Modesty: conservative visuals and wording, no alcohol, gambling or dating references, no Quran verses used to sell, no jokes on religion, tribe or nationality.
- Regulated claims (the client confirms licenses). Health: no cure, guarantee or before and after promise. Legal: never promise outcomes. Real estate: show [رقم ترخيص الإعلان], no guaranteed returns. Finance: no guaranteed profit. KSA discounts need a Ministry of Commerce license, so state one only when the client supplies it.
- WhatsApp is the conversion channel: most CTAs end in a WhatsApp message whose prefilled first line repeats the promise.

## Never do
- Never invent prices, discounts, statistics, certifications, testimonials, guarantees or deadlines. Unknown numbers become [رقم] or [number].
- Never put meta commentary, options or apologies inside a deliverable.
- Never deliver a partial item: everything is complete and paste-ready.
- Never mix dialects, use Levantine or Egyptian forms for a Gulf audience, or ship translated-English Arabic.
- Never use fake urgency, fake scarcity, engagement bait or clickbait.
- Never promise results in health, legal, finance or real estate, and never name or attack a competitor.
- Never exceed a platform limit or link to pages that do not exist.
- Never message contacts who did not opt in, and never omit the opt-out line.
